## 1. Purpose

Bring in **Sets**: a world, a visual, an original built-in instrument and an ambience, all designed together around one **mood**. Sets 1 and 2 also build the foundations that every later set reuses:
- the Sound Core
- the Ambience engine
- Living Sky
- the World Kit

### Success looks like
1. **Felt Glass** and **Orbit Pad** sound beautiful enough to give you goosebumps. They are original and made entirely inside the app, so they are yours forever.
2. **Moonlit Lake** and **Spaceship Lounge** look stunning and stay smooth on your Intel MacBook Pro on the Smooth setting.
3. One tap on a set loads everything: the world, the visual, the instrument and the ambience. Each part can still be swapped.
4. The four parts of a set feel like one place and one feeling.
5. Each set credits the song that inspired it.
6. You can hear the instruments from demo clips attached to the release before you even open the app.

### What you told me
- Every addition is at least **1:1:1**: one world, one visual that suits it, and one instrument that suits it. They should match perfectly where possible.
- Instruments are **original sounds built into the app** (option B), not your plugins.
- "Sounds match everything" means **mood**: soft piano on a calm lake. Ambience (water, crickets, hum) stays as well.
- **Living Sky** gives quick cycles, real time from your Mac's clock, moon phases from full back to full, and weather that is chosen or randomly beautiful.
- Art that inspires should be credited: an **"Inspired by"** homage in the instrument UI.
- Each sound must be **as good as or better than** the sound that inspired it. Where better isn't possible, it should be as close as possible in every way, so playing it along with the song sounds beautiful (harmonising or matching), never clashing. This is for personal use only, and the artists are never taken for granted or misused.
- This run builds Sets 1 and 2. Next run builds Set 3 (Golden Hour Beach) and a few others.
- After this release is ready for testing: brainstorm 100+ more worldscapes.
- Keep growing the skies and weathers. The backlog list is in the project.

### Faithful to the inspiration
- **The bar:**
  - Each instrument is as good as or better than the sound that inspired it.
  - Where "better" isn't possible in some respect, it is as close as possible in character: register, tone colour, attack, decay, movement and space.
- **Play-along ready:** every inspired instrument is built to blend when you play along with the song in Spotify:
  - **Concert tuning** (A = 440 Hz) by default.
  - A **Fine tune** dial (±50 cents) to lock onto a recording that sits slightly off standard pitch.
  - **Transpose** (±12 semitones).
  - Tone, brightness and space are matched in the register the song uses, so the instrument sits in the same "room" as the recording instead of fighting it.
- **Research first:** before building each instrument, I research how the original sound was made. That covers the instruments, synths and effects, published interviews and breakdowns, plus the song's key and tempo. The findings become a written **sound brief** in the plan, which the instrument is built and checked against.
- **Done means it passes your play-along test.** I compare demo renders and spectrograms against the brief. Then you play along with the song. We keep refining until it blends beautifully. A set isn't finished until the instrument passes.

### Boundaries (unchanged)
- Sounds are original. Nothing is sampled or copied from recordings.
- "Inspired by" cards are text and a link only. They never include album artwork or lyrics.

## 3. How it fits together

```
 Keyboard → MIDI ─┬─► Instrument (plugin OR built-in Sound Core) ─► Instrument bus ─┐
                  │                                       (sound-character tap here)  ├─► Main mix ─► Speakers
                  │        Ambience engine (built-in) ─► Ambience bus (own volume) ───┘
                  │              ▲ world state (weather, time, water…) + playing activity (duck)
                  └─► Visuals: Living Sky + World Kit + Visual layer  ◄── Mood (shared)
```

| Unit | What it does | Lives in |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound Core** | Real-time DSP: voices, oscillators, filters, piano model, Space reverb, ambience generators. No memory allocation or locks while playing | New C++ target `InStrumDSP` with a small C interface |
| **Built-in instrument** | Wraps the Sound Core as a real instrument plugin inside the app (in-process Audio Unit). Has a parameter tree (so Mapping sees its dials) and full-state save/restore | `InStrumAudio` |
| **Patches** | Recipes: engine type, dial values, mood bindings, credit. Felt Glass and Orbit Pad are patches | `InStrumCore` (Codable data) |
| **Ambience** | Second built-in Audio Unit (generator) on its own bus, driven by the world | `InStrumAudio` + `InStrumDSP` |
| **Mood** | Five values that every part reads | `InStrumCore` |
| **Living Sky** | Time, sun, moon, phase, stars, weather as pure, testable state, drawn by shared shaders | Core state in `InStrumCore`; drawing in `InStrumApp/Visuals` |
| **World Kit** | Layered scenery: sky, far, water/ground, near, frame, particles. Each layer has Smooth, High and Ultra versions | `InStrumApp/Visuals/WorldKit` |
| **Worlds and visuals** | Moonlit Lake, Spaceship Lounge; Lake Ripples, Galaxy Warp | Models in Core; renderers in App |
| **Sets** | Bundle of world + visual + patch + ambience + mood + credit; the gallery | Core (data) + App (UI) |

**Existing engine change:**
- Today the sound-character tap sits on the main mixer. It moves to the **instrument bus**, so ambience never changes the visuals' reading of your instrument.
- Panic silences the instrument. Ambience keeps playing.

## 4. The Sound Core

### 4.1 Rules
- Everything is allocated before playing starts.
- The audio thread never allocates memory, locks or logs.
- Denormals are flushed.
- Notes are sample-accurate: MIDI events land at the exact sample they were scheduled for.
- Every dial change is smoothed, so there is no zipper noise.
- Voice stealing takes the quietest voice and fades it over 20 ms. There are never clicks.
- **CPU budget:**
  - Measured in CI: Felt Glass with a 16-note sustained chord, plus Orbit Pad with an 8-note chord, plus full ambience, all render in **under 8% of real time**.
  - Of that, Felt Glass alone with a 32-note pedalled run renders in under 5%.
  - That leaves comfortable headroom (under a quarter of one core) on your Intel Mac at a 256 buffer.
- **Polyphony:** Felt Glass 32, Orbit Pad 16.

### 4.2 Built-in instrument plumbing
- Felt Glass and Orbit Pad appear in the **instrument picker** in their own "In Strum Meants" group. They load like any instrument and are remembered on relaunch.
- They don't depend on the system plugin catalogue: built-ins are listed and instantiated directly.
- The instrument's **window** is our own SwiftUI panel (not a plugin view) with:
  - its dials
  - presets
  - the **Inspired by** card (§9)
- Its dials appear in Mapping as `Instrument: Glass`, `Instrument: Bloom` and so on, via the existing dial reader.
- State is saved as the instrument's full state, the same way plugins are saved.

### 4.3 Felt Glass (after "Come Back to Earth")
A modelled felt piano with a glass-bell bloom layer.

- **String model (modal synthesis):**
  - Each note is a bank of decaying partials at inharmonic frequencies: `fₙ = n·f₀·√(1 + B·n²)`, where B rises toward the bass and treble like a real piano.
  - Partial count adapts by register: up to 40 in the bass, about 20 in the middle, about 8 at the top.
  - Partials are culled once they fall below −80 dB.
- **Two-stage decay and beating:**
  - Each partial is a pair of very slightly detuned components (as from unison strings).
  - This gives the natural "prompt then aftersound" decay and gentle shimmer.
- **Felt hammer:**
  - Velocity sets both loudness and hammer hardness.
  - Softer notes roll off the upper partials more, so they sound darker and rounder.
  - The strike position (about 1/8 of the string) shapes the partial balance.
- **Felt and key noise:**
  - A short, filtered thump at the start of each note.
  - A soft damper sound on release.
  - Both are set by the **Felt** dial.
- **Body:** a fixed soundboard character (a few resonant body filters) for woody warmth.
- **Sympathetic ring:** while the sustain pedal (or the on-screen hold) is down, other strings shimmer in sympathy through a low-level resonator bank fed by the instrument's own output.
- **Glass bloom:**
  - An inharmonic bell layer (glass-like partial ratios) that swells in over 0.3 to 1.5 s and rings beyond the piano.
  - Its amount is set by the **Glass** dial.
  - Velocity sets how bright the glass gets.
- **Release:**
  - Lifting a key damps the note over about 0.15 s.
  - With the pedal down, notes ring on naturally.
- **Dials:** Felt, Glass, Warmth (tone tilt), Ring (decay length), Space (reverb amount), Width (stereo spread by pitch).
- **Tuning (both instruments):** Fine tune (±50 cents) and Transpose (±12), shown in the instrument panel and saved with the set.
- **Presets:** Felt Glass (default), Bare Felt (no glass), Glass Choir (more bloom), Lullaby (darker, closer).

> *Effort note:* the string and hammer maths is the hardest part of this milestone. I'll suggest switching to **max** effort just for that step.

### 4.4 Orbit Pad (after "Innerbloom")
A warm, slowly blooming analogue-style pad.

- **Oscillators:**
  - Layer A is 5 saws in unison, detuned by the Drift dial.
  - Layer B is 3 soft pulses an octave below.
  - Plus a sine sub.
  - All are alias-free (PolyBLEP).
  - Each voice drifts slightly in pitch on its own slow random path, like hardware.
- **Filter:**
  - A 4-pole ladder (zero-delay-feedback design) with gentle saturation.
  - It is warm, never harsh.
- **Bloom:**
  - A per-voice filter envelope with a long attack (1 to 6 s, set by **Bloom**) that opens the filter as you hold.
  - Velocity sets how bright it opens.
- **Amp:**
  - Attack 0.4 to 2 s.
  - Release up to 8 s, so notes drift away.
- **Motion:**
  - Slow per-voice movement of cutoff, pan and detune (**Motion** dial).
  - A stereo chorus.
- **Shimmer:** an octave-up shimmer in the Space reverb's feedback (**Shimmer** dial).
- **Dials:** Bloom, Drift, Motion, Shimmer, Brightness, Space.
- **Presets:** Orbit Pad (default), Earthrise (warmer, slower), Weightless (more shimmer), Re-entry (brighter, faster bloom).

### 4.5 Space (shared reverb)
- 8-line feedback delay network:
  - input diffusion
  - slowly modulated delays (a lush, unmetallic tail)
  - frequency-dependent damping
  - pre-delay
  - wide stereo
- An optional **shimmer**: an octave-up pitch shift in the feedback.
- Each instrument sets its size and amount from its mood. The Space dial adjusts it from there.

## 5. Ambience engine

Ambience is generated live from synthesis, not loops, so it never repeats. It runs as its own built-in generator on the ambience bus.

**Generators (this milestone):**

| Generator | How |
|---|---|
| Water lapping | Filtered noise swells on slow random envelopes, plus occasional droplet "plips" |
| Crickets | Several individual chirpers (pulsed high tones) at different positions. They chirp more slowly as the night cools |
| Breeze / wind | Noise through slowly moving filters. Gusts grow with stormy weather |
| Owl | A rare, distant, soft two-note call with reverb |
| Rain | Dense droplet impulses plus a noise bed, in an "on water" or "on glass" variant |
| Thunder | Low rumble bursts following lightning flashes after a distance delay |
| Dawn chorus | Gentle synthesized birdsong as night turns to dawn |
| Ship hum | Low tones with slow beating, plus brown noise |
| Air flow | Soft filtered noise |
| Chime | A rare soft bell (shares the Glass partials) |
| Hull ticks | Rare tiny resonant clicks |

- **World-driven:**
  - The world sends its state (weather, time of night, wind, water presence, rain surface) to the ambience every frame.
  - Changes always cross-fade over at least 2 s.
- **Playing-aware:** ambience dips by 4 dB while you play and swells back over about 2 s in pauses.
- **Controls:**
  - Ambience on/off
  - Volume (default 25%)
  - **Closeness** (distant ↔ all around you)
- **Honesty clause:** synthesized birdsong and owls are hard to make beautiful. If either doesn't pass your ear test, it ships switched off by default until it does.

## 6. Mood

```
Mood { energy, warmth, brightness, space, motion }   // each 0…1
```

| | energy | warmth | brightness | space | motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Serene (Moonlit Lake) | 0.2 | 0.45 | 0.3 | 0.85 | 0.25 |
| Dreamy / awe-struck (Spaceship Lounge) | 0.35 | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.95 | 0.6 |

Every part maps the mood through its own **mood bindings**:

| Part | What the mood moves |
|---|---|
| Instrument | space → reverb size/amount; warmth → tone tilt; brightness → hammer hardness or filter peak; motion → drift/LFO depth; energy → response speed (attack, bloom time) |
| World | warmth → colour grade; brightness → exposure; space → mist and depth haze; motion → cloud, water and star drift speed |
| Visual | energy → how far reactions travel; motion → how long they linger |
| Ambience | space → distance and reverb; energy → activity density; brightness → high-frequency content |

- Mood works as an **offset under your dials**. Your own dial settings always win on top.
- The **mood dial** (sliding between named moods) is *not* in this milestone. Because everything already reads Mood, adding it later is small.

## 7. Living Sky

### 7.1 Time
- **Quick cycle:** a full day lasts 2 to 60 minutes (you choose; default 12). The moon steps through a phase each day so you see the whole cycle quickly.
- **Real time:**
  - Your Mac's clock plus a **city** chosen from a built-in list of about 200 cities.
  - The default is the city matching your Mac's time zone (Australia/Melbourne → Melbourne).
  - No location permission and no internet are needed.
- **Freeze:** pick a time of day (and a moon phase) and stay there.

### 7.2 Astronomy (all offline, pure and tested)
- Sun position, sunrise and sunset: NOAA solar algorithm, within 2 minutes.
- Moon position, phase, illuminated fraction, moonrise and moonset: Meeus low-precision lunar theory. Phase is correct to within a few hours, and rise and set to within about 10 minutes.
- Star field rotates with local sidereal time and latitude, so the night sky turns realistically.

### 7.3 Look
- **Sky palette:**
  - Keyed to the sun's altitude through night, astronomical, nautical and civil twilight, golden hour and day, blended smoothly.
  - Weather desaturates and greys it.
- **Moon:**
  - Drawn with its true phase shadow and soft earthshine.
  - Glow scales with phase. Its light colours the scene.
- **Stars and Milky Way:** on clear nights, fading with moonlight and cloud.
- **Clouds:** layered noise with coverage set by the weather, lit by the sun or moon.

### 7.4 Weather
- **Kinds:** clear, light cloud, overcast, mist, drizzle, rain, storm, snow.
- **Modes:**
  - **Pick one**
  - **Random beautiful**: drifts every 5 to 20 minutes, weighted toward the prettiest, transitions take about 60 s
  - **Real weather** later (it needs an online service)
- Lightning goes through the existing **flash limiter** and Gentle visuals.
- Rain and snow are instanced particles, with splashes on water and drops on glass.
- Worlds declare what applies: the Spaceship Lounge has no rain. Its "sky" is space, its sun and Earth follow the time setting, and in real time Earth shows its real day and night sides.

## 8. World Kit and the worlds

### 8.1 Layers
A world is a stack of layers:

`sky (Living Sky) → far (silhouettes/planets) → water or ground → near details → frame (window, cockpit; glass with raindrops) → particles → visual layer`

**Smooth rules** (from the 28 Oct freeze) apply to every layer:
- 1 MP or less.
- No per-pixel ray marching.
- Noise of 3 octaves or fewer.
- Simulations at quarter resolution.

High and Ultra add detail.

### 8.2 Moonlit Lake
- **Far:** layered mountain ridges and a pine treeline, with aerial-perspective haze.
- **Water:**
  - A reflective surface showing the sky, moon, ridges and trees.
  - A moonlight glitter path.
  - Gentle wind ripples.
  - Mist hovering above.
- **Near:** reeds at the edges that sway with the breeze.

### 8.3 Spaceship Lounge
- **Frame:**
  - A softly lit lounge interior: window struts, a low warm glow and faint reflections on the glass.
  - The window is the hero.
- **Outside:**
  - Earth with clouds, city lights on its night side and a glowing atmosphere rim, slowly turning.
  - The Moon in its real phase.
  - Sun glare.
  - Deep star fields and the Milky Way.

### 8.4 Visuals
- **Lake Ripples (Set 1):**
  - Each note drops a ripple at its key's position across the lake.
  - Velocity sets ring size and brightness.
  - The pedal lets rings linger.
  - Held notes brighten the moonlight glitter.
  - Chords release a few fireflies that drift up and fade.
  - The ripple simulation is a height field, at quarter resolution on Smooth.
- **Galaxy Warp (Set 2):**
  - Notes launch stars that streak past the window. Pitch sets the direction and velocity the brightness.
  - Playing intensity sets the warp speed of the whole star field.
  - Chords bloom a soft nebula glow tinted by chord quality.
  - Silence lets everything settle back to a still, drifting view.
- **Mix and match:**
  - Visuals declare what they need. Lake Ripples needs water, so it is only offered on worlds with water.
  - Galaxy Warp works anywhere (it uses the sky). So Galaxy Warp over Moonlit Lake is allowed; Ripples in the Spaceship is not offered.
- Both visuals use the existing roles and Reaction presets, so the Mapping editor and presets work unchanged.

### 8.5 Keys
- Both worlds show the existing on-screen piano strip (it can be hidden).
- On the lake, played keys glow moon-silver.
- In the lounge, played keys glow a soft console blue.

## 11. Reliability

| Situation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Built-in instruments fail to register | The "In Strum Meants" group shows a short message. Plugins still work |
| CPU spike on the audio thread | Built-ins drop their quietest voices first, never the newest note |
| Instrument changed while notes are held | Old notes are released, nothing sticks (existing panic path) |
| Panic | The instrument is silenced instantly. Ambience continues (it has its own switch) |
| Slow frames | The existing auto-quality and stall safety net apply to worlds too |
| Unknown city or old settings | Falls back to the city for your Mac's time zone, then UTC |

## 12. Testing

**Automatic (every build):**
- **Sound Core** (offline renders):
  - Tuning is within 2 cents across the range at A = 440 Hz.
  - Fine tune and Transpose shift pitch exactly (within 1 cent).
  - Softer velocity gives a lower spectral centroid (darker).
  - Releasing a key decays to below −90 dB within the expected time (pedal up).
  - No NaN, infinity, denormal slowdowns or DC drift.
  - Silence in gives true silence out.
  - Voice stealing produces no clicks (no sample jumps above a threshold).
  - Every dial change is smooth.
  - CPU budgets as in §4.1.
- **Built-in plumbing:**
  - Loads through the app's engine.
  - Full-state round trip.
  - The parameter tree is exposed to the dial reader.
  - The sound-character tap ignores ambience.
- **Ambience:**
  - Each generator produces sound in its expected band.
  - Ducking dips and recovers.
  - Cross-fades have no jumps.
- **Living Sky:**
  - Melbourne sunrise and sunset for set dates within 2 min.
  - Known full and new moons (2026 dates) within 6 h.
  - Moonrise within 15 min.
  - Quick-cycle phase stepping.
  - Weather transitions are smooth.
  - The random-beautiful schedule is deterministic per seed.
  - Ground-truth values come from a standard astronomy library during planning.
- **Worlds:**
  - Shaders compile.
  - Preview frames render non-black and structured at Smooth and High.
  - A 2,000-note burst stays within the visual frame budget.
- **Sets:** loading a set sets all parts; restore works; credits are present for both sets.

**Previews I check before release:**
- Both worlds at dusk, night, moonrise and in rain or storm, at Smooth and High.
- Spectrogram images of each instrument and ambience, so I can *see* tone, decay and noise.

**Demo audio on the release:**
- Short original demo phrases (not melodies from the songs) rendered by CI and attached as audio files:
  - `Felt Glass demo`
  - `Orbit Pad demo`
  - `Moonlit Lake ambience`
  - `Spaceship Lounge ambience`

**Comparing against the sound brief:**
- Before each preview build, I check spectrograms and demo renders against that instrument's sound brief: register, brightness, attack, decay, movement and space.

**Your ear-and-eye checklist:**
- **Play-along test:** play along with the inspiring song in Spotify. It should blend or harmonise, never clash. Use Fine tune if the recording sits off-pitch.
- Goosebumps test
- Soft vs hard touch
- Pedal ring
- Pad bloom
- Ambience balance
- Mood match
- Smoothness
- Inspired-by cards
