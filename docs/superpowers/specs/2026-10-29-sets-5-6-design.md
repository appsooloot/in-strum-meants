## 1. Purpose

From this run on, every run delivers at least **one of everything** per set. That's the 1:1:1:1:1:1 ratio:
- a world
- its visual
- an original instrument
- a new **sky** feature
- a new **weather**
- a new **ambience** sound

The new sky and weather features join the Living Sky, so **every outdoor world gets them**, not only the new ones.

### Success looks like
1. **Cabin Felt** blends when Michael plays along with "Stay", and **Morning Box** blends with "Dunno". Each is as good as, or better than, the sound that inspired it.
2. Both window worlds look stunning and stay smooth on **Smooth** on the Intel MacBook Pro.
3. Halos and sundogs, pastel dawns, blizzards and "after the rain" appear in every world with a sky.
4. One tap on a set loads its world, visual, instrument preset, ambience and mood. Each part can still be swapped.
5. Each set credits its song with an original living card.
6. Demo clips of both instruments and both ambiences are attached to the release.

## 4. The instruments

Both follow the Sound Core rules:
- nothing allocates, locks or logs on the audio thread
- denormals are flushed
- MIDI is sample-accurate
- every dial is smoothed
- voices are stolen quietest-first with a 20 ms fade
- tuning is concert pitch (A = 440 Hz), with Fine tune ±50 cents and Transpose ±12 semitones
- mood comes in through the hidden `mood*` parameters
- the shared Space reverb, PeakLimiter and soft limit finish the output

### 4.1 Cabin Felt (after "Stay")
An upright piano with a felt strip between hammers and strings, played close in a small log room, with a soft flugelhorn that follows your melody.

- **Strings:**
  - It uses the shared **piano strings core**: modal strings with inharmonicity and unison beating, a damper and the sympathetic ring.
  - It's tuned as an **upright**: about 1.6× Felt Glass's inharmonicity (shorter strings) and slightly wider unison detune.
- **Hammer:**
  - Velocity sets the felt's hardness. The contact time runs from about **4 ms at pp to 1 ms at ff**, so harder notes are brighter as well as louder.
- **Felt** (the moderator strip):
  - Lengthens the contact up to about 2.5×.
  - Rolls off the strike's top.
  - Lowers the level by up to about 6 dB.
  - This is the hushed, close felt-piano sound.
- **Mechanics** (close and quiet):
  - a **key thump** on every note (a low thud around 60–120 Hz, about 15 ms, by velocity)
  - a faint **damper lift** on note-on
  - a **damper return** on release (a short felt "chuff" as the string is stopped)
  - a **pedal thump** with the rising string resonance when the sustain pedal goes down (the app still works without a pedal)
- **Body and room** (the **Wood** dial):
  - The upright cabinet's soundboard resonances.
  - A small log room: early reflections at about 3–15 ms and a short warm tail (RT60 about 0.6–1.2 s), through the shared Space reverb.
- **Fire:** warm, gentle saturation, a low-mid warmth shelf and a slight high roll-off.
- **Horn (the top voice, after Keyon Harrold's trumpet):**
  - **Which note it plays:** a note becomes the horn's note if it is the **highest key held at the moment it's pressed**.
  - **Slurs:** if the horn is still sounding (its previous note still held), it **slurs** to the new note. The glide is about 60–90 ms, with a small dip in level, like a player slurring.
  - **New phrases:** otherwise it starts fresh with a **scoop** up from about −40 cents over about 70 ms and a puff of breath.
  - **Release:** when its note is released, the horn releases (about 120–150 ms). It never jumps down to a lower held note.
  - **Range:** it plays in a flugelhorn's range, about **E3–C6**. Notes outside the range are moved by octaves into it.
  - **Tone:**
    - a dark, soft brass tone whose spectrum brightens with how hard you play, and over each note's attack (dynamic waveshaping)
    - a gentle bell formant (around 0.7 and 1.3 kHz)
    - band-passed breath noise
  - **Vibrato:** fades in after about 0.5 s, at about 5 Hz and up to about ±12 cents.
  - **Horn** sets the level. At 0 the horn is off.
- **Dials (in order):** Felt, Horn, Fire, Wood, Space, Width.
- **Presets:**

| Preset | felt | horn | fire | wood | space | width |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cabin Felt (default) | .60 | .30 | .50 | .50 | .35 | .50 |
| Fireside | .55 | .00 | .70 | .60 | .30 | .45 |
| Snow Horn | .70 | .75 | .45 | .45 | .50 | .55 |
| Late Night | .85 | .20 | .60 | .35 | .25 | .40 |

- **Polyphony:** 32 piano voices plus the one horn.
- **Mood:**
  - warmth tilts the Fire tone
  - brightness sets the hammer hardness and the horn's brightness
  - space sets the room and reverb
  - motion sets the horn's vibrato depth
  - energy sets the hammer hardness and the horn's scoop speed
- **Felt Glass stays the same:** before the strings move into the shared core, a test records Felt Glass's output fingerprint for a fixed phrase (per-10 ms RMS and spectral centroid). Afterwards it must match within **0.1 dB and 1%**.

### 4.2 Morning Box (after "Dunno")
Two hearts: a struck **box** (celesta or music box) on top, and a warm **synth wall** underneath that builds the longer you play.

- **Celesta bars:**
  - Steel bars struck by felt hammers, as **modal bars** at about 1, 2.76, 5.40 and 8.93 × f₀. The higher modes die much faster.
  - A tuned **wooden resonator** under each bar strengthens and lengthens the fundamental. It rings about **2–4 s** in the middle register, shorter up high.
  - A short felt **tick** (filtered noise, about 5 ms, around 2–4 kHz) starts each note.
  - Dampers stop a released note within about 80 ms, unless the sustain pedal is down.
  - **Bars** sets the hammer hardness, and so the brightness of the strike.
- **Music box comb** (the **Box** dial morphs bars → comb):
  - A plucked **cantilever tine**: modes at about 1, 6.27 and 17.55 × f₀, with a pluck click.
  - It rings longer, about 4–8 s, with a tiny shimmer of detune.
  - A small **wooden box** body resonance (around 300–900 Hz) comes in with the comb.
- **Synth wall** (**Bloom** sets its level):
  - Per note: 2 band-limited saws, detuned, plus a sine sub, through a warm 4-pole low-pass.
  - Attack about 0.4–1.2 s, release about 1.5 s.
- **Build** (the wall that grows as you play):
  - The engine keeps a **build level** B (0…1).
  - It rises with a leaky measure of how much you're playing (held notes and note density): **τ about 20–40 s** depending on the Build dial.
  - In silence it falls with **τ about 12 s**.
  - As B rises, the wall:
    - stacks **unison voices** (2 up to 6 per note)
    - adds an **octave-up layer** (gain ∝ B)
    - adds a very soft **octave-and-a-fifth shimmer** (∝ B²)
    - opens its filter (cutoff × (1 + 2B))
    - widens in stereo
  - **Build** scales both the ceiling and the speed. At 0 the wall stays as it is.
- **Haze:** a two-voice chorus (about 0.3–0.6 Hz) and a soft tape blur (gentle saturation, a slight high roll-off and a tiny ±3-cent wow) over the whole instrument.
- **Dials (in order):** Bars, Box, Bloom, Build, Haze, Space, Width.
- **Presets:**

| Preset | bars | box | bloom | build | haze | space | width |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Morning Box (default) | .60 | .20 | .40 | .50 | .35 | .50 | .55 |
| Music Box | .50 | 1.0 | .10 | .20 | .25 | .40 | .40 |
| Wall of Light | .45 | .15 | .85 | .90 | .45 | .65 | .80 |
| Snooze | .35 | .30 | .55 | .40 | .70 | .55 | .50 |

- **Polyphony:** 24 box voices and 24 wall voices.
- **Mood:**
  - warmth tilts the Haze tone and the wall's filter
  - brightness sets the bars' hardness
  - space sets the reverb
  - motion sets the chorus depth
  - energy sets how fast the wall builds

### 4.3 Sound briefs and the play-along bar
- Before either instrument is built, its brief (`docs/superpowers/briefs/cabin-felt.md`, `morning-box.md`) records:
  - the song facts above, with sources

## 5. Ambience

Everything is generated live and never loops. New sources join the existing generator with the same ducking (−4 dB while playing, about 2 s recovery), 2.5 s glides and closeness behaviour. `ISD_AMB_COUNT` grows from 19 to 24.

| Source | Indoor? | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Fireplace** | yes | A soft roar (low-passed noise with slow flicker), **crackles** (sparse short noise clicks around 1–5 kHz, a few per second, varying), **pops** (rarer, louder, with a short low thump), **sap hiss** (a 1–3 s band of 3–8 kHz noise now and then) and a **log settling** every 1–3 minutes (a soft thud and a shower of crackles) |
| **Kettle** | yes (far) | Every 4–8 minutes: a low rumble rising over about 90 s, bubbling into a soft boil, then the switch's **click**, and 3–10 s later a **cup's clink** (a small inharmonic ping). Far and roomy, from the kitchen |
| **Waking street** | no | A far traffic bed whose passing cars come more often as the morning goes on (the world sends the level), a rare **bike bell**, a **bus** swelling and sighing to a stop, the odd **door** |
| **Drips** | no | Irregular plinks after rain: short pitched drops (about 1–3 kHz with a small upward glide), some soft on leaves, some brighter on a gutter |
| **Clock** | yes | A very soft tick-tock at 60 a minute, alternating timbre. Default level is low |

- **Indoors and through the glass:** `AmbienceScene` gains `indoors` (0 outdoors … 1 inside behind closed glass).
  - Outdoor sources pass through a "window" filter: a low-pass toward about 1.2 kHz and up to about −9 dB, scaled by `indoors`.
  - Indoor sources (fireplace, kettle, clock, and the lounge's ship sounds) are untouched.
  - Rain on glass is at the glass, so it isn't filtered.
  - A bedroom window open a crack sets `indoors` to 0.5.
- **Wet roads:** `AmbienceContext` gains `wetness`. The road source adds a bright tyre **hiss** in proportion to it.
- **Snow on glass:** the rain-on-glass source gains a snow-tick mode (`AmbienceContext.snowOnGlass`, 0…1): quieter, drier, softer ticks for flakes landing on the window.
- **Every outdoor world:**
  - **Drips** follow wetness while it isn't raining.
  - **Blizzard** gusts drive the existing wind source (level from the gust).

## 6. Living Sky additions (every world with a sky)

### 6.1 Weather values
`WeatherKind` gains **`.blizzard`** ("Blizzard") and **`.afterRain`** ("After the rain"). `WeatherValues` gains **halo**, **frost** and **gust** (0…1).

| Kind | cloud | rain | snow | mist | wind | halo | frost | gust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clear | 0 | 0 | 0 | .1 | .1 | 0 | .15† | 0 |
| light cloud | .35 | 0 | 0 | .15 | .2 | .7 | .1† | .1 |
| overcast | .85 | 0 | 0 | .25 | .3 | 0 | .1† | .1 |
| mist | .4 | 0 | 0 | .9 | .05 | .15 | .2† | 0 |
| drizzle | .8 | .3 | 0 | .4 | .2 | 0 | 0 | .1 |
| rain | .9 | .75 | 0 | .3 | .4 | 0 | 0 | .2 |
| storm | 1 | 1 | 0 | .2 | .9 | 0 | 0 | .6 |
| snow | .85 | 0 | .8 | .3 | .25 | .2 | .6 | .2 |
| **blizzard** | 1 | 0 | 1 | .6 | 1 | 0 | .9 | 1 |
| **after the rain** | .45 | 0 | 0 | .55 | .1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- The existing kinds keep their current five values exactly.
- † These frost values form only at night, scaled by darkness: `frost × (1 − smoothstep(−12°, −2°, sun altitude))`.
- **Halo:** in "Random, beautiful", each light-cloud spell rolls an ice amount of 0.5–1 (seeded) that scales its halo. In Pick mode, light cloud uses 0.7.
- **Beauty weights** ("Random, beautiful"): blizzard 0.3, after the rain 1.5.
  - After a drizzle, rain or storm spell, the next spell is **after the rain** with probability 0.6.

### 6.2 New sky state
`SkyState` gains:
- `halo`
- `frost`
- `wetness`
- `gust`: the live gust envelope, 0…1
- `dawnPastel`: 0…1

**Wetness:**
- While rain > 0.1, it rises toward 1 with **τ = 90 s / rain**.
- Otherwise it dries with **τ 8 minutes**.
- After the rain holds a floor of **0.85**.
- Snow doesn't wet.
- It's deterministic, and resets with `reset(settings:)`.

**Gusts:**
- When the weather's gust value is above 0, gusts come every **3–8 s** (seeded): a rise over about 0.5 s and a fall over about 1.5 s.
- `gust = weatherGust × envelope`.
- In a blizzard, mist rises with the gust (**mist + 0.3 × gust**). That's the whiteout every world already knows how to draw.

### 6.3 Halos and sundogs
Drawn in the shared sky dome, so every world with a sky gets them.

- **22° halo:**
  - A thin ring 22° from the sun: red on the inside edge (about 21.8°), through yellow, to a whitish-blue outer edge. It's about 1.5° wide.
  - Its brightness is a small fraction of the sky's brightness near the sun, scaled by `halo`.
  - It shows while the sun's altitude is above −1°.
- **Sundogs:**
  - Two bright, rainbow-tinted spots at the sun's altitude, either side of the sun, with a short white tail outward.
  - Distance from the sun: **22° / cos(sun altitude)**.
  - They fade out between sun altitudes of 30° and 45°.
- **46° halo:** a much fainter ring that appears only when halo > 0.7.
- **Moon halos and moondogs:** the same, around the moon, when the moon is up and its lit fraction is > 0.6. They're paler and almost colourless.
- All cost a few dot products per pixel. Nothing flashes.

### 6.4 Pastel dawns and the Belt of Venus
- **Belt of Venus (every twilight):**
  - When the sun is between −6° and +1°, a soft **pink band** sits about 5–20° above the horizon **opposite the sun**.
  - Below it is the blue-grey band of **Earth's shadow**.
  - It's stronger on pastel mornings.
- **Pastel dawn:**
  - **Each morning** (local hour < 12), the sky picks its dawn's character from the date and the city, seeded. About **1 morning in 3** is pastel.
  - In Quick day, each cycle is a new day.
  - When pastel, the palette for sun altitudes **−10° to +8°** blends toward pastel anchors:
    - lavender-blue zenith
    - peach-pink horizon
    - cloud undersides lit pink
  - `dawnPastel` glides in and out over that window, never jumping.
- **Hold the light** gains **Pastel dawn**:
  - It holds the sun at **+1.5°** and forces `dawnPastel = 1`.
  - Like the other holds, only the sun's altitude is held.
  - It's offered in every world that has Hold the light: beach, road, cabin and bedroom.

### 6.5 Blizzard and after the rain on screen
**Blizzard (in the shared weather layer):**
- The snow's sideways tilt rises with the gust: `(0.25 + 1.2 × gust) × wind`.
- Flakes speed up in gusts.
- A third particle kind, **ground streamers**, carries long thin wisps of blowing snow across the lower third of the view.
- No lightning.

**After the rain (in each world):**

| World | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Golden Hour Beach | The wet-sand mirror spreads up the whole beach with wetness; dry ripples darken |
| Coast Road | Wet tarmac: lamps, cat's-eyes, guardrail lights and tail-lights reflect as long vertical streaks below themselves, scaled by wetness; tyre hiss in the sound |
| Moonlit Lake | Low mist on the water (its existing mist), and drips in the sound |
| Snowbound Cabin | Wetness stays 0 (it's frozen); the mist fills the valley |
| Bedroom at Dawn | The street shines: it reflects the sky, the street lamp and lit windows; drips run off the window frame |
| Spaceship Lounge | No weather (unchanged) |

### 6.6 Frost on glass
- Frost is drawn by the **Window Kit's glass layer** (§7), in windows only.
- It isn't drawn on the car window (the heater keeps it clear) or in the lounge.
- The base frost comes from `SkyState.frost`. Rain melts it: frost glides to 0 while rain > 0.05.

### 6.7 Settings and UI
- The weather picker lists **Blizzard** and **After the rain**.
- Older settings files load unchanged.
- An unknown weather value in a settings file falls back to Clear.

## 7. The Window Kit (shared)

A reusable window that any world can put between its room and its view. The later backlog window worlds (Mansion Panes, Attic Thunderstorm, Night Train, Bookshop Rain) build on it.

### 7.1 Frost field (Core, deterministic)
- When a world loads, it **grows a branching crystal pattern once**: a grid of **384 × 216** "arrival times" (0…1).
- **Seeds:** the corners first, then along the frame bars, then a few random "flaws" in the glass.
- **Growth:** an anisotropic flood (fast-marching style). Steps along each seed's **six crystal axes** (60° apart, randomly rotated per seed) are cheap; steps across them are expensive. A little noise splits the fronts into fern-like branches.
- The result is stored as a 16-bit texture.
- **Frost shows wherever arrival time < the current frost level**, so fronts advance and retreat along real branches.
- The same seed always gives the same field. Generation is **< 150 ms** and runs off the main thread when the world loads; a plain radial fallback is drawn until it's ready.

### 7.2 Glass layer
Drawn over the view, inside the frame:
- **Frost:**
  - The field, thresholded by the frost level, with a soft feathered edge and fine crystal detail.
  - **Glints:** the crystal facets twinkle with the light that falls on them:
    - firelight or room light from inside (warm)
    - sun or moonlight from outside (gold, or silver-blue)
- **Clear patches:** up to 24 (Smooth) or 48 (High and Ultra) circles from the world's visual, each with a radius and a warmth. A patch clears the frost; as its warmth falls, frost regrows into it in the field's own order.
- **Frosted-glass scattering:**
  - On **High and Ultra**, the outside view is drawn first, a small blurred mip chain of it is made, and frosted areas sample the blur and brighten it, as real frost scatters light.
  - On **Smooth**, the view under frost turns a lit frost colour taken from the scene's average light.
- **Condensation:** a soft band of fog along the bottom of each pane (its height is a setting), with occasional **drips** running down from it.
- **Raindrops:** the weather layer's beads, as now.
- **After-rain drips:** while wet and not raining, the odd drop runs down from the top of the frame.
- **Stuck flakes:** while snowing, a few flakes land on the glass as tiny six-armed stars that melt over 5–10 s.
- **Reflection:** at night the glass faintly reflects the room's light: the fire's glow (cabin) or the fairy lights (bedroom). It's about 4–8% at normal viewing, so it shows when the outside is darker than the room.

### 7.3 Frame and room
- The frame is parameterised by:
  - panes (rows × columns)
  - bar width
  - material: **log wood** with grain (cabin) or **painted sash** (bedroom)
- It's lit by the room's light (the fire's flicker from below left, or the morning light).
- The room shows only at the edges of the frame, as dark walls lit by the room light.
- **Indoor acoustics:** window worlds send `indoors` with their ambience (§5).

### 7.4 Draw order (window worlds)
sky → far scenery → near scenery → weather (outside) → [High/Ultra: blur copy] → glass (frost, condensation, beads, flakes, reflection) → frame and sill → room effects (light shafts, motes, curtains) → keys

## 8. Set 5: Snowbound Cabin Window

### 8.1 World
- **Camera:** fixed, inside the cabin. The window fills about 70% of the frame and faces the equator, so the sun and moon cross it. The sky's horizon sits in the lower third of the panes.
- **Outside, back to front:**
  1. **Living Sky** with halos, sundogs, moon halos and pastel dawns.
  2. **Mountains:** two snowy ridges in aerial haze. **Alpenglow** lights the peaks pink-orange when the sun is between −2° and +8°.
  3. **Valley forest:**
     - three layers of snow-laden pine silhouettes, with snow on the branch tops lit by the sky
     - a **far cabin** whose window lights up at dusk and goes out late at night

  4. **Near ground:**

  Indoors is 1. Closeness and density follow the mood.

### 8.2 Visual: Frost Grow
- **Frost level:**
  - The level is `base + grow`.
  - **base** = `SkyState.frost` × the Frost setting.
  - **grow** rises while notes are held, or while the sustain pedal holds them, toward **0.75 × Frost** (τ about 6 s). It falls back with τ about 20 s in silence.
  - The level glides with τ about 2 s, and is capped at **0.85**, so the centre of the window never frosts over.
- **Patches:**
  - **Each note** melts a patch at its key's position across the window: x by pitch, using the shared key layout, mapped to 0.08–0.92 of the window's width.
  - Its height is **0.3 + 0.45 × velocity** (harder notes reach higher), and its radius is **0.025 + 0.05 × velocity** of the window's height.
  - **Held** notes keep their patch fully warm.
  - On **release**, warmth falls with τ about 3.5 s, and the patch is removed below 0.02.
- **Chords** (3 or more notes within about 60 ms) make the **fire flare**:
  - The flare rises with τ about 0.15 s and falls with τ about 1.5 s.
  - It warms the frame, the reflections and the crystal glints.
  - Its colour is the fire's own: a deeper ember for minor and a brighter gold for major, never anything unnatural.
- **High notes** (MIDI ≥ 72) make the snowflakes nearest the glass catch the window light.
- **Budget:**
  - patches: 24 on Smooth, 48 on High and Ultra, strongest kept, in their original order
  - at most **12 new patches per frame**, so a 2,000-note burst streams out over frames
- **Gentle visuals:** the flare and glints go through the **FlashLimiter**, halved.
- **Parameters:**
  - Visual (Frost Grow / Galaxy Warp)
  - Hold the light
  - Frost, Fire glow, Condensation, Distant cabin
  - Show keys, Keyboard height

## 9. Set 6: Bedroom Window at Dawn

### 9.1 World
- **Camera:** fixed, from the room toward a painted sash window behind sheer curtains. The wall and the floor near the window are at the frame's edges.
- **Outside, back to front:**
  1. **Living Sky** (Pastel dawn by default), with the sun rising between chimneys.
  2. **Low hills**, then a row of **rooftops and chimneys**. Chimney smoke shows on cold mornings, when frost > 0.2.
  3. **The street:**
     - houses across the road whose **windows light up one by one** as the morning comes, and go dark late at night
     - a **street lamp** that switches off when the sun rises above −2° and back on below −3°, with a 3 s warm-up flicker
  4. **A big tree** at one side. Its leaves move in the wind and the odd bird hops or flies off.
  5. **The street surface:** with wetness, it mirrors the sky, the lamp and the lit windows.
  6. **Weather** outside the glass, and mist between the houses after the rain.
- **At the window:**
  - the Window Kit glass: drops, morning condensation, frost on cold days
  - a painted sash frame
  - on the sill, an original **plant** and a **mug with steam** curling up (the steam bends when the window is open)
- **In the room:**
  - **Light shafts:** soft beams from the sun through the panes into dusty air. They're drawn analytically from the panes and the sun's direction, with no screen sampling. On High and Ultra a light noise gives them density.
  - **Window light** falls on the floor below the window, with the frame bars' shadows and **dappled leaf shadows** from the tree that move with the wind.
  - **Fairy lights** on the curtain rod, on from dusk (sun < 2°), with an adjustable level.
  - At night, the street lamp's glow comes through the curtains.
- **Window open a crack** (a setting): the curtains move more, the mug's steam bends, and `indoors` drops to 0.5.
- **Ambience:**

| Source | Level |
|---|---|
| dawn chorus | 0.6 from first light to mid-morning |
| kettle | 0.5 |
| waking street | rises from 0.15 before dawn to 0.6 by mid-morning, lower at night |
| breeze | 0.3 + wind |
| drips | wetness, while not raining |
| rain on glass | rain |
| clock | 0.15 |

  Indoors is 1, or 0.5 with the window open.

### 9.2 Cloth (Core)
- Two sheer curtain panels, each a **24 × 32** grid of points.
- **Integration:** Verlet, at a fixed 120 Hz substep (with the remainder carried over), with **6 constraint iterations** (structural and shear springs).
- **Top row:** pinned to rings on the rod.
- **Forces:**
  - gravity
  - damping
  - the **breeze**: the sky's wind, ×2 with the window open, as a slowly varying field
  - **puffs** from the visual (§9.3)
- **Safety:** displacement is clamped, so it can never explode.
- It's deterministic for a given seed and input, and costs **< 0.3 ms per frame**.
- The renderer draws each panel as a triangle mesh.
- **Light through the cloth:**
  - Translucency follows how the fabric faces the light and how folded it is: folds are denser and glow at their edges when back-lit.
  - The outside shows softly through the sheer.

### 9.3 Visual: Curtain Light
- **Morning glow:**
  - Holding notes raises the room's **morning glow** (τ about 2.5 s up, about 6 s back down when released).
  - The glow brightens the light coming through the cloth, the shafts, the floor patch and the warmth of the light.
  - It's capped, so the room never blows out.
- **Puffs:**
  - **Each note** sends a puff of air into the cloth at its key's x position, using the shared key layout.
  - Its strength comes from velocity.
  - It spreads sideways at about 1.5 m/s, so a run ripples across the curtains like wind.
- **Dust motes:**
  - A pool of **200 (Smooth) or 400 (High and Ultra)** motes drifts in the shafts.
  - **High notes** (MIDI ≥ 72) make 3–6 motes near a height set by pitch **glint**. The glints decay over about 0.8 s.
- **Chord tint:**
  - **Chords** tint the light: **gold** for major, **rose** for minor, **lilac** for tense.
  - The tint glides in RGB over about 1 s, so it never passes through other colours, and fades back to neutral when the chord lets go.
- **At night** (sun < −4°), notes **twinkle the fairy light** nearest their x.
- **Budget:** at most 12 new puffs and glint events per frame. A 2,000-note burst is rationed.
- **Gentle visuals:** the glints, twinkles and glow swells go through the **FlashLimiter**, halved.
- **Parameters:**
  - Visual (Curtain Light / Frost Grow / Galaxy Warp)
  - Hold the light
  - Morning light, Curtain sway, Dust motes, Fairy lights, Window open
  - Show keys, Keyboard height
- **Frost Grow in the bedroom** works unchanged through the Window Kit.

## 12. Performance (the rule for every new world)
- **Render size:** ≤ 1 MP on Smooth.
- **Noise:** at most 3 octaves (2 on Smooth where noted).
- **No per-pixel ray marching** anywhere.
- **Frost:** one texture read plus a loop over at most 24 patches on Smooth.
- **Shafts:** analytic beams, with no screen sampling.
- **Frosted-glass blur:** only on High and Ultra.
- **Cloth:** < 0.3 ms per frame on the CPU.
- **Frame budget:** each window world holds its budget with a **2,000-note burst** (tested), as the beach and road do.
- **Halos and the Belt of Venus:** a few dot products per sky pixel.
- **Buffers:** reused from rings as the weather layer does, never made per frame.

## 13. Reliability

| Situation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Sample-rate change, odd buffer sizes, switching instruments while notes are held | As the other built-in engines (same plumbing and tests) |
| Horn: the top note is released while other notes are held | The horn releases. It never jumps down |
| Horn: many notes at once | Only a note that's the highest held key when pressed takes the horn |
| Morning Box Build when playing stops and starts | The build level falls smoothly; it never resets with a jump |
| Frost field still generating when the world appears | A plain radial fallback is drawn until it's ready |
| 2,000-note burst | Patches, puffs and glints are capped and rationed; the frame budget holds |
| Cloth after a long stall or a huge `dt` | `dt` is clamped and substeps capped; displacement is clamped |
| Weather changes to rain in the cabin | It falls as snow |
| An old settings file | It loads; an unknown weather falls back to Clear |
| Gentle visuals | Flares, glints, twinkles and glow swells go through the FlashLimiter |
