## 1. Purpose

This run is about the lights of home: a whole city asleep below a hill, and the last tram carrying you back through it. It adds two worlds, two instruments, a sky feature and a weather, and two pieces of instrument core:
- the **organ core**, new: a tone bank, drawbar ranks, key contacts, percussion, scanner and rotor, plus flue pipes;
- the **piano core** grows: honky unisons, worn hammers and the shared mechanics, so a third piano joins Felt Glass and Cabin Felt.

As always, each set has a world, a visual, an original instrument, a sky feature, a weather and ambience sounds, all tied together by a mood. The planets reach every world with a night sky. The Cool Change reaches every outdoor world.

### Success looks like
1. **Hilltop Organ sounds like a real organ on a hill at night.** Soft drawbar registrations sit warmly under jazz chords; each key speaks with the faint click of its contacts; the rotor turns slowly and races when you push the wheel, the horn arriving before the drum; a small pipe rank stands still beneath it; the valley sends back a far echo; and the swell breathes with your touch. It blends when you play along with "Small Worlds" (F major, 78 BPM).
2. **The Last-Call Upright sounds like the bar's old piano after last call.** Its unisons drift apart and beat, its hammers are worn bright, the lid can be shut or open, the empty room answers, and the record's lo-fi warmth is a dial away. It blends with "Everybody" (C major, felt at 76).
3. Both worlds look stunning and stay smooth on **Smooth** on the Intel MacBook Pro.
4. **Real planets** shine in every world with a night sky: Venus first in the twilight, then Jupiter, then the stars; Mars orange; Saturn a steady gold that brightens as its rings open. Mars and Jupiter pass 1.2° apart on 15–16 November 2026; the Moon slides past Jupiter on 2 November 2026; Venus reaches 47° from the Sun on 3 January 2027.
5. **The Cool Change** comes through every outdoor world: a hot, dusty northerly, the dark arc of the roll cloud rising from the south-west, a lull, the gust front, the wind's turn and a few big drops, then cool, clean air with sharp stars.
6. **Ambience in key grows:** the far train's horn, the tram's bell and the stop chime ring in your key.
7. One tap on a set loads everything, and each part can still be swapped. Demo clips of both instruments and both ambiences come with the release.

### Standing rules (unchanged)
- One world, one visual, one instrument, ambience, a sky feature and a weather feature per set, tied by mood. Playing along should blend, never clash.
- Sounds are original synthesis: no samples, and no protected library is copied, extracted, converted or decrypted. The research names real instruments and products only to learn how they work; no product name, trademark or registration list appears in the app.
- **Credits are text and a Spotify link only:** no artwork, lyrics, logos or photos, and no imitation of music videos. Neither card nor world draws on *Swimming*'s or *Circles*' artwork.
- **Card art is original**, from the song's feeling, with tempo, key and track number as easter eggs. Cards breathe at the felt pulse.
- Every world runs on Smooth on the Intel Mac: ≤ 1 MP, no per-pixel ray marching, ≤ 3 noise octaves, and no buffers made per frame.
- Fireballs, sky brightenings, sparks, beacons and glow swells go through `FlashLimiter`.
- Sets and settings saved by older builds still load.
- Personal use: the app is Michael's own.
- **Respect over cliché.**
  - The city is original and generic, never a recognisable skyline. Its lights are people's homes, and the visual treats them gently.
  - The tram carries no operator's name, livery, logo, route number or destination text. Its passengers are quiet, anonymous silhouettes (someone dozing against a window, a night-shift worker going home), never comic.
  - The upright's looseness is an old instrument's, not a joke about drinking.
  - The Cool Change is weather people love; its dust and gusts never become disaster imagery (no fire, no smoke plumes).

### Research that shaped the design
- **"Small Worlds":**
  - **The release:** Mac Miller, *Swimming* (2018), **track 8** (4:31). It was the album's first single, released on 30 May 2018 with the non-album songs "Buttons" and "Programs"; the album followed on 3 August 2018 (Wikipedia, *Swimming*; Discogs CD credits).
  - **The players:** guitar, **John Mayer**; organ, **Jon Brion**; piano, **Aja Grant**; produced by Tae Beast, with additional production by Carter Lang (Discogs CD credits; Wikipedia personnel; Genius credits). Mayer: "when I heard 'Small Worlds' … I grabbed the nearest guitar in the room and within a couple of hours we had finished a tune" (NME, 2018).
  - **Key and tempo:** **F major at 78 BPM.** Tunebat, GetSongBPM and SongBPM (79) all take this from Spotify's analysis, so they count as one source. Chordify's own transcription gives F major at 77, and two hand transcriptions agree on F: Ultimate Guitar (Fmaj7 | Gm7 | C7♭9) and a guitarist's analysis ("F major/D minor … one note outside the scale, D♭"; r/JohnMayer). AcousticBrainz's low-confidence estimate says B♭ major at 76. The last minute changes pace (themusicalhype); Hooktheory analyses that outro in B♭ minor at 154, double time.
  - **The felt pulse is 78.** The song is already slow, so there is no half-time feel to find; the card breathes at 78.
  - **The sound:** "airy jazz" over a "slow and steady R&B" foundation (Spin, 2018); "a placid, languid flow with soft guitar, basic drums, and drifting vocals" with "a mild beat switch to piano and snapping near the end" (musiciwant); jazz chords (Fmaj7, Gm7, A7, C7, D♭7: Chordify). The organ sits under the guitar and piano as a warm bed.
- **"Everybody":**
  - **The release:** Mac Miller, *Circles* (2020), **track 6** (4:16), released on 17 January 2020; it debuted at #80 on the Hot 100 (Wikipedia, *Circles*; Spotify; Genius).
  - **The song:** a cover of **Arthur Lee's "Everybody's Gotta Live"**, a single in June 1972 and track 7 of his first solo album, *Vindicator* (A&M); Love re-recorded it on *Reel to Real* (1974) (Wikipedia). Produced by Mac Miller and Jon Brion, with Miller on vocals and bass and Brion and Tony Royster Jr. on drums (Wikipedia; Genius). Brion "heard 'Everybody' and thought it should be included" (Vulture, 2020).
  - **Key and tempo:** **C major**, per Musicnotes' piano solo of this recording ("C Major", 4/4) and Chordify (C major; chords C, E, Am, Cmaj7, E7). Spotify's analysis (Tunebat, SongBPM) hears its relative, A minor; the verse turns Cmaj7–E7–Am (Ukutabs). The felt pulse is about **76**: Spotify counts 152, which SongBPM halves to 76, and Chordify gives 75. Musicnotes marks its arrangement ♩ = 137 and musiciwant gives 60; neither matches the other analyses. The card breathes at 76.
  - **The piano:** "The piano starts off drinking on 'Everybody'", before the track turns "Ringo-rhythmic with a double tracked snare snap" (Variety, 2020); "Miller alone with a piano opens" it (The High Note); "slowed down, piano-driven and shortened" from Lee's original (KTSW); "an existential ballad from 1972 sung here as bluesman mumblecore" (The Guardian, 2020); the album leans on "lo-fi piano" (Rolling Stone, 2020).
- **The organ core:**
  - **Drawbars** mix sine-like harmonics named after pipe lengths: 16′ (an octave below), 5⅓′ (the fifth above the 8′), 8′ (the fundamental), 4′, 2⅔′, 2′, 1⅗′, 1⅓′ and 1′, which are harmonics 1, 3, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 16 of the 16′ (Apple's MainStage manual; HammondWiki). Each drawbar step is about 3 dB (HammondWiki).
  - **The tone generator:** 91 tonewheels over seven octaves, geared to approximate equal temperament within about 2 cents (the top seven wheels average 1.64 cents sharp). A drawbar adds the nearest *tempered* wheel, not a true harmonic, so the 2⅔′ is a tempered twelfth and the 1⅗′ a tempered third (Electric Druid; HammondWiki; quadibloc). Harmonics beyond the generator's range fold back by an octave (ISMA 2019, Non-linearities of the Hammond tone generator; Electric Druid).
  - **Key click:** each key closes nine contacts "more or less simultaneously" (HammondWiki); the switch's inconsistency is "responsible for generating the transient", and key velocity influences it "to a certain extent" (Moro, McPherson and Sandler, via ISMA 2019). Click was first thought a defect, then became part of the sound (Wikipedia).
  - **Leakage:** pickups catch neighbouring wheels; the crosstalk is "now considered part of the signature" (Wikipedia; HammondWiki).
  - **Percussion:** a second- or third-harmonic "ping", **single-triggered** (only from an all-keys-up state), which borrows the ninth drawbar's contact so the 1′ goes silent; the service check expects the fast decay to die away in about one second and the normal one in about four (Hammond-Leslie FAQ; Cherry Audio's Blue3 manual). A key pressed too soon after release finds the circuit only part recharged (Roland V-Stage manual).
  - **Scanner vibrato and chorus:** a delay line of low-pass sections read by a motor-driven capacitive scanner at a fixed **6.9 Hz**, with three depths; the widest vibrato bends the pitch by "about 1½%", and chorus mixes the vibrato with the dry signal (Martinic manual; the A-100 service manual).
  - **The rotary speaker:** an 800 Hz crossover, a rotating treble horn and a bass speaker firing into a rotating drum (Will Pirkle's rotary speaker app note). Slow (chorale) about **0.8 Hz** for the horn and **0.7 Hz** for the drum; fast (tremolo) about **6.8 Hz** and **5.6 Hz** (the norns *leslie* defaults, which "track the Leslie 122"; Cherry Audio's defaults are 0.75/6.75 and 0.73/6.4 Hz). The horn spins up in about 1.2 s and the drum in about 6 s (Cherry Audio). The rotors turn in opposite directions, and the horn's level peaks sharply as it faces you (US patent 6,873,708). Doppler is simulated with a modulated delay line, its depth the horn's tangential speed over the speed of sound (Smith, Serafin, Abel and Berners, DAFx 2002; Smith, *Physical Audio Signal Processing*). Pekonen et al. (DAFx 2011) model the organ cheaply.
  - **Pipes:** flutes speak fastest; the principal (diapason) family has "a strong second partial, especially in the attack"; strings are slow (Angster, Acoustics Today 2017). A Principal 8′ "starts with the chiff, a short noise that precedes the harmonic sound", centred near the third harmonic (Braasch and Ahrens). Broadband noise at the mouth sets the spectrum's floor (Angster). During the attack an open pipe can sound its octave and twelfth almost at once, and its pitch settles as it steadies (Pykett).
- **The Last-Call Upright:**
  - **Honky-tonk** comes from detuned unisons: "one of the two or three strings is moved slightly out of tune" (Don't Shoot the Piano Tuner, 2016); a technician found ±2–3 cents "way too tame" and ended near "a difference of twenty-four cents in one unison" (Pianotech list). Hardening the hammers gives "a brighter, harsher sound which is reminiscent of what happens when normal hammers become grooved through many years of use"; tacks and the "mandolin rail" give a tinny effect but damage the hammers (the same blog; Perfessor Bill).
  - **Mistuned unisons give the piano's double decay:** a loud prompt sound and a long, quiet aftersound (Weinreich, *Five Lectures on the Acoustics of the Piano*); unisons are mistuned by around 1 Hz (Keane, NZ Acoustics 2004).
  - **Hammers:** hard hammers sound "bright, tinny, or harsh", soft ones dull; felt is a hardening spring, so loud notes are brighter (Russell, Penn State). Heavily grooved hammers "can result in a dull or metallic sound" (Don't Shoot the Piano Tuner, on voicing).
  - **Uprights** have short strings and more inharmonicity (Keane), which Cabin Felt already models (`kUprightB`).
- **Real planets:**
  - **Positions:** JPL's *Approximate Positions of the Planets* (Standish and Williams): Keplerian elements and rates for 1800–2050, with nominal heliocentric errors of 15″ (Mercury), 20″ (Venus and the Earth–Moon barycentre), 40″ (Mars), 400″ (Jupiter) and 600″ (Saturn), and the algorithm itself (elements at T, Kepler's equation, rotation to the J2000 ecliptic, then equatorial with ε = 23.43928°).
  - **Brightness:** Mallama and Hilton (2018), the formulas *The Astronomical Almanac* has used since 2021 (USNO). Mercury: a sixth-order phase polynomial (V₁(0) = −0.613). Venus: −4.384 + … up to α = 163.7°, a second formula beyond. Mars: −1.601 + 0.02267α − 0.0001302α². Jupiter: −9.395 − 3.7×10⁻⁴α + 6.16×10⁻⁴α². Saturn with its rings: −8.914 − 1.825 sin β + 0.026α − 0.378 sin β e^(−2.25α), with β the effective ring inclination (√(β_E β_S), or 0 when the Earth and the Sun see opposite faces). Each adds 5 log₁₀(rd). Venus is brightest at a mean −4.81, about 37° from the Sun.
  - **Colour:** B−V is 0.97 for Mercury, 0.81 for Venus, 1.43 for Mars, 0.87 for Jupiter and 1.09 for Saturn (Wikipedia, colour index). Most planets are yellowish, differing mainly in lightness (Young, *Sky & Telescope* 1985). Planets shine steadily: Saturn is "a steady yellowish point that does not twinkle like a star" (Farmers' Almanac).
  - **Saturn's rings:** edge-on to Earth on 23 March 2025; opening (south face) −9.16° on 21 July 2026, −6.09° on 6 December 2026 and −14.44° on 3 August 2027 (Abrams Planetarium, *Appearance of Saturn's Rings 2025–2039*). About 7.5° at the 4 October 2026 opposition, when Saturn shines at +0.3 (EarthSky; Space & Telescope). Fully open, about 27°, around 2032.
  - **Jupiter's moons:** Meeus' lower-accuracy method (*Astronomical Algorithms*, ch. 44) is good to about 0.1–0.25 Jupiter radii (Burnett's checks); its Example 44.a gives Io at X = −3.44, Y = +0.21 on 16 December 1992, 0h (as quoted on Space Exploration Stack Exchange).
  - **Events for tests** (all UT):
    - **Venus:** superior conjunction on 6 January 2026. Greatest eastern elongation 45.9° on 15 August 2026 (about 06 UT; EarthSky, BBC Sky at Night, In-The-Sky.org); from mid-southern latitudes it then stands about 30° up in the west an hour after sunset, at −4.4 (Astronomy, Southern Hemisphere sky, August 2026). Greatest evening brightness on 18–22 September; inferior conjunction on 24 October 2026; greatest morning brilliancy about −4.9 around 25–29 November 2026 (Telescope Advisor; timeanddate gives 27 November). Greatest western elongation **46°57′ at 17:57–17:58 UT on 3 January 2027**, at −4.5 to −4.6 (Star Walk; Forecast Bar; Stargazing Buddy, from astronomy-engine).
    - **Mercury:** greatest elongations on 19 February (18.1°), 3 April (27.8°), 15 June (24.5°), 2 August (19.5°), 12 October (25.2°) and 20–21 November 2026 (19.6°, 23:31 UT on the 20th, −0.6) (Sira Uysal's 2026 calendar; Stargazing Buddy; Forecast Bar).
    - **Conjunctions:** Venus 1.6° from Jupiter on 9 June 2026 (Sky & Telescope, via Forbes; NASA's *What's Up*). Mars 1.2° (1°11′) north of Jupiter on 15–16 November 2026 (Night Sky Today; Star Walk; BBC Sky at Night). The Moon 29.1′ from Jupiter at 22:45 UT on 2 November 2026, occulting it from parts of Australia (In-The-Sky.org, from DE440).
    - **Oppositions:** Saturn on 4 October 2026 (+0.3); Jupiter on 10–11 February 2027 (−2.6, Leo); Mars on 19 February 2027 (−1.2, Leo) (EarthSky; Stargazing Buddy).
- **The Cool Change:**
  - **What it is:** on a "change day", north-westerlies drag hot interior air over Melbourne; the south-westerlies behind the front come off the Southern Ocean, so temperatures "can sometimes fall 15 °C or more in a matter of minutes", and once the fronts round Cape Otway "they often accelerate up the coast towards Melbourne and produce strong, squally winds" (Bureau of Meteorology blog, *Meet the locals*). Ahead of a front, hot northerlies "push Melbourne's temperature into the mid-40s"; after it, the temperature "can drop by up to 20" degrees (BoM, *What influences air temperature?*). The typical sequence is warm northerlies with clear skies, then a front from the ocean when "everything can change within minutes", with cloud, rain and sometimes thunderstorms (BoM, *Why Melbourne can have 4 seasons in one day*, 2026).
  - **How sharp:** "Hot, gusty northerly winds often strengthen with the approach of a southerly change"; hot weather "usually ends with an abrupt cool change when the temperature may fall by 10°C in 20 minutes. Some of these changes are dry; others are accompanied by showers and thunderstorms" (*Encyclopedia of Melbourne*). Dry summertime fronts "are commonly called cool changes", preceded by dry north or north-west winds from the interior; Melbourne's largest day-to-day drop on record is 24.9 °C on 13 December 1924 (Reeder et al., *Geophysical Research Letters* 2015; Reeder and Smith 1987 describe the change's dynamics). Black Saturday's late-afternoon change behaved like "an unsteady gravity current" (Engel et al. 2013).
  - **Its cloud and dust:** a southerly buster's leading edge "is marked by a spectacular roll cloud", with gusts to 24.7 m/s and temperature falls of more than 21 °C (Colquhoun et al., *Monthly Weather Review* 1985). On 8 February 1983 a dry change gathered Mallee and Wimmera topsoil "into a large cloud oriented along the line of a cool change": Melbourne reached 43.2 °C at 2:25 pm, then "a spectacular reddish-brown cloud" arrived just before 3 pm, "accompanied by a rapid temperature drop, and a squally wind change", with visibility down to 100 m and the dust cloud up to 320 m deep (ABS *Year Book Australia 2008*, from the Bureau).
  - **How it is forecast and watched:** forecasts give the wind's turn ("northwesterly 20 to 30 km/h turning west to southwesterly during the day"; Bureau forecast text, via WillyWeather). Watchers follow it arriving through the coastal stations west of Melbourne (Cape Otway, Aireys Inlet, Point Wilson, Avalon; BoM's Victorian coastal station list), whose half-hourly wind and temperature show the change coming (McCarthy, *The timing of cool changes in Melbourne*, 2014). Since Ash Wednesday, fire-weather briefings carry "regular updates on the progress of wind changes" (ABS 2008).
- **The worlds:**
  - **A city's lights from a hill:** Melbourne's distributors have replaced most residential streetlights with LEDs since 2009 (63% of CitiPower's legacy lights, 86% of Powercor's), while major roads' high-pressure sodium lamps change over only as they wear out (CitiPower and Powercor public lighting proposals, AER 2025). New residential LEDs are often 3000 K and main roads 4000 K (an Ausgrid program, NSW). Sky glow depends on distance, city size and the air; long wavelengths scatter least (US DOE / PNNL 2017). So the default city is warm-white grids crossed by orange arterials, with a cooler white CBD.
  - **Melbourne's late trams:** on Friday and Saturday nights since 2016, the Night Network runs six tram routes every half hour all night (Wikipedia, *Night Network*; Transport Victoria). A tram driver's floor carries two foot buttons, the **gong** and the **sand**; a heritage tram's "dugga-dugga" is its compressor refilling the air tank, and trams braking on sanded rail seem to stop "in a cloud of dust" (Melbourne Tram Museum). A driver remembers the night shift as "a lot quieter", and "doing the last tram from the terminus and running in was good" (Carolyn Cleak, Melbourne Tram Museum).

## 4. The instruments

### 4.1 The organ core (`OrganCore.hpp`)
- **The tone bank:** 91 tones like a tonewheel generator's. Tone w (1…91) sounds at 440 × 2^((w − 46)/12) Hz × Fine tune, from C1 (32.70 Hz) to F♯8 (5,919.9 Hz).
  - Each tone is a recursive phasor (renormalised every 1,024 samples), so tones never restart for a new note: a held chord's partials keep the steady, locked phases of a real generator. While the organ sleeps, each tone's phase is recomputed from the sample clock when it wakes, so it stays locked.
  - Each tone carries a trace of its own second harmonic (−38 dB, from 2·sin·cos), and the top seven tones a stronger one (−30 dB): the real generator's top octave has no filters, which gives its squeal (ISMA 2019).
  - The tones are equal-tempered. A rank takes the nearest tempered tone, so the 2⅔′ is a tempered twelfth (2^(19/12) = 2.9966 × f) and the 1⅗′ a tempered third (2^(28/12) = 5.0397 × f, 13.7 cents above the true fifth harmonic). Two keys that share a tone (C4's 4′ and C5's 8′) share it exactly, so they never beat.
- **Ranks (drawbars):** nine footages, offset from the key's 8′ note by −12 (16′), +7 (5⅓′), 0 (8′), +12 (4′), +19 (2⅔′), +24 (2′), +28 (1⅗′), +31 (1⅓′) and +36 (1′) semitones.
  - Each has a level of 0…8. The gain is 0 at level 0, else 10^(−3(8 − level)/20), so each step is 3 dB. Levels in between follow the same curve.
- **Foldback:** a rank whose tone would fall below 1 or above 91 takes the tone an octave inside, as often as needed, as the real generator folds. So any MIDI note from 0 to 127 plays.
- **Buses:** each tone's bus is the sum of the rank gains of every held key that uses it, as in the real organ, where one wheel feeds every key that needs it.
  - The buses update once per 32-sample block, with linear ramps.
  - Each sample sums tone × bus over the 91 tones: 91 multiply-adds, however many keys are down.
- **Key contacts and the click:**
  - A key closes nine contacts, one per rank, 0.2–3 ms apart in a seeded random order per press. The spread is multiplied by (1.3 − 0.6 × velocity), so soft presses close more raggedly.
  - Each closure is a step in that rank's tone. Its click is the step itself: the tone's value at that instant × the rank's gain, through a 1 ms high-passed decay (a one-pole high-pass at 1.2 kHz and a 0.8 ms exponential), with one to three bounces in the first millisecond at 30–70% strength.
  - Release opens the contacts in reverse order with half the click.
  - The steady tone enters through 0.4 ms ramps, so the only click is the modelled one. A click amount of 0 lengthens the ramps to 5 ms, and nothing clicks.
- **Leakage:** each tone with a live bus leaks into four seeded neighbours (the tones that would share its compartment and shaft), at −44 to −56 dB relative to its own level. It is heard as a faint whisper under held chords.
- **Percussion, single-triggered:**
  - It is armed when every key is up. The first key down, and any others within 30 ms, fire it. While any key stays down, new keys don't.
  - It sounds the firing keys' 2⅔′ tones (third harmonic; the 4′ is a core option) and decays with T60 1.0 s (the fast decay; 4 s is the slow option).
  - While percussion is on, the 1′ rank is silent, because the circuit takes its contact.
  - **Recharge:** if fewer than 120 ms passed since the last key went up, it fires at the share recharged, 1 − e^(−t/40 ms).
- **Scanner vibrato and chorus:**
  - A 1.4 ms delay line read by a tap swept in a triangle at 6.9 Hz over a third, a half or all of the line (depths 1–3). The pitch moves about ±0.5%, ±0.8% or ±1.5%. Reads use Lagrange-3 interpolation.
  - The line darkens a little with depth (a one-pole from 9 kHz to 6 kHz), as the real delay line does.
  - Chorus mixes the swept output 1:1 with the dry one.
- **The preamp's drive:** an asymmetric soft clip, softSat(d·(x + 0.12x²))/d, with d from 1 to 6. The second harmonic comes first, then a growl.
- **The rotor:**
  - **Crossover:** 800 Hz, fourth-order Linkwitz–Riley.
  - **The horn:**
    - Each mic reads a modulated delay: 0.6 ms ± 0.44 ms × sin(θ − φ_mic), the Doppler of a mouth turning at a radius of 0.15 m.
    - A peaky level, 0.25 + 0.75·((1 + cos(θ − φ_mic))/2)^1.6, as the horn sweeps past.
    - A two-pole low-pass swinging from 3.5 kHz to 9 kHz with the angle.
  - **The drum:** turns the other way, with a level swing of 0.35 (the scoop) and a 0.3 ms Doppler.
  - **Speeds:**
    - The horn: 0.80 Hz slow, 6.8 Hz fast.
    - The drum: 0.70 Hz slow, 5.6 Hz fast.
  - **Inertia:**
    - The horn moves toward its new speed with τ 0.45 s up and 0.6 s down, arriving in about 1.2–1.5 s.
    - The drum moves with τ 2.0 s up and 1.6 s down, arriving in about 5–6 s.
    - So the treble races ahead and the bass catches up.
  - **Brake:** a stop slows both rotors at their slow-down rates, and the horn comes to rest at a random angle.
  - **The cabinet:** four early reflections per mic, at 2.1, 3.4, 5.2 and 7.9 ms and −10 to −18 dB.
  - **The mics:** two on the horn at ±(15° + 75° × width), and the drum's panned ±0.3 × width.
  - **Cost:** three short Lagrange-read delays, three filters and two angle oscillators: about 60 multiplies per sample.
- **Flue pipe ranks** (the core's second voice):
  - **A principal's partials:** 1, 0.55, 0.35, 0.22, 0.12 and 0.08. A flute's: 1, 0.25, 0.12 and 0.05.
  - **Speech:**
    - The second partial speaks first (onset 10–15 ms), the fundamental by 30–45 ms, and the upper partials at 20–30 ms.
    - A **chiff:** noise band-passed at the third harmonic (Q 4), decaying over 25 ms, at −18 dB relative to the steady tone.
    - **Wind:** a breath at −46 dB, shaped at the second harmonic.
    - A pitch settle from −6 cents to 0 over 50 ms.
  - **Release:** 40 ms, with the wind's last breath.
  - **Voices:** 12 (pool 16). Each costs six partials and two band-passes.
- **Cost:** the tone bank and buses take about 700 multiplies per sample; the rotor about 60; the pipes about 30 per voice. The whole core stays well under 6% with every key down.
- **Future use:** this starts the organ core. Later organs (a reed organ, a chapel or church organ, a fairground band organ) can reuse its tone bank, ranks, contacts, percussion and rotor.

### 4.2 Hilltop Organ (after "Small Worlds")
A warm drawbar organ on a hill at night. Soft registrations sit under jazz chords, a slow rotor drifts like the wind, a small pipe rank stands still beneath it, the valley sends back a far echo, and the swell breathes with your touch.
- **Polyphony:** no limit for the tone bank, which sounds every key at once (a held ten-note chord costs what one note costs). The pipe rank has 12 voices (pool 16).
- **Range:** the whole keyboard, through foldback.
- **Touch:** organ notes don't respond to velocity, and Hilltop's don't either, but velocity shapes the click (0.5 + 0.5 v) and the contacts' stagger, and it drives the Swell (below).
- **Registration (0…1):** morphs the drawbars through five anchors, each drawbar moving smoothly between neighbouring anchors.

  | Registration | Anchor | 16′ | 5⅓′ | 8′ | 4′ | 2⅔′ | 2′ | 1⅗′ | 1⅓′ | 1′ |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0.00 | Whisper | 0 | 0 | 5 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  | 0.25 | Hilltop | 3 | 0 | 7 | 5 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  | 0.50 | Ballad | 6 | 0 | 8 | 6 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  | 0.75 | Jazz | 8 | 8 | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  | 1.00 | Full | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 |

  At the default 0.35 the drawbars read about 4, 0, 7.4, 5.4, 3.8: round and airy.
- **Ping (0…1):** the percussion's level, on the third harmonic with the fast decay. 0 is off; 0.4 is soft (about −12 dB relative to a full 8′); 1 is normal (about −5 dB). The 1′ falls silent while it's on.
- **Click (0…1):** the contacts' click and the tones' leakage (leakage at −48 dB on average × (0.3 + 0.7 × Click)). 0 is a clean, modern organ; 1 is an old organ's every contact and whisper.
- **Rotor (0…1):** the mix of the rotary speaker against the straight (chorused) sound: 0 straight, 1 all rotor.
- **Keys (0…1, default 0.22):** a light piano layered under the organ, after Aja Grant's piano on "Small Worlds". It is a felt piano from the shared piano core (`PianoStrings.hpp`), 12 voices (pool 16), struck by velocity: the one part of Hilltop that answers how hard you play. It sits outside the rotor, dry of the valley echo, and is high-passed at 180 Hz so it never muddies the 16′. 0 is organ alone.
  - **Speed:** slow (chorale) by default. It goes fast while any of these holds:
    - the mod wheel is above halfway (CC 1, hysteresis 0.45/0.55);
    - channel pressure is above 0.6;
    - a **hard chord** was played (three or more notes within 40 ms at velocity ≥ 110), until 2.5 s after the last one: the hill wind gets up.
  - **The hill wind:** the slow speed wanders ±8% over 20–40 s.
- **Chapel (0…1):** the pipe rank.
  - 0: none.
  - 0.5: a principal 8′ at −8 dB under the wheels.
  - 1: the pipes lead (0 dB) with the wheels 9 dB under them, and from 0.6 a 4′ principal joins.
  - The pipes don't go through the rotor: they stand still while the wheels turn.
- **Swell (0…1, `ISD_P_SWELL`):** how much your touch works the swell pedal.
  - A follower of your playing sets the expression: each note's velocity pushes it up (attack τ 0.35 s), and it sinks toward the floor (release τ 2.8 s). At Swell 1 it ranges from −14 dB (soft) to 0 dB (hard); at Swell 0 the swell stays open, like an organ with its pedal down.
  - A closed swell also adds a loudness-compensating low shelf (+4 dB at 150 Hz at the bottom), as an organ's expression does.
  - An expression pedal (CC 11) takes over from your touch as soon as it moves, until reset.
- **Drive (0…1):** the preamp, from clean (0) to a warm growl (1).
- **Space (0…1):** the hill.
  - A far echo off the valley's far side: one reflection at 420–640 ms (Space sets the distance), −16 dB, low-passed at 2.4 kHz, and a fainter one at 1.7 × the delay at −25 dB.
  - An open-air reverb: size 0.3–0.7, decay 1.5–3.5 s, damped.
- **Width (0…1):** the rotor's mic spread, and the pipe rank's pitch spread (±0.4).
- **The scanner:** a gentle chorus (depth 2, mix 0.5), always on; mood motion sets its depth.
- **Wheel and pedal:** the pitch wheel does nothing (an organ doesn't bend). The sustain pedal holds the keys until it lifts, as an organ holds a key.
- **Mood:**
  - energy adds Ping (+0.15) and Click (+0.1);
  - warmth adds Drive (+0.2) and a step of 16′;
  - brightness moves the Registration ±0.08;
  - space adds Space and the echo;
  - motion moves the rotor's slow speed ±15% and the scanner's depth.
- **Dials, in order:** Registration .35, Ping .00, Click .35, Rotor .65, Chapel .20, Swell .55, Drive .25, Space .55, Width .60.
- **Presets:**

  | id | Name | Registration | Ping | Click | Rotor | Chapel | Swell | Drive | Space | Width |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `hilltop-organ` | Hilltop Organ | .35 | .00 | .35 | .65 | .20 | .55 | .25 | .55 | .60 |
  | `small-hours` | Small Hours | .20 | .00 | .20 | .80 | .10 | .70 | .15 | .65 | .70 |
  | `valley-chapel` | Valley Chapel | .30 | .00 | .10 | .30 | .75 | .40 | .10 | .75 | .55 |
  | `lookout-ping` | Lookout Ping | .75 | .55 | .55 | .60 | .00 | .50 | .45 | .40 | .60 |

- **Test hooks:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_ROTOR = 217` (the rotor bypassed: the straight sound at full) and `ISD_P_DEBUG_WHEELS_ONLY = 218` (the tone bank alone: no click, leakage, percussion, pipes, scanner, drive, rotor, echo or reverb, and the swell open).
- **Targets (brief):**
  - **Tuning:** with WHEELS_ONLY at Registration 0, the 8′ within 0.5 cents of equal temperament from A2 to A6. Fine tune ±50 moves it exactly.
  - **Ranks:** a single 8′ at level 6 is 6 ± 0.3 dB below level 8.
  - **Tempered harmonics:** the 2⅔′ at 2^(19/12) × f0 and the 1⅗′ at 2^(28/12) × f0, each within 0.3 cents.
  - **Shared tones:** C4 with its 4′ and C5 with its 8′ make one spectral line, with no beating.
  - **Foldback:** nothing above 5.92 kHz from the top note's 1′.
  - **Click:** Click 1 against 0 raises the 2–10 kHz energy over the first 5 ms by ≥ 15 dB. At Click 0 the onset is within 3 dB of the steady state.
  - **Percussion:** from all keys up, the 2⅔′ band over 0–150 ms rises ≥ 10 dB above Ping 0. A key added while another is held raises it ≤ 1 dB. T60 is 1.0 ± 0.2 s. The 1′ is silent while Ping > 0.
  - **The scanner:** its modulation at 6.9 ± 0.2 Hz.
  - **The rotor:**
    - At slow, the horn band's level swings at 0.80 ± 0.08 Hz and the drum's at 0.70 ± 0.08 Hz; at fast, 6.8 ± 0.3 and 5.6 ± 0.3 Hz.
    - After the wheel goes up, the horn passes 90% of fast within 1.5 s and the drum within 6.5 s (the debug counts).
    - At fast, a 2 kHz partial shows a Doppler swing of ±20–40 cents.
  - **Swell:** at Swell 1, a phrase at velocity 30 against 110 is ≥ 9 dB quieter; at Swell 0, within 1 dB. CC 11 at 0 against 127: ≥ 12 dB.
  - **Chapel:** at Chapel 1 the second partial's onset leads the fundamental's by ≥ 10 ms, and the chiff lifts the third-harmonic band over 0–40 ms by ≥ 6 dB.
  - **Panic:** everything, including the rotor's lines, the echo and the reverb, is silent within 50 ms.
  - **CPU:** < 6% for ten held keys with the rotor fast, Chapel 1 and Ping on.

### 4.3 The piano core grows (`PianoStrings.hpp`)
- **`UnisonPlan`:** a note's strings and how each is tuned.
  - Strings per note: one below MIDI 34, two from 34 to 47, three from 48, as on an upright.
  - The first 8 partials get one component per string; partials 9–16 get two (the centre and an averaged side); higher partials get one. This fits the existing 64-component bank.
  - Side strings ring 2.6 times longer at a quarter of the level, as Cabin Felt's two-component approximation already does. The plan generalises it to real one-, two- and three-string unisons, so the prompt sound and aftersound of Weinreich's double decay emerge from the tuning.
- **`HammerFelt`:** the hammer.
  - Its cutoff follows the string, 360 Hz × (f0/261.63)^0.6, as in Cabin Felt, and opens with velocity (dynamic hardness: loud notes are brighter).
  - A **wear** term (0 fresh … 1 worn hard):
    - the cutoff × (0.75 + 1.65 × wear);
    - the contact time × (1 − 0.6 × wear);
    - a zing (a +5 dB × wear peak at 2.5–4 kHz);
    - above 0.6, a faint metallic tick on hard notes (1 ms, 3–7 kHz, −28 dB): the grooved hammer face.
- **`MechBurst`:** Cabin Felt's private `Burst` moves here unchanged, so both uprights share the key thump, the damper chuff and the pedal's thud. A new Cabin Felt fingerprint test locks its sound before the move, so the move changes nothing.
- **Cost:** as Cabin Felt: 64 components per voice in 8-lane banks.
- **Future use:** the piano core now plays three pianos: Felt Glass's grand, Cabin Felt's felted upright and the Last-Call Upright.

### 4.4 Last-Call Upright (after "Everybody")
The bar's old upright after last call: unisons drifting apart, hammers worn hard, the lid up or shut, an empty room, and the lo-fi warmth of the record.
- **Voices:** 32 (pool 40). **Range:** A0–C8.
- **Pedal:** the sympathetic bank (`SympBank`) rings while it's down, as in Cabin Felt. No bend, no pressure: pianos have neither.
- **Honky (0…1):** how far the unisons have drifted.
  - Side strings sit off by ±d cents, with d = 0.4 + 14 × Honky². That is 0.4 at 0 (a well-kept upright's gentle double decay), about 4 at 0.5, and about 14 at 1 (the classic honky-tonk sound).
  - Each note's d is multiplied by a seeded 0.6–1.4.
  - Two-string notes move one string, as tuners do. Three-string notes move both sides, unevenly (−d·k₁, 0, +d·k₂).
  - The centre strings drift ±(0.5 + 4 × Honky) cents from note to note: an old piano's uneven tuning.
  - The beats run about 0.5–1 Hz on A3 at the default and 3–5 Hz at Honky 1.
- **Wear (0…1):** the hammers, from freshly voiced and round (0) to worn hard, bright and a little metallic (1).
- **Lid (0…1):**
  - 0: the lid shut and the front panel on. A high shelf −9 dB above 2.2 kHz, a boxy +3 dB at 250 Hz, and the mechanics quieter.
  - 1: the lid up and the front off. Open and bright: +2 dB above 6 kHz, and the mechanics +4 dB.
- **Room (0…1):** the empty bar.
  - Early reflections off the floor, the bar and the walls at 2.4, 4.1, 6.8, 11.3 and 16.2 ms.
  - A reverb of size 0.12–0.37.
  - Two glasses left on the lid: high-Q resonators at seeded 1.0–1.3 kHz and 1.6–2.1 kHz that ring faintly (−40 dB) when a partial lands on them, only above Room 0.4.
- **Bass (0…1, default 0.45) and the split:** after Mac's own bass on "Everybody". Keys below the split (default B2; Advanced, A1–E3) also play a round, short electric-style bass an octave down: a sine plus a soft second harmonic, a plucked envelope (3 ms attack, decay 0.9 s, release 0.15 s) and a gentle low-pass that opens with velocity. 4 voices, mono-legato by default. Bass sets its level against the piano; 0 turns the split off and the low keys are piano alone.
- **Tape (0…1):** the record's lo-fi colour, through `TapeKit`.
  - Wow at 0.5–1.1 Hz up to ±9 cents, and flutter at 7–9 Hz up to ±1.5 cents.
  - Saturation, and a band narrowing from 40 Hz–16 kHz to 90 Hz–7 kHz.
  - A hiss up to −58 dB, heard only while the piano sounds and for 1 s after.
- **Space (0…1):** the reverb's wet level (0–35%).
- **Width (0…1):** the pitch spread, as in Cabin Felt.
- **Mechanics** (fixed levels, scaled by the Lid): the key thump, the damper chuff and the pedal's thud (`MechBurst`). A worn action also lets a loose key knock faintly on release (2 ms, 0.8–2 kHz, −36 dB × Wear).
- **Mood:**
  - energy adds Wear (+0.1);
  - warmth lowers the Lid (−0.1) and warms the Tape;
  - brightness tilts a high shelf ±3 dB;
  - space adds Space and Room;
  - motion deepens the Tape's wow.
- **Dials, in order:** Honky .35, Wear .45, Lid .40, Room .55, Tape .30, Space .35, Width .45.
- **Presets:**

  | id | Name | Honky | Wear | Lid | Room | Tape | Space | Width |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `last-call-upright` | Last-Call Upright | .35 | .45 | .40 | .55 | .30 | .35 | .45 |

- **Test hooks:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_IN_TUNE = 219` (every string exactly at pitch: no unison spread and no drift) and `ISD_P_DEBUG_STRINGS_ONLY = 220` (no mechanics, glasses, room, tape or reverb).
- **Targets (brief):**
  - **Tuning:** with IN_TUNE, within 2 cents from A0 to C8.
  - **Honky:** at Honky 1, A3's low partials show side lines at ±(12–17) cents, and the fundamental's envelope beats at 1.5–6 Hz with a depth ≥ 0.3 over 0.2–2 s. At Honky 0 the depth is ≤ 0.08.

## 5. Ambience

Nine sources are appended to the end of `ISDAmbienceSource`, bringing the count to 47:
- `ISD_AMB_CITY_HUM`, `ISD_AMB_FAR_TRAIN`, `ISD_AMB_DOG`, `ISD_AMB_GRASS`, `ISD_AMB_TRAM_MOTOR`, `ISD_AMB_TRAM_BELL`, `ISD_AMB_WHEEL_SQUEAL`, `ISD_AMB_TRAM_STOP`, `ISD_AMB_GUST_FRONT`
- with `AmbienceSource.cityHum, .farTrain, .dog, .grass, .tramMotor, .tramBell, .wheelSqueal, .tramStop, .gustFront`

- **City hum (outdoor):** the city from 30 km away, a continuous bed calibrated to −24 dBFS at level 1.
  - A low, broadband roar of traffic: brown and pink noise shaped to 30–400 Hz, breathing slowly (0.02–0.08 Hz).
  - Now and then a far motorbike or truck: a 10–20 s tonal swell at 80–300 Hz.
  - The world sets the level by the hour: it is quieter after midnight.
- **Far train (outdoor, in key):** a train crossing the plain far below, started by the world (`isd_ambience_trigger_train`), at −28 dBFS peak.
  - The pass: a 40–70 s swell of rail rumble (30–200 Hz), with the faint rhythm of wheels over joints.
  - The horn, once or twice: two reedy tones a fourth apart, sounding **the dominant and the tonic above it** of the key you are playing in (in 330–660 Hz), each 0.8–1.6 s.
  - It is far: low-passed above 1.5 kHz, with a long valley echo.
  - It follows `isd_ambience_set_key` and retunes only between passes (E major until a key is set).
- **Dog (outdoor):** a dog across the valley, at −30 dBFS peak.
  - Two to six barks: each a 0.15–0.3 s harmonic burst at 300–700 Hz with a noisy attack and formant colour.
  - Every 2–6 minutes ÷ density. Sometimes a second, farther dog answers 3–8 s later.
  - Far: low-passed above 2 kHz and reverberant.
- **Grass (outdoor):** wind in long, dry grass, calibrated to −24 dBFS at level 1.
  - A soft high hiss (pink noise band-passed to 2–9 kHz) that swells with each gust.
  - In strong wind, the brittle ticks of seed heads: sparse 1–3 ms clicks above 4 kHz.
  - The world sets the level by the wind.
- **Tram motor (indoor: you are aboard):** the tram's traction, following `isd_ambience_set_speed` (0…1 of 0–60 km/h), calibrated to −24 dBFS at level 1 at cruise.
  - A gear-mesh tone at 36 × the wheels' revolution rate (about 195 Hz at 40 km/h), with two harmonics.
  - An inverter whine that steps up through three soft notes as the tram pulls away, between 300 and 900 Hz.
  - The wheels' rumble on the rail (brown noise shaped to 40–300 Hz by speed), and a couple of clacks over points and crossings.
  - The brakes: as speed falls, an air hiss and a falling regenerative whine.
- **Tram bell (outdoor: it hangs under the tram, in key):** the driver's foot gong, started by the world (`isd_ambience_trigger_tram_bell`), at −22 dBFS peak.
  - A struck dome bell with inharmonic partials (1, 2.32, 3.93 and 5.6 × f0). Its strike note is **the tonic** of your key in 520–1040 Hz. It rings 0.9–1.4 s.
  - Once at a crossing, twice (0.22 s apart) on leaving a stop.
  - It retunes only between strikes.
- **Wheel squeal (outdoor):** on tight curves, started by the world with a strength and a duration (`isd_ambience_trigger_squeal`), at −26 dBFS peak.
  - A strong tonal squeal whose fundamental wanders between 1.8 and 4.5 kHz, with two harmonics, in 1–4 s swells.
- **Tram stop (indoor):** the stop's sounds, started by the world (`isd_ambience_trigger_tram_doors`).
  - **Doors opening:** a chime of two soft notes from the ceiling speaker, **the fifth falling to the tonic** of your key (in 660–1320 Hz, each a sine with a second partial at −14 dB, 0.6 s apart); the doors' slide and thud; then, 0.6 s later, a wordless announcement: 1.2–2 s of voice-like formant glides at a syllable rhythm of 4–5 Hz through a small speaker (350 Hz–3.4 kHz), at −34 dBFS. Never words.
  - **Doors closing:** three short beeps (the chime's tonic), the slide and a soft thud.
  - The chime retunes only between stops.
- **Gust front (outdoor):** the change arriving, calibrated to −24 dBFS at level 1. `WeatherAmbience` sets its level from the sky's change (§6.4).
  - A broadband roar (pink noise at 60 Hz–5 kHz) with turbulent swells at 0.5–3 Hz.
  - Trees thrashing: a mid band at 300 Hz–2 kHz with leaf rattle.
  - Grit, by the dust: sparse 0.5–2 ms clicks at 2–8 kHz.
- **New C API:**
  - `isd_ambience_trigger_train(ISDAmbience *, float distance)`: a far train passes now (0 near … 1 far). Silent unless the far train is up.
  - `isd_ambience_trigger_tram_bell(ISDAmbience *, int strikes)`: one or two strikes now.
  - `isd_ambience_trigger_squeal(ISDAmbience *, float strength, float seconds)`: a curve's squeal.
  - `isd_ambience_trigger_tram_doors(ISDAmbience *, int open)`: 1, the doors open (chime, slide, announcement); 0, they close.
  - `isd_ambience_debug_tonic_hz(const ISDAmbience *, ISDAmbienceSource)` (tests): the tonic an in-key source is ringing, or will ring next.
  - Each trigger is heard only while its source is up, so a one-shot never carries into the next world.
- **`AmbienceScene`** gains the one-shots `trainPass: Double?`, `tramBell: Int?`, `squeal: Squeal?` (strength, seconds) and `doors: Bool?` (true opening, false closing). The App passes them to the triggers, as it does `wave`, `thunder` and `bubbleBurst`. The tram reuses `speed`.
- **Levels** are calibrated as before: continuous beds near −24 dBFS RMS at level 1, events by their peaks.
- **`WeatherAmbience`:**
  - In the hot northerly before a change, the wind rises (≥ 0.5 × wind) and the grass with it.
  - In the **lull** just before the gust front, the wind drops to 0.15 for about 20 s.
  - The **gust front** rises from 0 to 1 as the arc nears overhead, peaks as it passes and decays over 30–60 s; grit follows the dust.
  - After the change the wind settles at 0.45, and if it rained, drips follow.

## 6. Living Sky additions

### 6.1 Weather values
`WeatherValues` gains `dust: Float = 0`: dust in the air ahead of a change (`mix` blends it).

`.coolChange` ("Cool change") is the one weather whose values move through time: they follow the change's phases (§6.4).

| Phase (share of the change's minutes) | cloud | rain | mist | wind | gust | dust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hot northerly (0–0.35) | 0.12 | 0 | 0.30 | 0.60 | 0.50 | 0.30 |
| The front comes in (0.35–0.55) | 0.12 → 0.45 | 0 | 0.40 | 0.60 → 0.15 (the lull) | 0.20 | 0.30 → 0.85 (the dust wall) |

- Appended at the end of `allCases`, after `.seaSmoke`. Beauty weight 0.6.

### 6.2 New sky state
`SkyState` gains:
- **`planets: [PlanetNow]`:** five entries, always in the order Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn. Each holds:
  - `direction: SkyDirection` as drawn (after the moon lens, §6.3) and `trueDirection`;
  - `magnitude: Float` and `visibility: Float` (0…1, after twilight, cloud and the Advanced scale);
  - `elongation: Double` (degrees from the Sun; positive east, in the evening sky);
  - `phaseAngle: Double`, `illuminated: Float` and `distanceAU: Double`;
  - `ringTilt: Double?` (Saturn's B, degrees) and `moons: [Double]?` (Jupiter's four moons' X, in Jupiter radii; refreshed once a second).
- **`dust: Float`:** the weather's dust, after the world's rule.
- **`coolChange: CoolChangeNow?`:** while `.coolChange` is in the sky:
  - `phase` (hot northerly, front, change, after);
  - `frontAltitude: Double?` (degrees: the arc's leading edge above the horizon it comes from; nil while it is still below the horizon or long gone);
  - `approachAzimuth: Double` (degrees);

  - `arrived: Double?` (the sky's local hour when the gust front came through).
- **`windTurn: Float`:** which way the wind blows on screen: +1 as always, gliding to −1 over 20 s when a change comes through.

- **Positions (`Planets`, Core):**
  1. JPL's algorithm: the elements at T (centuries from J2000; universal time is close enough to TDB here); the mean anomaly wrapped to ±180°; Kepler's equation iterated to 10⁻⁶°; then the heliocentric position in the J2000 ecliptic.
  2. Geocentric: the planet minus the Earth–Moon barycentre (the Moon's offset is too small to matter here).
  3. One light-time correction: the planet as it was Δ × 0.0057755 days ago.
  4. Precessed to the date and turned to equatorial coordinates, in the one frame the whole sky now shares (§7.1).
  5. Then hour angle and altitude like the Sun's and the Moon's, with refraction.
- **Phase and elongation:** with r (Sun–planet), Δ (Earth–planet) and R (Sun–Earth), the phase angle is cos α = (r² + Δ² − R²)/(2rΔ) and the lit fraction k = (1 + cos α)/2. The elongation is cos ψ = (R² + Δ² − r²)/(2RΔ), signed east or west by the ecliptic longitudes.
- **Magnitudes:** Mallama and Hilton (2018), each with 5 log₁₀(rd):
  - Mercury, their equation 2;
  - Venus, equations 3 and 4 (either side of α = 163.7°);
  - Mars, equation 6 without its rotation and season terms (±0.1);
  - Jupiter, equation 8;
  - Saturn, equation 10 with its rings.
- **Saturn's rings (`SaturnRings`):** B and B′, the Saturnicentric latitudes of the Earth and the Sun, from the ring plane's inclination and node (Meeus, ch. 45). β = √(B·B′) when they share a sign, else 0 (we see the unlit face). The rings set Saturn's brightness: that is how their tilt shows to the eye.
- **Jupiter's moons (`JupiterMoons`):** Meeus' lower-accuracy method (ch. 44), for the panel's strip only.
- **Quick and frozen modes:**
  - In real time, every planet sits exactly where it is among the stars.
  - Quick and frozen modes show the March-equinox sun at the chosen hour (as the stars and the virtual moon already do), while their calendar keeps the real date. So each planet is computed for the calendar's date and drawn at its real angle from the Sun, measured from the *shown* sun, as the virtual moon is drawn by its phase.
  - So Venus is still the evening star this month, an opposition planet still rises at sunset, and the virtual moon still sweeps past the planets. In these modes the planets are not among their true background stars.
  - Quick mode walks the planets a calendar day per cycle, as it walks the meteor calendar.
- **Visibility:** planets come out before the stars.
  - The faintest magnitude the sky shows falls with the sun:

    | Sun | 0° | −1° | −2.5° | −4° | −6° | −9° | −12° | −16° | −18° |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | Limit | −4.6 | −3.6 | −2.4 | −1.0 | +1.0 | +3.0 | +4.5 | +6.0 | +6.5 |

  - visibility = smoothstep(m − 0.4, m + 1.2, limit − 1.2 × moonGlow − 2.5 × mist) × (1 − 0.9 × cloud) × the Advanced "Planets" scale.
  - moonGlow is as the meteors' (the Moon's lit fraction × the sine of its altitude).
  - With the sun above 0° the planets hide.
  - So Venus shines in the sunset glow, then Jupiter, then the first stars.
- **The moon lens:**
  - The Moon is drawn large (1.25° × the moon size, against a true 0.26°), so a close conjunction would put the planet inside the drawn disc.
  - Within 6° of the Moon, a planet's drawn separation maps [R_true, 6°] linearly onto [R_drawn, 6°]: s′ = R_drawn + (s − R_true)(6° − R_drawn)/(6° − R_true), along the great circle from the Moon's centre. So a planet a quarter of a degree from the real limb is drawn about a quarter of a degree from the drawn limb.
  - A planet behind the real Moon (s < R_true) is hidden, so occultations happen at their real times.
- **The look** (`skyRadiance`, after the stars, before the aurora, the Moon and the clouds, so clouds hide them):
  - **The core:** a Gaussian of radius pixelAngle × (1.0 + 0.45 × max(0, −m)), so Venus is about 3 px across and Saturn 1 px. Its peak is min(2.2, b^0.55), where b = 10^(−0.4(m + 1.46)) puts Sirius at 1.
  - **The glow:** below magnitude −1, a soft halo of 0.035 × b^0.5 × e^(−θ/0.35°).
  - **Steady light:** no twinkle above 10° altitude; below it, a gentle 0.92–1.0 shimmer at 3–6 Hz.
  - **Colour:** each planet's B−V through the star texture's curve (Mercury 0.97, Venus 0.81, Mars 1.43, Jupiter 0.87, Saturn 1.09), with Mars's saturation lifted 1.3× because the eye sees it orange.
  - **Cost:** five dot products per sky pixel; a slot whose cone misses the pixel exits at once.
  - Because it runs in `skyRadiance`, the planets appear in every world's sky and every reflection: Venus on the bay, Jupiter in the lake.
- **What's tonight:** the Living Sky panel shows one or two lines, for example:
  - "Planets tonight: Venus is the morning star, rising at 04:12; Mars and Jupiter rise together after midnight, 1.2° apart; Saturn sets at 02:40."
  - Up to three planets, brightest first. A planet counts if it is above 8° at some point while the sun is below −8°.
  - Mercury and Venus are "the evening star" or "the morning star". Others are "up all night" (within 30 days of opposition), "rises at …", "sets at …" or "high in the north" or "south".
  - "Near the Moon" when within 5° tonight; "close to …" when two planets are within 3°.
- **Through binoculars:** a small strip under that line draws up to three glyphs for the planets up tonight: Mercury's or Venus's phase (lit toward the Sun), Jupiter with its four moons in a line, and Saturn with its rings opened by B (an ellipse whose minor axis is sin|B| of its major).

### 6.4 The Cool Change (WeatherKit)
- **The event (`CoolChange`, Core):** a change is a story that plays out over the change's minutes (M = 6 of viewing by default; Advanced 2–20). It runs on its own seeded stream:
  1. **Hot northerly (0–0.35 M):** a hot, dry, gusty wind; a warm dust haze; a few high clouds.
  2. **The front comes in (0.35–0.55 M):** the roll cloud's dark arc rises from the horizon it comes from. Ahead of it a dust wall lifts, and the wind falls into a lull.
  3. **The change (0.55–0.70 M):** the gust front arrives as the arc passes overhead. The wind turns (`windTurn` +1 → −1 over 20 s), gusts peak, the event's rain falls if it has any, and the dust blows through and clears.
  4. **After the change (0.70 M on):** cool, clean, breezy air; broken cloud clearing; sharper stars.
- **The front's geometry:**
  - **Where it comes from:** the pole-ward west: 225° in the southern hemisphere and 315° in the northern, ± a seeded 15°.
  - **How high it stands:** as a line of cloud about 1.2 km up drawing in from 25 km to overhead: frontAltitude = atan(1.2 km / x), with x shrinking from 25 km to 0 over the front phase. After it passes, the arc runs on to the far horizon over 0.15 M.
- **When it plays:**
  - Picked, it plays once and then holds "after the change"; picking it again replays it.
  - In "Random, beautiful", a cool-change spell starts with the hot northerly and holds the after-phase for the rest of the spell.
  - Leaving and re-entering the weather starts a new event.
- **On screen** (`skyRadiance`, in the cloud pass, while a change is active):
  - **The arc** uses the aurora band's geometry: a great circle through the points 90° either side of the approach azimuth, raised to frontAltitude over it.
    - Its leading edge is smooth and laminar, scalloped by one octave of noise along it.
    - Its underside is turbulent (two octaves) and lit from below: by a low sun's gold, or at night by the world's ground glow (`SkyLayer.groundGlow`, zero by default: the city's orange for the hill).
    - Above and behind it, the cloud deck thickens with the weather's cloud cover.
    - Never brighter than the cloud's own light.
  - **The dust:** the horizon band mixes toward a dusty amber (0.62, 0.45, 0.30) × the sky's light, by dust × 0.7, below 15°. A dust wall stands 2–6° high just under the approaching arc. The sun's disc reddens and dims in dust.
  - **Cost:** one band test per sky pixel during a change only; pixels far from the arc exit early.
- **The dust layer and the wind's turn** (§7.3): dust motes blow across every outdoor world, and the shared weather layer's rain, streamers and motes turn with `windTurn`.
- **The rules** (§7.4): in the cold worlds the change brings snow and no dust; at the reef there is no dust.
- **Sound:** `WeatherAmbience` (§5): the hot wind, the lull, the gust front and the grit.
- **What's happening:** the Living Sky panel shows one line, for example:
  - "Cool change: a hot northerly — the change is a few minutes away"
  - "Cool change: here it comes"
  - "Cool change: through at 18:40, about 13° cooler"

  It speaks of the drop in relative terms only. There is no temperature in the sky yet.

### 6.5 Settings and UI
- **Settings:** `SkySettings` gains `planetScale: Double = 1` (0…1.5) and `changeMinutes: Double = 6` (2…20). Missing keys decode to the defaults, and values are clamped.
- **Names:** `SkyControls.weatherName` maps `.coolChange` to "Cool change". The panel gains the "Planets tonight" line with its strip, and the "Cool change" line.
- **Advanced:** Sky extras gains "Planets" (0…1.5: "how bright the planets look; 0 hides them") and "Cool change" (2…20: "how many minutes a cool change takes to come through").
- **Looks:** Gentle 0.8 and 8 min; Dramatic 1.3 and 4 min; Dreamy 1.15 and 6 min. Reset restores 1 and 6.

## 7. Shared pieces

### 7.1 One frame for the whole sky (`Precession`, Core)
- **Why:** the Sun (NOAA) and the Moon (Meeus ch. 47) are computed for the date, while the star catalogue and meteor radiants are J2000. In 2026 that puts them 0.37° apart, about a third of the way to a close conjunction.
- **What:** `Precession` gives the rotation from J2000 to the date (Meeus ch. 21, ζ, z and θ). It is applied:
  - in `SkyLayer.equatorialBasis`, so the real stars, the Milky Way and the constellation lines are drawn for the date;
  - in `MeteorSky.direction`, so the radiants are too;
  - to the planets.
- The sky's calendar date sets it, so quick mode precesses by its calendar too.
- **Cost:** one 3 × 3 matrix per frame. Stars move by at most 0.4°; tests that place stars use the shared basis.

### 7.2 `SkyU` growth
- After `flash`, add `float4 planets[5]`: direction xyz and brightness (0 means hidden), in the order Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn. Their colours are constants in the shader.
- Add one `float4 change` row: approach azimuth (radians), frontAltitude (degrees; −90 for none), the arc's strength, and dust.
- **Real planets add 5 rows; the Cool Change adds 1.** `SkyU` grows from 44 to 50 rows (800 bytes). It is still one `setFragmentBytes` call.
- `SkyLayer` gains `groundGlow: SIMD3<Float>` (zero by default), which a world sets to light the arc's underside, as `ripples` and `auroraTint` are set.

### 7.3 The dust layer and the wind's turn
- **The motes:** `WeatherLayer` gains an eighth instance set, **dust** (kind 7): up to 80 (Smooth) / 160 / 260 warm specks blowing across the view with the wind, a few large soft ones near the camera. Their count follows `dust`.
- **The haze:** a thin warm haze over the lower view, with alpha 0.3 × dust.
- **The turn:**
  - The weather layer's rain tilt, streamers, motes and steam lean all take the screen wind's sign from `windTurn`.
  - `SkyLayer.screenWind` follows it too.
  - The two new worlds turn their grass, trees and streets with it.
  - Older worlds' own swaying (palms, reeds, chimney smoke) keeps blowing right this run.

### 7.4 `WorldWeather` and the change
- **`cold`** (the cabin, the fjord, the tea house): `.coolChange` stays itself, because a cold front is at home there. The rule's rain becomes snow (a snow squall behind the front) and dust is 0.
- **`warm`** (the reef): dust is 0, because the air comes off the sea. The arc and the gusts stay.
- **Where it shows:**
  - **The open-air worlds** show it whole: the lake, the beach, the road, the fjord, the bay, the garden, the reef above the water and the two new worlds.
  - **The window worlds** (the cabin, the bedroom, the tea house's screens) show it through their windows: the arc in the sky, the gust in the trees, the dust haze, rain on the glass.
  - **The submarine** feels it only at shallow depth: darker light and a choppy surface in Snell's window.
  - The lounge has no weather.

### 7.5 The tram window (Window Kit)
- **The frame:** `WindowSpec.Material.tramFrame`: one large pane with rounded corners (radius 4% of its height), a black rubber gasket and a painted steel frame in the interior's cream-grey.
- **The glass:**
  - lightly tinted (a 6% grey-green);
  - the interior's reflection (the ceiling's light strip, the poles, the seat tops), strongest where the outside is dark;
  - drops that run backward with the tram: `WeatherLayer.glassFlow` (window units per second) slants the glass's drops back and down at (−0.9 × speed, −0.35), and straight down at a stop;
  - condensation: after a cool change, cool air outside fogs the warm glass's edges.

### 7.6 Ambience in key grows
The far train's horn (the dominant and the tonic), the tram's bell (the tonic) and the stop chime (the fifth falling to the tonic) follow `AmbienceScene.key` through `isd_ambience_set_key`, as the temple bell and the sonar do. Each retunes only between its events: a horn never bends mid-blast, and a chime never changes mid-pair.

### 7.7 Controllers reach the engines
- `Engine` gains `virtual void controller(int number, float value)` (0…1), a no-op by default.
- `handleMidi` forwards every controller except 64 (the sustain pedal), 120, 121 and 123 to it. Reset (121) also sends the mod wheel 0 and the expression 1.
- Hilltop Organ uses CC 1 (the rotor's speed) and CC 11 (the swell). Every other engine ignores them, so nothing changes for them.
- `MIDIInput` already passes controllers to the Audio Unit as raw MIDI 1.0 bytes, and `PerformanceEvent.control` already carries them for the visuals.

## 8. Set 13: Sleeping City from a Hill

### 8.1 World
You stand at a hilltop lookout at night, high above a plain, looking west over a sleeping city about 30 km away. The city is original and generic: a coastal city spread over a plain, Melbourne-like in size and lighting, never a copy of a real skyline.
- **The frame:**
  - The horizon sits a little below the middle. The plain of lights spreads from just under it to the hill's dark foreground.
  - The camera faces west (270°), so the sun sets behind the city, the evening star hangs over it, and a cool change comes in from the left.
- **The city** (Core: `CityLights`, seeded and built once):
  - **The streets:** a plain 40 km deep and 60 km wide, about 450 m below the hill. Jittered street grids (100–150 m) are drawn in perspective, so far rows compress toward the horizon.

    - Residential grids in warm-white LED (3000 K: 1.0, 0.82, 0.58).

    - the arc comes in across the bay from the south-west, its underside lit orange by the city (`groundGlow`);

## 13. Reliability
- **The organ:**
  - Panic (`allSoundOff`) stops every key at once, empties the rotor's lines, the echo and the reverb within 50 ms, and sets the rotor to slow.
  - Panic and an instrument change reset the swell follower, the mod wheel and the expression.
  - A stuck controller can't hold the rotor fast: reset (CC 121) clears it.
- **The upright:** its hiss never plays alone; panic clears its room and tape.
- **The planets:**
  - They are valid from 1800 to 2050. Outside that range they keep working with growing error; the app's calendars stay well inside it.
  - A missing star texture never hides them, because they don't use it.
- **The Cool Change:** the event is bounded. After the change it holds; each spell ends normally; a re-entered weather starts a new event.
- **The tram:**
  - It never sticks at a stop (20 s at most).
  - The run loops forever, and ids wrap.
  - Its one-shots never carry into the next world.
- **The far train:** only one runs at a time.
- **Ambience in key:** the horn, the bell and the chime never retune mid-event.
