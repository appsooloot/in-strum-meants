## 1. Purpose

Every run delivers at least **one of everything** per set: a world, its visual, an original instrument, a new sky feature, a new weather, a new ambience sound. The sky and weather features join the Living Sky, so every outdoor world gets them.

### Success looks like
1. **Polar Choir** blends when Michael plays along with "Wings", and **Plankton Keys** blends with "Happiness Is a Butterfly". Each is as good as, or better than, the sound that inspired it.
2. Both worlds look stunning and stay smooth on **Smooth** on the Intel MacBook Pro.
3. The aurora, the real stars with constellations, humid nights with fireflies, and diamond dust with light pillars appear in every world with a sky.
4. One tap on a set loads its world, visual, instrument preset, ambience and mood. Each part can still be swapped.
5. Each set credits its song with an original living card.
6. Demo clips of both instruments and both ambiences are attached to the release.

### Standing rules (unchanged)
- Everything is a set plus ambience, tied by mood. Playing along should blend, never clash.
- Sounds are original synthesis. Nothing is sampled, and no protected library is touched.
- Credits are text and a Spotify link only: no artwork, lyrics, logos or photos; no imitation of music videos.
- Card art is original, from the song's feeling, with tempo, key and track number as easter eggs.
- Every world runs on Smooth on the Intel Mac: ≤ 1 MP, no per-pixel ray marching, ≤ 3 noise octaves, no buffers per frame.
- Flares, glints and glow swells go through `FlashLimiter`.
- Settings and sets saved by older builds still load.

### Research that shaped the design
- **"Wings"** (Mac Miller): *Swimming* (2018), **track 6** (4:10), produced by **Alexander Spit** (Wikipedia). **A♭ major, 119 BPM** (Tunebat, GetSongBPM; a half-time feel of about 60). Airy, slowly swelling synth pads under a floating vocal; the feel is lift and wide space.
- **"Happiness Is a Butterfly"** (Lana Del Rey): *Norman Fucking Rockwell!* (2019), **track 13** (4:32), produced by **Lana Del Rey and Jack Antonoff**, with Rick Nowels (Wikipedia). **D major, about 68 BPM** (GetSongBPM, Chordify; Musicnotes 64, Hooktheory 69). A soft, close piano with bell-like electric-piano colours and a slow tremolo haze; wistful and dreamy.
- **Aurorae**: the oval sits near 65–72° geomagnetic latitude; from Melbourne (38° S) the aurora australis shows low on the southern horizon in strong storms (Kp 6+), from Hobart more often, from Tromsø or Fairbanks overhead most clear nights. Colours: green (557.7 nm) at 100–150 km, red tops (630 nm) above, a blue-purple lower fringe in strong events. Curtains are thin sheets aligned east–west with vertical rays; they ripple along their length.
- **Real stars**: the HYG database v4.1 (astronexus, CC BY-SA 4.0; positions and magnitudes are facts) gives 925 stars to magnitude 4.5. The sky already turns stars by sidereal time and latitude, so a real catalogue drops into the same basis. Crux (the Southern Cross) and the Pointers are the test case for Melbourne.
- **Diamond dust and light pillars**: in still, very cold air, plate crystals drift and glitter; lights (the sun, the moon, village lamps) throw vertical pillars above (and below) themselves.
- **Fireflies**: humid, still nights near water; swarms blink in loose synchrony near the ground and the shoreline.

## 4. The instruments

### 4.1 Polar Choir (after "Wings")

A vowel pad that breathes: airy voices that swell while held, with a bowed-glass layer on top.

- **Voices (16 polyphony, pool 20, 32-sample blocks):** two detuned band-limited saws (the Orbit Pad BLEP) through three parallel formant band-passes (RBJ, Q 6–10). Vowel formant sets (F1, F2, F3 Hz): /u/ 325, 700, 2530; /o/ 450, 800, 2830; /a/ 700, 1220, 2600; /e/ 530, 1850, 2500; /i/ 270, 2290, 3010. The **Vowel** dial morphs along u → o → a → e → i (0…1), and a slow per-voice vowel drift (±0.06, 0.05–0.12 Hz) keeps the choir alive. Formants key-track 20% so basses stay dark and sopranos open.
- **Breath:** white noise through the same formants, mixed by the **Breath** dial, with a slow 0.2–0.4 Hz amplitude wander; adds the "air" that reads as voices.
- **Glass:** a bowed-glass partial layer (sines at 1, 2, 3, 4.2 × f0, with a 0.6–1.5 s onset and gentle unison beating) by the **Glass** dial.
- **Swell:** attack 0.3–3.0 s by the dial, and while a note is held its level and formant Q keep rising over 4 s (a bow pressing in), easing back over 2 s after release. Release 1.5–5 s.
- **Shimmer:** the Space reverb's shimmer (octave-up tail) by the dial.
- **Space** (reverb size 0.25–0.9) and **Width** (stereo spread of the voices and a two-tap chorus).
- **Mood:** energy shortens the swell and release; brightness tilts a high shelf ±4 dB at 4 kHz; warmth a low shelf ±3 dB at 200 Hz; space adds to Space; motion widens the vowel drift.
- **Dials, in order:** Vowel .55, Breath .35, Glass .30, Swell .55, Shimmer .25, Space .60, Width .60.
- **Presets:** `polar-choir` "Polar Choir" (.55/.35/.30/.55/.25/.60/.60), `far-voices` "Far Voices" (.70/.55/.10/.75/.40/.80/.75), `bowed-glass` "Bowed Glass" (.40/.10/.85/.45/.30/.55/.50), `hush` "Hush" (.30/.50/.20/.35/.10/.45/.40).
- **Test hook:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_VOICES_ONLY = 206` (no breath, no glass, no chorus; one saw, no drift).
- **Targets (brief):** tuning within 2 cents; formant peaks within 6% of the table at Vowel 0, 0.5 and 1 on A3; breath raises the 2–6 kHz noise floor ≥ 6 dB between Breath 0 and 1; the held level rises ≥ 3 dB from 0.5 s to 4.5 s at Swell 1; glass adds a 4.2·f0 partial within 2%; CPU < 6% for a 12-note chord.

### 4.2 Plankton Keys (after "Happiness Is a Butterfly")

A modelled electric piano whose notes scatter glowing sparks.

- **Tines (24 voices, pool 30):** each note is a tine (sine fundamental with a slow 2nd and a decaying 3rd partial, inharmonicity 1.002–1.008 up the keyboard) plus a tone-bar partial a little under the fundamental's level with a 2–4× longer decay. The pickup is modelled as an asymmetric soft saturator whose drive rises with velocity: soft notes are pure, hard notes **bark** (the **Bark** dial scales it). A hammer thump (40–120 Hz, 10 ms) and a release click (2–5 kHz, 4 ms) by velocity. Decay to −40 dB: 4–9 s in the middle, shorter up top.
- **Tine** dial: the balance of tine to tone bar (0 all bar, warm; 1 all tine, bell-like).
- **Sparkle:** each note throws 2–5 sparks 15–90 ms later: short glassy sines at 5×, 7× and 9× f0 (±25 cents), 60–250 ms decays, spread across the stereo field. The **Sparkle** dial sets how many and how loud (0 none).
- **Tremolo:** a stereo pan tremolo 2–7 Hz (4.6 Hz at 0.5), depth by the dial, the classic suitcase sway.
- **Phase:** a four-stage phaser (0.1–0.3 Hz), depth by the dial.
- **Space** (reverb 0.2–0.8) and **Width**.
- **Mood:** energy adds bark drive and sparks; warmth tilts the tine/bar balance; brightness a high shelf ±4 dB; space adds to Space; motion speeds the tremolo ±30%.
- **Dials, in order:** Tine .55, Bark .40, Sparkle .50, Tremolo .35, Phase .20, Space .50, Width .55.
- **Presets:** `plankton-keys` "Plankton Keys" (.55/.40/.50/.35/.20/.50/.55), `glow-wake` "Glow Wake" (.65/.30/.85/.50/.35/.65/.70), `tide-tines` "Tide Tines" (.85/.60/.25/.20/.10/.40/.50), `midnight-bay` "Midnight Bay" (.35/.20/.40/.45/.30/.70/.60).
- **Test hooks:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_SPARKLE = 207`, `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_TREMOLO = 208`.
- **Targets (brief):** tuning within 2 cents on the fundamental; velocity 40 vs 120 on A3: the 2–6 kHz energy over 0–120 ms rises ≥ 8 dB (bark); Sparkle 1 vs 0: at least 2 transients in 15–120 ms after the note in the 3–12 kHz band; tremolo at 0.5: the left/right envelope autocorrelation peaks at 1/4.6 s ± 10%; decay to −40 dB on A3 4–9 s; CPU < 6% for a 12-note chord with sparks.

### 4.3 Sound briefs and the play-along bar
Each instrument gets a brief in `docs/superpowers/briefs/` (facts with sources, measurable targets, what we do better, play-along notes, preset starting values). The play-along test: "Wings" in **A♭ major** at its half-time pocket (about 60) on Polar Choir; "Happiness Is a Butterfly" in **D major** at 68 on Plankton Keys.

## 5. Ambience

Four sources appended to the end of `ISDAmbienceSource` (`ISD_AMB_ICE_GROAN`, `ISD_AMB_WATERFALL`, `ISD_AMB_LAPPING`, `ISD_AMB_BOAT`; `ISD_AMB_COUNT` becomes 28), with `AmbienceSource.iceGroan, .waterfall, .lapping, .boat`.

- **Ice groan:** deep groans (a 40–120 Hz resonant sweep, 1–4 s, every 20–90 s / density), sharp cracks (a 1–3 ms broadband click with a 300 ms 1–2 kHz ring, every 15–60 s), and a faint continuous creak bed at −40 dBFS.
- **Waterfall:** a far white roar (pink noise through a 200 Hz–3 kHz band, slow 0.1 Hz flicker) at −26 dBFS at level 1, with a low 60–120 Hz rumble under it.
- **Lapping:** small waves against a hull and rocks: a soft slosh (band-passed noise 300–2000 Hz, 0.3–0.8 s swells every 2–6 s) with occasional hollow knocks (a 180 Hz damped resonance, every 8–30 s).
- **Boat:** a distant outboard that passes every 150–400 s / density: a 12–20 s swell of a low pulsing engine (60–90 Hz saw-ish buzz at 25 pulses/s through a low-pass), panned across, with a soft wash after it.
- **WeatherAmbience** grows: under a humid night (`fireflies > 0.3`) crickets rise to at least 0.6 × fireflies; in diamond dust (`diamondDust > 0.3`) the wind is capped at 0.2 (diamond dust itself is silent: still, cold air).
- Levels are calibrated so continuous beds sit near −24 dBFS RMS at level 1 (`kGain`).

### 6.2 New sky state
`SkyState` gains:
- `aurora: Float` 0…1 — how bright the aurora is tonight (0 by day).
- `auroraAltitude: Float` degrees — where the band's base sits above the pole-ward horizon (from the city's latitude and the activity).
- `auroraStorm: Float` 0…1 — the substorm phase (rays and movement).
- `fireflies: Float` 0…1 — the weather's fireflies × darkness.
- `diamondDust: Float` 0…1 and `pillar: Float` 0…1 — glittering crystals and light pillars (the weather's values, pillars only with the sun or moon low or at night).
- `constellationLines: Float` 0…1 — the Advanced setting.
- `hemisphere: Float` — +1 north, −1 south (from the latitude).

### 6.3 The aurora
- **`AuroraMachine`** (Core, seeded): a slow activity index **Kp** 0…9 wandering with a 6–18 h correlation time (mean 2.5), and **substorms** that arrive every 20–60 min while Kp ≥ 3, lasting 5–15 min (0–1 envelope: 1 min onset, a 3–8 min expansion with rays, a slow recovery).
- **Visibility by latitude:** the oval's equatorward edge sits at about 67° − 3.2·Kp geomagnetic latitude (≈ geographic here). With |lat| the city's latitude and `edge` that edge: `band altitude = 90 − (edge − |lat|) × 2.2` degrees, clamped 0…90 when `edge > |lat|` (the band is toward the pole); below 2° it's not visible. Brightness = `smoothstep(0, 2, Kp − (edge − |lat|)/8)` × night (sun < −10°) × (1 − 0.9·cloud) × (1 − 0.6·mist).
- **Aurora mode** (per world, like Hold the light): `AuroraMode` `.follow` "Follow the sky" (the machine), `.always` "Always" (Kp held at 6 with a substorm every 8–12 min; the band at 25–60° for any latitude), `.never` "Never". The world parameter is `aurora.mode`; `SceneDirector` passes it to `sky.step(now:dt:hold:aurora:)`. The Advanced setting `auroraScale` (0…2, default 1) multiplies the follow-mode brightness.
- **On screen** (`SkyShaders.skyRadiance`, before clouds): the aurora is drawn pole-ward (azimuth 180° in the south, 0° in the north, with a ±20° slow wander). Two curtains: each is a thin sheet whose base follows `auroraAltitude` (+ a 1-octave noise wobble along azimuth), rising with an exponential vertical profile (scale 8° + 6°·storm) modulated by vertical rays (`fbm` along azimuth at 3 octaves, time-shifted, sharpened in a substorm). Colour: green (0.25, 1.0, 0.45) at the base, red (0.95, 0.25, 0.4) × storm toward the top, a purple fringe (0.6, 0.35, 1.0) × storm² at the base. Brightness scaled so a full aurora reads about 0.35 of the daytime sky luminance (it never flashes). Ripples (from Curtain Lights, §8.2) are eight `float4` in `SkyU`: (azimuth position 0…1 along the band, age s, strength, width) — each adds a travelling bulge and a brightness pulse along the curtain. Reflections get it for free (`skyRadiance`).
- **Smooth:** the curtain noise uses 2 octaves on Smooth, 3 above.

### 6.4 Real stars and constellations
- **`StarCatalogue`** (Core, generated data): the 925 HYG stars with magnitude ≤ 4.5: `ra` (degrees), `dec` (degrees), `mag`, `bv` colour index, `hip`, `con`, plus `proper`/`bayer` names for the brightest (for tests and a future "what's that star" feature). Attribution in the file header (HYG v4.1, CC BY-SA 4.0; Anthropic Claude generated the Swift literal from the CSV).
- **`ConstellationLines`** (Core, data): stick figures as lists of HIP id pairs for 26 figures: Crux, Centaurus (the Pointers), Carina (the False Cross with Vela), Scorpius, Sagittarius (the Teapot), Orion, Canis Major, Canis Minor, Taurus, Gemini, Auriga, Perseus, Cassiopeia, Cepheus, Ursa Major (the Plough), Ursa Minor, Draco (head), Boötes, Corona Borealis, Leo, Virgo (Spica line), Lyra, Cygnus, Aquila, Pegasus (the Square) with Andromeda, Grus, Triangulum Australe, Pavo. Each figure also has a label position (its brightest star) for a future label feature.
- **`StarTexture`** (Core, CPU, deterministic): an equirectangular 2048 × 1024 RGBA8 image: RGB is the summed star light (a Gaussian footprint whose radius and amplitude follow magnitude: radius 1.0 + 0.9·max(0, 2.5 − mag) pixels, amplitude ∝ 10^(−0.4·mag), colour from B−V: −0.3 → (0.72, 0.8, 1.0), 0.6 → (1, 0.98, 0.9), 1.6 → (1, 0.78, 0.55)); alpha is the constellation lines (1-pixel anti-aliased segments drawn in the texture's RA/Dec space, shortened 1.2° from each star). Built once on a background queue (< 300 ms optimised) and uploaded by `SkyLayer`.
- **On screen:** `skyRadiance` samples the texture at (RA, Dec) of the equatorial direction `v` (bilinear), multiplies by `starVis`, a per-star hashed twinkle (hash of the texel cell, 0.8–1.0 at 1–5 Hz), and a magnitude-scaled gain; it **replaces the brightest 30% of the procedural star field** (the procedural stars stay as the faint background, scaled to 0.45 so the real stars lead). Lines: `col += (0.55, 0.65, 0.9) × alpha × 0.06 × constellationLines × starVis`. Both come before the clouds and the moon, so clouds hide them. The Milky Way is unchanged.
- **Every world that calls `skyRadiance`** (the dome, the lake, the ocean, the beach, the road, the bay, the fjord) binds the star texture at fragment texture index 0; `SkyLayer.starTexture` is shared.
- **Advanced:** `constellationLines` 0…1 (default 0.35, "Constellation lines"); `starScale` already exists.

### 6.5 Fireflies (humid nights)

Count scales with `fireflies`. A world can add its own (the lake's chord fireflies stay).

### 6.6 Diamond dust and light pillars
- `LivingSky.step`: `s.diamondDust = values.pillar` (the dust) and `s.pillar = values.pillar × (sun below 12° or night)`.
- `WeatherLayer` gains a sixth instance set, **crystals** (kind 5): up to 150 (Smooth) / 400 / 800 tiny points over the whole view, falling very slowly (0.01–0.03 NDC/s) with sideways drift by wind, each twinkling (hashed, 2–6 Hz) with a cold white-blue tint and a 1–3 px size; count scales with `diamondDust`. Not the snow set: no flakes.
- **Light pillars** (`skyRadiance`): for the sun (when −2°…12°) and the moon (when up and lit > 0.3): a vertical column above and below the light, width ≈ the light's apparent radius × 1.5, intensity `exp(−|Δalt| / 6°)` × 0.35 × pillar × the light's colour (sun: warm; moon: pale), masked by the moon disc. World lights (the fjord's village, the bay's lantern) draw their own pillars (§8.1, §9.1) with the same profile.
- Halos are already on (`halo 1`), so diamond dust days show the 22° and 46° rings and sundogs.

## 8. Set 7: Aurora Fjord

### 8.1 World
From a jetty at the head of a fjord, looking down it toward the pole (south from Melbourne; the camera faces the aurora's azimuth, gliding over 4 s as it wanders). Steep snow-streaked walls rise on both sides and frame a V of sky; the far end opens to the sea. Still black water mirrors the aurora, the stars and the walls (a reflective water plane on the lake pattern with a slow 0.3 m swell and wind ripples). A fishing village of 6–10 warm lights sits on the far left shore; in diamond dust each light throws a pillar. Drifting ice floes (6–14 pale slabs) turn slowly with the current and catch the aurora on their tops. Snow lies on the jetty's rail in front; the keys are ice-blue glass along the jetty's edge.
- **Day:** blue-white walls with rock showing through, grey-green water, the village lights off. **Dusk/dawn:** alpenglow on the peaks. **Night:** the aurora over the sea-end, stars, the village lights and their reflections.
- **Weather:** rain falls as sleet here (rain → 0.5 rain + 0.5 snow), storms are blizzards (the cabin's `coldSky` logic shared as `WorldWeather.cold(_:)` in Core), fog banks hide the far end, diamond dust glitters over the water.
- **Parameters:** `visual` ["Curtain Lights", "Galaxy Warp"]; `SkyHold.spec(default: .deepNight)`; `AuroraMode.spec(default: .always)`; `floes` .6 ("Ice floes", hero); `village` .7 ("Village lights"); `swell` .4 ("Swell"); `keys.show`, `keys.height`.
- **Ambience:** `iceGroan` 0.5 (+0.3 at night), `waterfall` 0.35 (a far falls on the right wall), `water` 0.5, `wind` 0.2 + 0.8·wind, `lapping` 0.3, `owl` 0 (none), crickets 0; `breeze` 0.2; `WeatherAmbience.levels`. Closeness base 0.45. Thunder as the lake.
- **Mood:** `electricAwe` = (energy 0.45, warmth 0.25, brightness 0.45, space 0.95, motion 0.6).

### 8.2 Visual: Curtain Lights (Core: `CurtainLights`)
- Each **note** sends a **ripple** along the curtain from the position of its key (x 0…1 across the keyboard → position along the band, 0.08…0.92), travelling outward both ways at 0.12 /s (strength 0.3 + 0.7·velocity, width 0.04 + 0.08·velocity, life 3.5 s, τ-fade). At most 8 ripples are sent to the shader (the strongest, in order); a burst is rationed to 6 new ripples per step.
- **Held notes** swell `glow` (τ 2 s up, 6 s down, cap 1): the curtain brightens up to 1.6× and its rays sharpen.
- **Chords** (3 notes inside 60 ms, `PlayingAnalyzer.classify`) set the **hue shift**: major → warm (+1: more red tops, a gold fringe), minor → cool (−1: blue-violet), tense → 0 with a purple fringe pulse; glides in RGB-equivalent over τ 1.5 s.
- **Intensity** (`roles[.energy]`) speeds the curtain's shimmer up to 2×.
- Galaxy Warp is offered as the alternative visual (streaks over the fjord's sky).
- Everything goes through `FlashLimiter` (the glow swell and ripple brightness are "sparkle").

## 9. Set 8: Bioluminescent Bay

### 9.1 World
A sheltered cove at night from a small beach: headlands either side with palms and mangroves in silhouette, a still bay mirroring the real stars (Crux over the southern headland from Melbourne), a moored boat with a lantern (whose light pools on the water and pillars in diamond dust), a lighthouse on the far point blinking every 6 s, and the **glow line**: bioluminescent plankton along the waterline that lights pale blue-green where small waves break (a slow 0.15 Hz wash). The keys are a dark glass strip along the sand with cyan glow where played.
- **Day:** a turquoise cove, white sand, the lantern off, no glow (the plankton needs the dark). **Night:** the magic.
- **Weather:** humid nights bring fireflies to the headlands; rain dulls the glow (×(1 − 0.6·rain)); storms whip the bay (reuse `OceanSwell` at small amplitude).
- **Parameters:** `visual` ["Glow Wake", "Galaxy Warp"]; `SkyHold.spec(default: .deepNight)`; `AuroraMode.spec(default: .follow)`; `glow` .7 ("Plankton glow", hero); `lantern` .6 ("Boat lantern"); `lighthouse` .5 ("Lighthouse"); `keys.show`, `keys.height`.
- **Ambience:** `lapping` 0.7, `water` 0.3, `crickets` 0.5 × night, `boat` 0.4, `breeze` 0.25 + wind, `owl` 0.15 × night, `rainOnWater` rain, `gulls` 0.2 by day; `WeatherAmbience.levels`. Closeness base 0.6.
- **Mood:** `secretGlow` = (energy 0.3, warmth 0.55, brightness 0.35, space 0.7, motion 0.4).

### 9.2 Visual: Glow Wake (Core: `GlowWake`)
- Each **note** lights a **patch** of water where its key sits (x 0.06…0.94 across the bay, depth 0.15 + 0.5·(1 − velocity): hard notes land close), radius 0.02 + 0.05·velocity, brightness 0.4 + 0.6·velocity; it stays lit while held (and under the pedal) and fades with τ 2.5 s after release (τ 5 s with linger). Cap 48 patches, 12 spawns per step, `visible(limit:)` keeps the brightest.
- **Runs** (notes within 0.25 s of the last) draw a **wake**: a streak from the previous patch to the new one that fades in 1.5 s; fast runs also send 1–3 fish darts (short bright streaks that shoot off the wake and fade in 0.6 s).
- **Chords** bloom a wide **pool** (radius 0.25) at the chord's mean x, brighter for major, deeper teal for minor.
- The glow is cyan-green (0.3, 1.0, 0.75) with a white core, and it **reflects nothing**: it is the water. Clouds of glow drift slowly with the current (patches move +0.004 /s sideways).
- All brightness goes through `FlashLimiter`.

## 12. Performance
- The aurora adds at most 2 `fbm3` calls per sky pixel (2 octaves on Smooth); the star texture one sample; pillars a few `exp`s. No new per-pixel loops.
- Both worlds: the water plane is one full-screen pass (no ray marching), the walls/headlands are analytic silhouettes with ≤ 3-octave noise, floes/boat/lights are instanced quads. ≤ 1 MP on Smooth.
- Fireflies and crystals are instanced through the weather layer's existing ring buffer (no buffers per frame).
- The star texture is built once off the main thread; the fallback until it arrives is the procedural star field.

## 13. Reliability
- Panic and instrument switches stop Polar Choir's swells and Plankton Keys' sparks within 50 ms; nothing sounds by itself.
- Switching visuals lets held notes go (no stuck ripples or patches).
- Leaving a world with `AuroraMode.always` returns the aurora to Follow the sky (the mode lives in the world's parameters, not the sky settings).
- A missing or failed star texture never blocks a frame.
- Ids wrap safely (ripples, patches) in hours-long sessions; ripple and patch lists are capped.

## 14. Testing
Every task writes its failing tests first (CI on GitHub Actions is the test runner; the local g++ mirror measures the DSP). The suite must stay under the test job's 8-minute budget, so new render tests reuse the small 640 × 360 harness and at most a few seconds of stepping.

Highlights:
- **DSP:** tuning, formant peaks, breath, swell, glass, bark, sparks, tremolo rate, decay, stealing, dial sweeps, parameter tables, extremes finite, panic, CPU budgets.
- **Sky:** weather values; aurora visibility by latitude and Kp; `always` and `never`; the aurora only at night and dimmed by cloud; the star catalogue (925 entries, Sirius/Canopus/Acrux present with their magnitudes, no NaNs, RA 0…360, Dec −90…90); constellation lines reference only catalogue stars; the star texture is deterministic and puts Sirius's light at its RA/Dec; `deepNight`; `constellationLines` decodes with a default; `humid` and `diamondDust` decode; fireflies only at night; pillars only with a low light.
- **App:** halos test style for the aurora (pole-ward sky brighter with aurora 1 than 0; with the view away from the pole no change); real stars (Sirius's screen point bright); constellation lines brighten the segment between Acrux and Gacrux at lines 1 vs 0; fireflies and crystals appear only in their weathers and scale with quality; both worlds' shaders compile and squares are written without `pow`; previews structured at night, dawn, noon; Curtain Lights ripples brighten the band near the note's azimuth; Glow Wake patches light the water at the key's x; any-aspect framing; burst budgets; gentle visuals honoured; the new sets load their engines and presets; the cards breathe at their tempos; every credit has art.
