## 1. Purpose

This run adds two water worlds, one deep and hushed and one bright and alive. It also adds two new instrument **cores**:
- an **analogue voice**, which starts the vintage-synth family;
- **modal mallet bars**, which start the mallet family.

As always, each set has a world, a visual, an original instrument, a sky feature, a weather and ambience sounds, all tied together by a mood. The meteors reach every world with a night sky. Sea smoke reaches every world with open water.

### Success looks like
1. **Blue Depth: hold a chord and it blinks.** A sequenced pluck, through a murky dotted-eighth echo, with ghostly voices rising behind it and a big bass under the left hand. It blends when you play along with "Underwater" (F♯ minor, 118 BPM).
2. **Reef Marimba sounds like struck wood.**
   - A soft yarn mallet is round and dark; a hard one is bright.
   - The resonator tube blooms.
   - The bar's overtones die 4 and 10 times faster than the note, as on a real marimba.
   - Hold a note and it rolls like a marimbist's. Press harder and the roll swells.
3. Both worlds look stunning and stay smooth on **Smooth** on the Intel MacBook Pro.
4. **Real meteor showers** appear on their real dates in every world with a night sky.
   - Each comes from its real radiant, at the rate your city would actually see. From Melbourne there are no Perseids at all, because their radiant never rises there. The Eta Aquariids, by contrast, are a southern favourite.
   - By default the rate is a little more generous than a real night, so shooting stars are a treat rather than a rarity.
5. **Sea smoke** curls off the water on bitter, still mornings. It appears over the lake, the beach, the road's sea, the fjord and the bay, and never on the reef, which is too warm.
6. **Ambience in key grows:** the submarine's sonar pings in your key.
7. One tap on a set loads everything, and each part can still be swapped. Demo clips of both instruments and both ambiences come with the release.

### Standing rules (unchanged)
- Everything is a set plus ambience, tied by mood.
- Sounds are original synthesis: no samples, and no protected library is copied, extracted or decrypted.
- **"Underwater" is credited as text and a link only.** Neither the card nor the world draws on the song's artwork, its lyrics or its 360° short film. The submarine is the backlog's own world (#60), not an image taken from the song.
- **The reef is born from its place**, so it has no song credit and gets a place card (the Sets 9 & 10 pattern).
- **Respect over cliché.**
  - The reef sounds of its own life: its shrimp, fish, birds, water and weather. No "tropical" shorthand and no borrowed cultural sounds.
  - The marimba's Steel dial is a set of tuned partials, not an imitation of a style.
- Every world runs on Smooth: ≤ 1 MP, no per-pixel ray marching, ≤ 3 noise octaves, and no buffers made per frame.
- Fireballs, sky brightenings and glow swells go through `FlashLimiter`.
- Sets and settings saved by older builds still load.

### Research that shaped the design
- **"Underwater":**
  - **The release:** RÜFÜS DU SOL, *Solace* (2018), track 7. It was released on 10 August 2018 as the album's second single, runs 5:49 and was produced by Jon George, Tyrone Lindqvist and James Hunt (Wikipedia; Spotify).
  - **Key and tempo:** F♯ minor at 118 BPM. Tunebat and SongBPM both give this, but both take it from Spotify's analysis, so it counts as one source.
  - **How it began:** the band say the song "came from a patch that we found on the Prophet-6. An arpeggiated delayed part starts the entire song" (MusicTech, 2021).
  - **The arp and its echo:** "the very first thing is … that arp … we were taking turns trying to put a delay on it … so it makes it hard to play in time" (PAPER, 2019).
  - **The Prophet:** it is "a celebration of the Prophet … every different sound is a different iteration of what it does well, like the big bass hits" (PAPER).
  - **How it sounds:** "blinking, repetitive synth, murky as if coming through depths of blue. A chorus of ghostly voices lures you deeper" (Billboard, 2018).
  - **The album's gear:** *Solace* leaned on the Sequential Prophet-6 and OB-6 and the Minimoog Model D (MusicTech).
- **Marimba bars:**
  - They are tuned 1 : 4 : 10. The third partial sits about 9.2–10 times the fundamental (CCRMA; La Favre).
  - The second partial must be within ±15 cents of the double octave for a clear pitch.
  - Tuned 1 : 4 : 10, the second partial decays 4× faster than the fundamental and the third 10× faster (Bork 1995).
  - Players use soft, heavy mallets for the bass and hard ones for the treble (La Favre).
  - Each bar has a closed tube tuned to its fundamental; "without the resonator pipe, there would just be a soft popping sound" (Yamaha).
  - Coupling the tube radiates more at the fundamental and shortens the bar's decay (J. Sound Vib. 2023).
  - Mallet contact lasts about 1–2 ms. Steelpan sticks contact for 1.2–2.2 ms, less when struck harder (KTH, 1988).
  - Steelpan notes tune their lowest three partials to the octave and the twelfth, or the double octave (Hansen & Rossing).
- **Reef sound:**
  - Snapping shrimp crackle above 2 kHz all the time, like Rice Krispies or frying bacon. Fish calls sit at 100–1000 Hz (Kaplan et al. 2015; ASA 2022).
  - The shrimp grow about 10 dB louder at dusk and peak at dawn and dusk.
  - Fish choruses also peak at dawn and dusk. On the Great Barrier Reef the drumming builds through the late afternoon, peaks at dusk and then stops abruptly (McCauley & Cato, via New Scientist; Lillis & Mooney 2018).
  - Healthy reefs are loud.
- **Coral spawning:**
  - It happens one to six nights after the October, November and December full moons, at night, usually at least about three hours after sunset, when plankton-eating fish sleep.
  - Inshore reefs spawn a month before the outer reef.
  - Pink, yellow and orange bundles rise like an upside-down snowstorm and form slicks on the surface (GBRMPA; Queensland; AIMS).
- **Humpbacks:**
  - The east Australian population breeds in the reef's lagoon between June and October, peaking in August.
  - Only males sing. The song is built from units, grouped into phrases, repeated as themes in a fixed order. It lasts 10–20 minutes and is repeated for hours.
  - Units mostly fall between about 100 Hz and 2 kHz and sweep up and down (Payne & McVay; Macknight et al. 2001; IMMA; J. Biosci. 2015).
- **Deep light:**
  - Water absorbs red within the top 10 m, orange by 40 m and yellow before 100 m. About 1% of the light remains at 100 m.
  - The sunlight zone ends at 200 m and the twilight zone at 1,000 m. Below that the only light is bioluminescence, nearly all of it blue (NOAA; NSF; Johnsen).
  - **Snell's window:** from below, the whole sky squeezes into a 97° cone, bounded by the 48.6° critical angle. Outside it, the surface mirrors the water (Wired; University of Arizona optics).
- **Bubbles:** a bubble rings at its Minnaert frequency, f·a ≈ 3.26 m/s, so a 1 mm bubble rings at about 3.3 kHz. This is the "plink" of a dripping tap (Minnaert 1933).
- **Meteor showers:**
  - **The data:** each shower's activity period, peak solar longitude, radiant and its drift, V∞, r and ZHR come from the IMO 2026 working list (Tables 5 and 6).
  - **Activity curves:** Jenniskens' (1994) visual curves follow ZHR = ZHRmax·10^(−B·|λ⊙ − λmax|). The Perseids, Geminids, Quadrantids, Ursids and Leonids have both a peak and a base component (as tabulated by the NASA Meteoroid Environment Office, 2017).
  - **Angular speeds:** about 2–30°/s, depending on speed, elevation and distance from the radiant (IMO table).
  - **Colour and trains:** slow meteors look yellow-orange and fast ones blue or green. Trains form above 80 km and mostly follow fast meteors (AMS).
  - **Sporadic meteors:** more of them fall before dawn (AMS).
- **Sea smoke:**
  - It forms when very cold air (typically 0 °C or below, and much colder than the water) drifts over warmer water in light wind.
  - It looks turbulent: wisps and rotating columns ("steam devils").
  - It is usually a few metres deep, sometimes 20–30 m (SKYbrary; Maine Sea Grant; Wikipedia).
- **Composers' precedents for water:**
  - massed harps and a high choir with no bass (ABZÛ);
  - a low hum instead of literal water (Finding Nemo);
  - marimba and steel as the tropical precedent;
  - spaces under water muffled, with no clear direction.

## 4. The instruments

### 4.1 The analogue voice core (`AnalogVoice.hpp`)
- **Oscillators:** two PolyBLEP oscillators per voice.
  - Oscillator A has a continuous shape, from saw through square to narrow pulse.
  - Oscillator B is a saw that can be detuned.
  - There is also a sub-octave square taken from A's phase, and white noise.
- **Slop:** each oscillator's pitch wanders ±3 cents on a smoothed random walk (0.1–0.4 Hz, seeded per voice), and the cutoff wanders ±1%. This is the gentle unsteadiness of an analogue poly.
- **Filter:**
  - A 4-pole (24 dB/oct) zero-delay-feedback ladder low-pass: TPT one-poles with a `tanh` in the feedback, so resonance stays soft.
  - Resonance 0…0.95, below self-oscillation. Cutoff 20 Hz–18 kHz with key tracking.
  - A 2-pole state-variable high-pass to clear low mud.
- **Envelopes:** ADSRs for the amp and the filter. Retriggers start from the current level, so they never click.
- **Formant helper:** two band-passes with gliding centre frequencies, for voice-like layers.
- **Cost:** about 120–160 cycles per voice per sample.
- **Future use:** this is the start of the vintage-synth core (#6 in the pairings ranking). Replicant Brass, Juno Prayer and the anthem voices reuse it later.

### 4.2 Blue Depth (after "Underwater")
- **Voices:** 12 (pool 16).
- **The voice:**
  - Oscillator A at shape 0.35 (a saw-leaning pulse); oscillator B a saw at +6 cents; the sub at −10 dB; noise at −32 dB.
  - Ladder resonance 0.3.
  - Filter envelope: decay 140–320 ms, amount +2.5 octaves.
  - Amp envelope: attack 2 ms, decay 220–500 ms, sustain 0.15, release 260 ms. This is the pluck.
  - Key tracking 50%.
- **Blink (0…1):**
  - At 0 the instrument plays as struck: each key is one voice.
  - Above 0, notes you hold at **C3 (MIDI 48) or above are arpeggiated**:
    - An arp steps through them in the order you pressed them. Notes pressed within 30 ms of each other are sorted low to high.
    - It takes one step per sixteenth note at the Tempo and retriggers that note's voice, both filter and amp. This is the "blinking, repetitive synth".
    - The gate shortens from 70% to 25% of a step as Blink rises.
    - A gentle four-step accent pattern (1.0, 0.82, 0.9, 0.82 × the note's velocity) keeps the beat.
  - A single held note repeats; a held chord ripples.
  - Notes held by the pedal stay in the arp until the pedal lifts.
  - **The arp clock** starts on the first note of a phrase (when no notes were held before) and runs on the sample clock. When you let go, the arp stops after the current step, and the tails ring on through the echo.
- **Echo (0…1):**
  - A stereo dotted-eighth echo: 3/16 of a bar at the Tempo, which is 381.4 ms at 118 BPM.
  - Feedback 0.25–0.7 and mix 0–45%.
  - Each repeat is darker: a 2.6 kHz one-pole in the loop, which Murk lowers to 1.2 kHz.
  - A slow wow (0.25 Hz, ±3 ms), and ping-pong set by Width.
  - Against the sixteenth-note arp it gives the off-grid shimmer the band describe ("hard to play in time").
- **Murk (0…1):** the underwater muffle.
  - The filter's ceiling falls from 7 kHz (0) to 650 Hz (1).
  - A slow "current" sways it (0.06–0.15 Hz, ±18%).
  - The reverb's highs die faster: damping drops from 4 kHz to 1.2 kHz.
- **Ghosts (0…1):** a wordless choir behind the held notes.
  - Each held note at C3 or above also sounds an octave up through the formant helper, drifting between "oo" (325, 700 Hz) and "oh" (450, 800 Hz).
  - The sound source is a soft pulse with 25% breath noise.
  - It swells in over 1.2–2.5 s, is panned wide and sent mostly to the reverb, at −16 … −4 dB relative to the pluck.
  - Ghosts are not arpeggiated: they sustain under the blinking.
- **Bass (0…1):** notes below C3 are the "big bass hits".
  - They are not arpeggiated. Each is a held voice with the sub up to −2 dB, the ladder opened one octave, gentle drive (`tanh`, ×1.5 at Bass 1) and a slower filter decay (450 ms).
  - The dial sets their level and drive. At 0 they play like the other notes and join the arp.
- **Tempo:** 70…160 BPM, default 118. It sets both the arp and the echo. At 118 it locks to the record.
- **Pitch wheel and pressure:**
  - The wheel bends every voice ±2 semitones.
  - Aftertouch (channel or poly) opens the filter by up to +0.8 octave and swells the ghosts by up to +6 dB. Leaning in brings the voices forward.
- **Space:** a dark reverb, size 0.35–0.9, decay 2.5–7 s.
- **Width:** oscillator spread, echo ping-pong and the ghosts' spread.
- **Mood:**
  - energy shortens the gate and the decay;
  - warmth darkens the ghosts' formants and adds sub;
  - brightness moves the Murk ceiling ±20%;
  - space adds Space and echo feedback;
  - motion deepens the current and the slop.
- **Dials, in order:** Blink .60, Murk .55, Ghosts .40, Echo .50, Bass .55, Tempo 118, Space .55, Width .60.
- **Presets:**

  | id | Name | Blink | Murk | Ghosts | Echo | Bass | Tempo | Space | Width |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `blue-depth` | Blue Depth | .60 | .55 | .40 | .50 | .55 | 118 | .55 | .60 |
  | `ghost-signal` | Ghost Signal | .35 | .45 | .75 | .65 | .30 | 118 | .70 | .70 |
  | `pressure-hull` | Pressure Hull | .75 | .80 | .25 | .40 | .80 | 118 | .45 | .50 |
  | `surface-light` | Surface Light | .50 | .25 | .35 | .55 | .40 | 118 | .50 | .65 |

- **Test hooks:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_ARP = 213` (Blink is ignored; plain voices) and `ISD_P_DEBUG_DRY = 214` (no echo, ghosts, reverb, slop or current).
- **Targets (brief):**
  - **Tuning:** within 2 cents with DRY, and within 6 cents with slop.
  - **The arp:** onsets on a sixteenth grid at 118 BPM (127.1 ms apart), each within 1 ms.
  - **The echo:** its first repeat at 381.4 ± 1 ms.
  - **Murk:** Murk 1 against 0 lowers the spectral centroid by ≥ 50%.
  - **Ghosts:** on a held C-minor triad, Ghosts 1 against 0 raises the 250–900 Hz energy over 1–2 s by ≥ 6 dB.
  - **Bass:** a note below C3 at Bass 1 makes a single onset (it is not arpeggiated) and has ≥ 6 dB more energy below 120 Hz than at Bass 0.
  - **The wheel:** full up reads +200 ± 5 cents.
  - **Aftertouch:** at 1 it raises the 1–4 kHz energy by ≥ 4 dB.
  - **Panic:** everything, including the echo and the reverb, is silent within 50 ms.
  - **CPU:** < 6% for 8 held, arpeggiated notes with the echo and ghosts.

### 4.3 The mallet core (`ModalBar.hpp`)
- **A struck bar** is up to 8 modes. Each is a decaying sinusoid computed by a recursive complex oscillator (4 multiplies per sample), with its own frequency ratio, decay time and gain.
  - Bars do not bend, so pitch is fixed per note.
  - A new strike adds to the running modes, as when a real bar is struck again while ringing. Rolls depend on this.
- **Mallet:**
  - Each strike is a half-sine force pulse with contact time τ (0.5–5 ms). Each mode is excited by the pulse's spectrum at that mode's frequency: |P(f)| ∝ |cos(π f τ)| / |1 − (2 f τ)²|, normalised at f → 0.
  - So a soft mallet (long τ) barely reaches the upper modes, and a hard mallet rings them all.
  - Velocity shortens τ by up to 40%, because a harder strike compresses the yarn.
  - Hard mallets also add a 1 ms contact click shaped by the same spectrum.
- **Strike position:**
  - Each mode's amplitude is the free-free bar's mode shape at the strike point, tabulated at 9 positions from the centre to the end and interpolated.
  - At the centre the 4× mode sits on a node and is silent. Off centre, all three tuned modes ring.
- **Tube:**
  - A closed tube tuned to the fundamental is a high-Q two-pole resonance at f1, fed by the fundamental mode. It rings about 60% as long as the bar.
  - It adds the bloom. With the tube, the bar's fundamental radiates more and decays 15% sooner, following the 2023 coupling result.
- **Untuned modes:** a torsional mode (about 2.7 × f1, −24 dB, fast) and a lateral one (about 6.3 ×, −30 dB) give the wood its knock.
- **Cost:** up to 8 modes × 4 multiplies, plus the tube: about 1,000 multiplies per sample for 32 voices.
- **Future use:** this starts the mallet core (#2 in the ranking). Canopy Marimba, Motel Vibes, Hammock Pan, Thumb Kalimba, Star Glock, Ombak Bells and Harbour Chimes reuse it later.

### 4.4 Reef Marimba
- **Voices:** 32 (pool 40), because mallet players play fast runs and rolls.
- **Range:** C2–C7 (five octaves). Notes outside the range fold in by octaves.
- **The bar:**
  - Tuned modes at 1 and 3.99. The third tuned mode is at 9.2 from C5 up, rising to 10.0 in the bass (C2–C4), plus the torsional and lateral modes.
  - The fundamental's T60 is 2.6 s at C2, falling log-linearly to 0.35 s at C7.
  - Each mode decays faster by its ratio (Bork): the 4× mode 4 times faster, the 10× mode 10 times faster.
- **Mallet (0…1):**
  - τ runs from 4.5 ms (0, soft yarn) to 0.6 ms (1, hard rubber).
  - Below C3 the mallet is a little softer automatically (τ × 1.4), as players use softer, heavier mallets low down.
- **Strike (0…1):** 0 is the centre (round, with the 4× partial silent), 0.5 a standard off-centre stroke, 1 near the end (thin and knocky).
- **Tube (0…1):** the resonator's level. 0 is a dry wooden pop; 1 is the full concert bloom (+9 dB at the fundamental).
- **Roll (0…1):**
  - Hold a note past 0.3 s and it rolls: strokes alternate between two mallets with slightly different strike points and τ.
  - The rate is 9–13 Hz (set by Roll) with ±12% timing jitter. Each stroke is 0.55 × the note's velocity.
  - Aftertouch raises the roll's rate up to 16 Hz and its strokes up to 0.95 × the velocity: a crescendo under your finger.
  - Up to 4 notes roll at once; the others ring and decay.
  - At Roll 0 nothing rolls.
- **Steel (0…1):** a tuned-steel layer that shimmers over the wood, like sunlight on the bars. At 0 the bars are pure wood.
  - Partials at 1, 2 and 3 × f0 (the octave and the twelfth, as steelpans tune them).
  - A gentle beating pair on the fundamental, 0.5–1.5 Hz apart.
  - A decay 1.5 × longer than the wood's.
  - Harder strikes add more upper partials (their gains ∝ velocity²).
- **Space:** open air over the lagoon. Reverb size 0.15–0.45, decay 0.6–1.5 s, with a 6 ms slap off the water.
- **Width:** bars spread from low on the left to high on the right (±0.6), like standing at the instrument.
- **Mood:**
  - energy hardens the mallet;
  - warmth adds tube;
  - brightness adds Steel and moves the strike outward;
  - space adds Space;
  - motion speeds the roll.
- **Dials, in order:** Mallet .45, Strike .40, Tube .70, Roll .35, Steel .20, Space .35, Width .55.
- **Presets:**

  | id | Name | Mallet | Strike | Tube | Roll | Steel | Space | Width |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `reef-marimba` | Reef Marimba | .45 | .40 | .70 | .35 | .20 | .35 | .55 |
  | `soft-yarn` | Soft Yarn | .15 | .35 | .85 | .45 | .00 | .40 | .50 |
  | `lagoon-steel` | Lagoon Steel | .60 | .45 | .55 | .25 | .70 | .40 | .60 |
  | `night-reef` | Night Reef | .30 | .50 | .80 | .55 | .10 | .55 | .60 |

- **Test hooks:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_ROLL = 215` and `ISD_P_DEBUG_BAR_ONLY = 216` (no tube, steel, untuned modes or reverb).
- **Targets (brief):**
  - **Tuning:** within 2 cents from C2 to C7.
  - **Partials:** with BAR_ONLY on A3, spectral peaks at 1, at 3.99 ± 0.02 (within 15 cents of the double octave) and at 9.2–10 × f0.
  - **Decays:** the 4× partial's T60 is 1/4 ± 20% of the fundamental's, and the third partial's is 1/10 ± 25%.
  - **Mallet:** on A3, Mallet 1 against 0 raises the 2–6 kHz energy over 0–50 ms by ≥ 12 dB.
  - **Strike:** at Strike 0 the 4× partial is ≥ 20 dB below its level at Strike 0.5.
  - **Tube:** Tube 1 against 0 lifts the fundamental band by ≥ 6 dB.
  - **Roll:** a held note at Roll 0.5 shows onsets at 9–13 Hz after 0.3 s, and none at Roll 0. Aftertouch raises the rate.
  - **Steel:** at Steel 1 the partial at 2× is present at ≥ −20 dB.
  - **CPU:** < 6% for a 12-note chord plus a 4-note roll.

## 5. Ambience

Six sources are appended to the end of `ISDAmbienceSource`, bringing the count to 38:
- `ISD_AMB_SONAR`, `ISD_AMB_HULL_CREAK`, `ISD_AMB_WHALE`, `ISD_AMB_BUBBLES`, `ISD_AMB_SHRIMP`, `ISD_AMB_FISH_CHORUS`
- with `AmbienceSource.sonar, .hullCreak, .whaleSong, .bubbles, .snappingShrimp, .fishChorus`

- **Sonar (in key):** a soft ping, every 14–30 s ÷ density, at −26 dBFS peak.
  - A pure sine at the key's tonic in the octave 880–1760 Hz (E6 at 1318.5 Hz for E).
  - A 25 ms attack and a 0.35 s body, ringing into a long underwater reverb (3–5 s, low-passed at 3 kHz).
  - A faint return 1.4–2.6 s later at −20 dB: the echo off the seabed.
  - It follows `isd_ambience_set_key` (Sets 9 & 10) and retunes only between pings. With no key sensed it keeps its last root, starting on E, the temple bell's default.
- **Hull creak:** the pressure hull settling, every 12–45 s ÷ density, at −30 dBFS. The world scales it with depth.
  - Low groans: a stick-slip pulse train gliding between 8 and 30 Hz, through resonances at 60–250 Hz, lasting 0.6–3 s.
  - Rarer metal ticks: a 1–3 kHz ping of 0.2 s.
- **Whale song:** a far-off humpback, at −30 dBFS.
  - **Units:**
    - moans: 100–400 Hz, with slow glides over 1–3 s;
    - whoops: rising from 200 to 800 Hz;
    - cries: 800–1,800 Hz;
    - grunts.
  - **Structure:** units form phrases of 3–6 units. Each phrase repeats 2–4 times as a theme. Themes follow a seeded fixed order that drifts slowly over the season, as real songs change.
  - **Distance:** low-passed above 2 kHz, with a long reverb.
  - **Timing:** 20–60 s of singing, then 30–180 s of quiet ÷ density.
  - **Season:** the worlds gate it by season. The reef hears it June to October. The submarine hears it all year, more quietly.
- **Bubbles:** at −28 dBFS peak.
  - Each bubble is a damped sine at its Minnaert frequency (radius 0.4–3 mm, so 1.1–8 kHz), lasting 5–25 ms with an 8% upward chirp.
  - Bubbles come in bursts of 4–30 over 0.3–1.5 s, with idle bursts every 8–30 s ÷ density.
  - `isd_ambience_trigger_bubbles(ISDAmbience *, float strength)` starts a burst. A world asks for one through `AmbienceScene.bubbleBurst: Double?`, a one-shot strength 0…1 like `wave` and `thunder`; the App passes it to the trigger.
- **Snapping shrimp:** the reef's crackle, a continuous bed calibrated to −24 dBFS at level 1.
  - Poisson snaps at 400–1,200 per second. Each is a 0.1–0.3 ms broadband click, high-passed at 2 kHz, with Pareto-distributed amplitudes so a few snaps stand out.
  - Scattered across the stereo field.
  - The world sets the rate by the hour: day 1.0, dusk and dawn 1.6, night 1.25.
- **Fish chorus:** at −30 dBFS; the world gates it by the hour.
  - Low pops, grunts and knocks at 100–800 Hz: damselfish-like trains of 3–10 pulses at 20–60 Hz, knocks and croaks.
  - They gather into a chorus at dawn and dusk.
  - The dusk drumming builds through the late afternoon and stops abruptly after dusk, as on the reef.
- **Levels** are calibrated as before.
- **`WeatherAmbience`:**
  - In sea smoke (`steam > 0.3`) the outdoors is a little muffled and the wind drops, as in mist.
  - Under water, rain is a soft surface hiss: in the submarine only at shallow depth, and in the reef's lower half.

### 6.2 New sky state
`SkyState` gains:

- **`meteors: [Meteor]`:** the meteors now in the sky, at most 6 slots of live heads and trains. Each `Meteor` holds:

### 6.3 Real meteor showers (SkyKit)
- **`MeteorShowers` (Core, data):** 14 showers. The data comes from:
  - the IMO 2026 working list (Table 5): activity window, peak λ⊙, radiant, V∞, r and ZHR;
  - IMO Table 6: the radiant's drift per day;
  - Jenniskens' visual activity curves, as the NASA MEO tabulated them. Where the IMO's ZHR differs, the curve is scaled to the IMO peak.

  - A shower is active only inside its IMO dates.
  - The radiant drifts linearly from its peak position.
  - ACE has no published slope, so it uses the typical B of 0.2.
- **Solar longitude:** `SolarLongitude.degrees(at:)` gives the Sun's apparent ecliptic longitude (Meeus, low precision; equinox J2000; accurate to about 0.01°). It uses the sky's own clock, so quick mode moves through the calendar as its days pass. The calendar works for any year.
- **Visible rate:** for each shower, the hourly rate over the whole sky is
  `HR = ZHR(λ⊙) × sin(h_R) × r^(LM − 6.5) × (1 − 0.95·cloud) × (1 − 0.5·mist)`
  for a radiant altitude h_R > 0, and zero otherwise.
  - The limiting magnitude falls with moonlight and twilight: `LM = 6.5 − 2.5·moonGlow − 3·twilight`. Here moonGlow = moon illumination × sin(moon altitude) when the moon is up, and twilight = smoothstep(−18°, −6°, sun altitude).
  - There are no meteors with the sun above −6°.
- **Sporadic meteors:** 7 per hour × (1 + 0.6·cos(2π(t − 6 h)/24 h)) in local solar time, so 1.6× at 6 a.m. and 0.4× at 6 p.m. The same sky factors apply.
- **The Advanced "Meteors" setting** (0.5…10, default 2) multiplies everything. Rates count per real second of viewing, not per sky hour, so quick mode isn't sparser.
- **Spawning** (deterministic, on its own seeded stream like the aurora's):
  1. A Poisson process at the total rate picks a source by its share of the rate.
  2. **Magnitude:** from the source's population index, P(brighter than m) ∝ r^m, from +5.5 down to −8. With r = 2.5, about 1 meteor in 100 is brighter than magnitude 0 and 1 in 1,000 is a fireball (≤ −3).
  3. **Distance from the radiant D:** 15°–100°, weighted by sin D, at a random position angle around the radiant.
  4. **Path:** the meteor runs away from the radiant along the great circle through it. Its length is (6° + 14°·u)·√(sin D) for a uniform u, and 15% longer for each magnitude brighter than 2.
  5. **Speed:** ω = (0.62·V∞ − 0.0018·V∞²)°/s × sin(h) × sin(D), where h is the meteor's elevation. This fits the IMO table: about 30°/s at 60 km/s, high and far from the radiant. Duration is length ÷ ω, clamped to 0.08–2.5 s.
  6. **Rejection:** a meteor below 5° elevation is dropped. Brightness is multiplied by (0.3 + 0.7·√(sin h)) for the airmass.
  7. **Sporadic radiants** are random over the celestial sphere above −20° altitude, with speeds of 20–70 km/s (mean 40).
- **Look:**
  - A meteor is a thin streak (at least 1.2 px; the shader gets its pixel's angular size from `fwidth` of the view direction).
  - A bright head runs along the path, with a tail fading over the last 25% behind it.
  - The light curve is sin(πt)^0.7 over the meteor's life (its base is never negative). Core computes it each frame and passes the head's brightness, so the shader has no `pow`. 10% of bright meteors end in a terminal flare.
  - **Colour by speed:** slow (< 30 km/s) meteors are warm yellow-orange (1.0, 0.8, 0.55); medium ones white (1.0, 0.97, 0.9); fast (> 55 km/s) bright ones blue-green-white (0.75, 1.0, 0.9). Faint meteors look white, because the eye loses colour in faint light.
  - Brightness follows the stars' magnitude scale.
  - A fireball (≤ −4) also sets `meteorFlash`, which brightens the sky by at most 8%.
- **Trains:**
  - A fast meteor (V∞ ≥ 55 km/s) brighter than −1 leaves a faint greenish glow (0.5, 1.0, 0.7) along its path, at 3–10% of the head's peak.
  - The train fades over 1–8 s (longer for brighter meteors), widens and drifts or curls by 0.2–1° (a one-octave noise offset over its life).
- **On screen:**
  - `skyRadiance` evaluates the 6 slots in the dome pass, before the clouds and the moon, so clouds hide them and mist dims them.
  - Because it runs in `skyRadiance`, meteors appear in every world's sky and in every reflection: the lake, the fjord, the bay and the reef's lagoon.
  - **Cost:** a bounding dot product per slot rejects nearly every pixel, and empty slots exit at once.
- **What's tonight:** when a shower is active, the Living Sky panel shows one line, for example:
  - "Meteors: the Leonids — peak in 13 days; the radiant rises at 01:12"
  - "… the radiant stays below your horizon" (the Perseids from Melbourne)

### 6.4 Sea smoke (WeatherKit)
- **Sky state:** `LivingSky.step` sets `s.steam = values.steam`. A world's rule may change it: the warm rule zeroes it.
- **The steam layer:** `WeatherLayer` gains a seventh instance set, **steam** (kind 6).
  - **Where:** up to 40 (Smooth) / 90 / 150 wisps rise from the world's **water band**, `WeatherLayer.waterBand: SIMD4<Float>` (x0, y0, x1, y1 in NDC). The world sets the band each frame; with no band there is no steam.
  - **Shape and motion:**
    - Each wisp is a soft column of 4–6 overlapping blobs. It rises 3–12% of the view height over 6–14 s while widening 2–3×.
    - Its blobs rotate slowly round its axis (the "steam devil" look), and it leans downwind.
    - It fades in over 1 s and out over the last 40% of its life.
    - Wisps nearer the bottom of the band are larger.
  - **Colour:** the sky's horizon colour × 1.25, plus the sun's glow when the sun is low and in front, which turns it gold-pink at sunrise. At night it is a moonlit blue-grey.
  - **Density** follows `steam`. A thin flat mist sits just above the band (alpha 0.25·steam).
- **Worlds with water set their band:**
  - the lake (the water below the far shore)
  - the beach (the sea out to the horizon)
  - the road (the sea below the cliffs)
  - the fjord
  - the bay
- **Worlds without open water** show sea smoke as a low frost haze: ground mist and frost on the glass. These are the cabin, the bedroom, the tea house and the garden. The lounge has no weather.
- **The reef:** `WorldWeather.warm` turns sea smoke into a light morning haze (steam 0, mist 0.25).
- **The submarine** sees it from below: at shallow depth Snell's window turns milky (§8.1).
- **Sound:** sea smoke hushes the outdoors a little (§5).

## 7. Shared pieces

### 7.1 `MeteorMode`
- **The type:** `public enum MeteorMode: String, Codable, CaseIterable { case follow = "Follow the calendar", always = "Always a shower", never = "Never" }`, with `static func spec(default:) -> ParamSpec` (id `meteors.mode`, group "Sky", name "Meteors"). It follows the `AuroraMode` pattern.
- **Always:** a steady shower of ZHR 60, plus the sporadics. Its radiant sits 80° up, nearly overhead, so meteors streak down toward the horizon whichever way a world looks.
- **The director:** `SceneDirector.step` reads the mode from the parameters (defaulting to `.follow` when it is absent) and passes it to `LivingSky.step(now:dt:hold:aurora:meteors:)`.
- **Which worlds offer it:** every world with a night sky, defaulting to follow. That is the lake, beach, road, cabin, bedroom, fjord, bay, tea house, garden and reef. The lounge and the submarine don't list it.

### 7.2 `SkyU` growth
- After `still`, add `float4 meteorA[6]`, `meteorB[6]` and `meteorC[6]`:
  - A: start direction xyz, head progress 0…1.
  - B: end direction xyz, brightness (0 means the slot is empty).
  - C: colour rgb, train 0…1 (0 means a live meteor).
- Add one `float4 flash` row: (meteorFlash, 0, 0, 0).
- `SkyU` grows from 25 to 44 rows (704 bytes). It is still one `setFragmentBytes` call.
- `SkyLayer` fills the slots from `SkyState.meteors`, advancing head progress by the frame's time.

### 7.3 The Underwater Kit (WorldKit, `UnderwaterShaders.swift`)
Shared MSL helpers for any world under water: the submarine, and the reef's lower half.
- **`waterColour(depth, distance, light)`:**
  - Beer–Lambert attenuation per channel, with clear-ocean coefficients: red 0.35 /m, green 0.07 /m, blue 0.03 /m.
  - The light from above falls to 1% at 100 m.
  - The tone curve keeps a deep blue readable to about 400 m, the way the eye adapts. Below that, only living light and lamps remain.
- **`snellWindow(viewDir, depth, t)`:**
  - Looking up within 48.6° of the vertical, you see the whole sky, squeezed by Snell's law and sampled through `skyRadiance`, with the sun or moon in it.
  - Beyond that angle, the surface mirrors the water below.
  - The window's edge has a faint rainbow fringe, and it wobbles with the surface (2-octave noise on the normal).
  - It fades out by 40 m.
- **`caustics(p, t)`:** the dancing net of light on sand and coral in the top 15 m. A two-layer warped sine and cell pattern, 2 octaves.
- **`lightShafts(uv, lightDir, t)`:**
  - 6–10 soft slanted bands along the refracted sun or moon.
  - Their brightness flickers with the surface (1-D noise along each band), and they fade with depth.
  - Drawn in 2-D, with no ray marching.
- **Marine snow:** a Core particle set of drifting flecks (150 / 300 / 400 by quality), shared by both worlds.
- Everything here stays within 3 noise octaves.

### 7.4 The porthole (Window Kit)
- **Shape and material:** `WindowSpec` gains `shape: Shape` (`.panes`, the default, or `.round`) and `Material.brass`: a thick brass ring with 12 bolts and a rubber gasket, with warm highlights from the cabin light.
- **The glass:** round glass is one pane, with the condensation and drip fields masked to the circle. There is never frost under water.
- **Thick acrylic:** the outside is sampled through a slight barrel warp (4% at the rim), with a faint reflection of the cabin.
- **Condensation:** cold sea outside and warm, damp air inside fog the glass's edge, more so at depth.

### 7.5 `WorldWeather.warm`
"It's warm here." The rule changes the weather like this:
- snow and flurries fall as rain;
- blizzards become squalls;
- sleet and hail become rain;
- frost, rime, diamond dust, ice fog and sea smoke never happen (sea smoke and ice fog become a light haze);
- light pillars go too.

It leaves fireflies alone; a world that has none (the reef) zeroes them itself.

### 7.6 The instrument's pulse (Core)
- **The type:** `SceneDirector` gains `public var instrumentPulse: InstrumentPulse?`, where `struct InstrumentPulse: Equatable { var bpm: Double; var gate: Double }`.
- **Who sets it:** the App, from the current patch, when the built-in instrument has an arp (Blue Depth: its Tempo, and Blink as the gate). It is nil for every other instrument.
- **Who uses it:** Deep Lights blinks on that grid, starting at a phrase's first note as the arp does. The visual and the arp then agree within a buffer.

### 7.7 Ambience in key: the sonar
The sonar uses the temple bell's key input (Sets 9 & 10: `AmbienceScene.key`, `isd_ambience_set_key`) and retunes only between pings.

### 9.2 Visual: Coral Pulse (Core: `CoralPulse`)
- **The coral groups:** the reef holds 7 groups across the frame, from low notes to high: brain corals at the left for the bass, plate and soft corals in the middle, and staghorn tips at the right for the treble.
- **Notes:** each note **opens and lights** the polyps of the group nearest its key.
  - The colour is fluorescent green, orange or pink by group, because reef corals fluoresce.
  - Brightness follows velocity.
  - Held notes keep them glowing; after release they fade with τ 2.5 s.
- **Runs:** the **school** darts along the melody.
  - It is 20–80 small fish; the Fish dial sets how many.
  - Its path follows the last 8 notes' pitches across the reef, and the school turns together, flashing silver on accents.
- **Chords:** a **colour wave** sweeps the whole reef from the chord's mean x. Major is warm pinks and oranges, minor blues and violets, and tense a flicker of purple.
- **Limits:** everything goes through `FlashLimiter`. At most 64 lit polyp patches and 12 spawns per step. The school is one simple, deterministic flocking set (Core, at most 80 points).

### 9.3 Coral spawning (Core: `CoralSpawning`)
- **When:**
  - On nights +1 … +6 after the October, November and December full moons, using the Living Sky's moon age (15.8–20.8 days) and the month of the full moon on the sky city's local date.
  - From 2.5 to 5 hours after sunset.
  - Strongest on nights +3 … +5 in November and December, as on the outer reef.
  - "Tonight" forces it on the current night; "Off" turns it off.
- **Look:**
  - Pink, orange and yellow bundles, beads of 1–2 px, rise slowly through the water (0.01–0.03 per second), wobbling.
  - They thicken into an upside-down snowstorm for 20–40 minutes, then gather into a pinkish slick on the underside of the surface.
  - The spawning hours ramp in and out over 10 minutes.
- **It mirrors the sky:** on a shower night, meteors fall above while the spawn rises below.
- **Limits:** at most 600 beads (300 on Smooth), deterministic from the seed.

## 12. Performance
- **Engines:** Blue Depth runs 12 analogue voices. Reef Marimba runs 32 modal voices with up to 8 modes each and one tube per voice. Both stay inside 6%.
- **Meteors:** at most 6 slots, each rejected for most pixels by one dot product.
- **Steam:** at most 40 / 90 / 150 instanced wisps.
- **Under water:**
  - Analytic layers, ≤ 3 noise octaves, caustics at 2.
  - No ray marching, ≤ 1 MP on Smooth.
  - The reef draws the sky above the waterline and the reef below it, each in its own scissored part of the frame.
- **Particles:** spawn beads, marine snow and the school are instanced points whose buffers are made once.

## 13. Reliability
- **The arp:** it stops and its echo empties on panic (`allSoundOff`), and when the instrument or world changes. Its clock resets after 2 s with no held notes.
- **Bend and pressure** reset when the instrument or world changes (Sets 9 & 10).
- **Flashes:** fireballs and `meteorFlash` stay inside `FlashLimiter`'s rules.
- **The calendar** works for any year, because it uses solar longitude, not dates. Outside every activity window only sporadic meteors fall.
- **Boundedness:** spawning, meteors, steam, the school and marine snow stay bounded over hours, and ids wrap.
- **The sonar** never changes pitch mid-ping.
- **The submarine at the surface:** Snell's window samples the sky only through `skyRadiance`, so a missing star texture leaves it dark rather than broken.

- **The modal core:** mode ratios and decays, the mallet spectrum, the strike nodes and the tube.

- **The meteor calendar:**

  - Each shower's ZHR is right at its peak and at its half-width.
  - The Perseids' radiant never rises from Melbourne (−37.8°).
  - The Geminids' rate from Melbourne is below their rate from Tokyo.

## 17. Decisions made without asking (with the cost if Michael decides otherwise)
1. **The reef is a split view at the waterline** rather than fully under water, so its sky can show the meteor showers. Cost: a fully underwater reef would drop the sky half; the Underwater Kit covers it.
2. **Blue Depth starts the vintage-synth core.** The pairings doc names only the mallet core for this run, so this run now adds two cores. Cost: none to the sound. A smaller analogue voice could live inside the engine instead.

5. **The default meteor rate is 2 × a real night.** Cost: the Advanced setting.
6. **Coral spawning uses the reef's own calendar** (October to December, after the full moons), not the sky city's seasons. Cost: none.
7. **The arp plays notes in the order you press them** (simultaneous presses go low to high), and **notes below C3 are the bass.** Cost: a mode switch (up, down, as played) and a split dial later.
8. **Rolling on held notes is on by default** (Roll .35). Cost: the dial goes to 0.
