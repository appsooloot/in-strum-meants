## 1. Purpose

Two more Sets, both inspired by songs from Lana Del Rey's *Honeymoon* (2015). Each one is a world, a visual, an original built-in instrument and an ambience, all tied together by one mood.

### Success looks like
1. **Honey Tape** and **Haze Bells** blend when Michael plays along with their songs in Spotify. Each is as good as, or better than, the sound that inspired it.
2. **Golden Hour Beach** and **Coast Road** look stunning and stay smooth on Smooth on the Intel MacBook Pro.
3. One tap on a set loads its world, visual, instrument preset, ambience and mood. Each part can still be swapped.
4. The waves you see and the surf you hear land together.
5. Each set credits its song with an original, one-of-a-kind living card.
6. Demo clips of both instruments and both ambiences are attached to the release.

### What Michael said
- Two beaches, both from *Honeymoon*, with a distinct instrument each:
  - **Set 3, Golden Hour Beach**, after **"Music to Watch Boys To"**
  - **Set 4, a completely different beachscape and worldscape**, after **"High by the Beach"**. He chose the **coastal drive**.
- He approved the instruments (Part 2), the worlds and visuals (Part 3), and the ambience, cards, testing and build order (Part 4).
- Standing rules still apply:
  - Everything is a 1:1:1 set plus ambience, tied by mood.
  - The bar is "as good as or better than the inspiration, or as close as possible", so playing along blends.
  - It's for personal use only, and the artists are never taken for granted.
  - Sounds are original synthesis. Nothing is sampled.
  - Credits are text and a Spotify link only: no artwork, lyrics, logos or photos, and no imitation of music videos.
  - Card art is original, drawn from the song's feeling, with tempo, key and track number as easter eggs.
  - Every world runs on Smooth on the Intel Mac (≤ 1 MP, no per-pixel ray marching, ≤ 3 noise octaves).

### Research that shaped the design
- **"Music to Watch Boys To":**
  - Produced by Lana Del Rey, Rick Nowels and Kieron Menzies. Critics describe a "narcotic haze" of synth orchestrations, "flute-like sounds" and crawling beats.
  - Del Rey compared its feel to a bonus track on Les Baxter's ocean-themed exotica album *Jewels of the Sea*. (Wikipedia: *Honeymoon*; *Music to Watch Boys To*.)
- **"High by the Beach":**
  - Synth-led trap-pop: dreamy synth "beeps", a brooding organ, TR-808 drums, and an opening of waves and vintage keys. Its chorus was inspired by frequent drives along a beach. (Wikipedia: *High by the Beach*.)
  - Kieron Menzies' production breakdown lists **organ, flute and dulcitone** (a keyboard that strikes tuning forks) under a "hazy, lo-fi aesthetic": saturation, compression, bit-crushing, delay and filtering. (Mix with the Masters, Kieron Menzies on "High by the Beach".)
- The plan's **sound briefs** pin down key, tempo, track numbers and measurable targets before either instrument is built.

## 4. The instruments

Both instruments follow the Sound Core rules from Sets 1 & 2:
- nothing allocates, locks or logs on the audio thread
- denormals are flushed
- MIDI is sample-accurate
- every dial is smoothed
- voices are stolen quietest-first with a 20 ms fade
- tuning is concert pitch (A = 440 Hz), with Fine tune ±50 cents and Transpose ±12 semitones
- mood comes in through the hidden `mood*` parameters

### 4.1 Honey Tape (after "Music to Watch Boys To")
A tape-replay keyboard made from scratch. It has two voices, a flute and a string section, that pass through one shared tape.

- **Flute voice:**
  - A soft, almost pure tone: a sine fundamental with a few gentle overtones (2nd and 3rd, plus a touch of 4th), shaped by **Breath**.
  - **Breath noise** is band-passed around the note's pitch and its 2nd harmonic, and follows the note's envelope. More Breath means airier.
  - A short **chiff** (a puff of filtered noise, about 30–60 ms) starts each note. Velocity sets how strong it is.
  - **Delayed vibrato** grows in over about 0.6–1.2 s of holding, at about 5 Hz with a little randomness per voice.
  - Attack is about 60–120 ms (faster for hard notes), and release about 250 ms.
- **Strings voice:**
  - A soft section of 4 slightly detuned band-limited saws (±6–10 cents) per note through a warm 2-pole low-pass. The cutoff follows the key and velocity.
  - It swells in over about 0.3–0.8 s and releases over about 0.6 s, with gentle ensemble movement (slow, independent detune drift).
  - **Strings** blends flute ↔ strings: 0 is flute only, 1 is strings only, and the default is about 0.3.
- **Tape** (shared by both voices, after the voice mix):
  - **Wow:** a slow pitch sway at about 0.4–0.8 Hz, up to ±12 cents at Wow = 1.
  - **Flutter:** a fast shimmer at about 6–10 Hz, up to ±3 cents.
  - Both come from a modulated, interpolated delay line. Wow is the dial and flutter follows it.
  - **Age:**
    - soft tape saturation
    - a high roll-off (from about 16 kHz down to about 6 kHz)
    - a gentle low "head bump" around 80–120 Hz
    - hiss (none at Age 0, a soft bed at Age 1)
  - Each note also gets a tiny **start wobble**, as if its tape strip were just engaging (about 3–6 cents over 80 ms).
- **Reel:**
  - 0 means notes sustain indefinitely.
  - Above 0, a held note runs out like a tape strip: its length shrinks from 20 s at Reel 0.01 down to **8 s at Reel 1**, then it fades over about 0.4 s.
  - The **Old Reel** preset uses it.
- **Space and Width:** the shared Space reverb, and stereo spread by pitch.
- **Dials (in order):** Breath, Strings, Wow, Age, Reel, Space, Width.
- **Presets:**
  - **Honey Tape** (default, flute-led with strings underneath)
  - **Sea Flute** (flute only, breathier)
  - **Velvet Strings** (strings only)
  - **Old Reel** (more Wow and Age, Reel 1)
- **Polyphony:** 16.
- **Mood:**
  - warmth tilts the Age tone
  - brightness opens the strings filter
  - space sets the reverb size and amount
  - motion deepens the vibrato and ensemble drift
  - energy shortens the attacks

### 4.2 Haze Bells (after "High by the Beach")
A dulcitone-style tuning-fork keyboard over an airy organ, through a hazy lo-fi chain.

- **Bells (modal tuning forks):**
  - Each note is a struck fork:
    - a strong fundamental
    - a weaker octave
    - a bright, fast-decaying **clang mode** at about 6.25× the fundamental, the classic tuning-fork overtone
    - a felt-hammer thump (short filtered noise, about 10 ms)
  - The clang decays in about 50–150 ms and the fundamental rings for about 3–8 s, depending on the register (shorter higher up).
  - Velocity sets the hammer hardness, which sets both how bright the clang is and how loud the note is.
  - It uses the same modal-resonator style as Felt Glass's glass layer (decaying complex oscillators).
  - **Bells** sets the level.
- **Organ:**
  - An airy drawbar-style organ: sine partials at 8′, 4′ and a little 2⅔′, plus a soft breathy component.
  - It has slow vibrato and chorus at about 6 Hz, and a gentle key click when Organ > 0.5.
  - Attack and release are about 30 ms and 150 ms.
  - **Organ** sets the level. It's 0.25 by default, so the organ sits underneath.
- **Lo-fi haze** (after the mix):
  - Gentle tape-like saturation.
  - **Crush:** sample-and-hold rate reduction from none down to about 11 kHz, plus a bit-depth reduction from none down to about 10 bits. It's mixed in parallel so it never turns harsh.
  - **Echo:**
    - a filtered stereo echo (band-passed repeats that darken as they fall away)
    - the time is set from the song's tempo in the sound brief (a dotted-eighth at that tempo)
    - **Echo** sets both the mix and the feedback
  - **Drift:** slow, shared pitch wander of the whole instrument (like a warped record), up to ±8 cents.
- **Space:** the shared Space reverb.
- **Dials (in order):** Bells, Organ, Echo, Crush, Drift, Space.
- **Presets:**
  - **Haze Bells** (default)
  - **Church Keys** (organ forward, bells softer)
  - **Night Drive** (more echo and space)
  - **Lo-Fi** (more Crush and Drift)
- **Polyphony:** 24 (bells ring long).
- **Mood:**
  - warmth softens the bell strike
  - brightness sets the echo filter
  - space sets the reverb
  - motion sets the vibrato and drift depth
  - energy sets the hammer hardness

### 4.3 Sound briefs and the play-along bar
- Before building either instrument, its **sound brief** records:
  - the song's key, tempo and track number (with sources)
  - what is documented about how the sound was made
  - measurable targets:
    - register
    - spectral centroid at mf
    - attack time
    - decay to −40 dB
    - movement depth and rate
    - space
- Demo renders and spectrograms are checked against the brief.
- **Done means it passes Michael's play-along test.** Each instrument gets a **preview build** for an ear test as soon as it exists.

### 4.4 CPU budget (measured in CI)
- Honey Tape with a 12-note sustained chord: **< 5% of real time**.
- Haze Bells with a 24-note pedalled run: **< 4%**.
- The beach ambience or the road ambience at full: **< 2%**.

## 5. Ambience

All of it is generated live. New sources join the existing ambience generator, keeping the same ducking (−4 dB while playing, about 2 s recovery), cross-fades (≥ 2 s) and closeness behaviour.

| Source | How |
|---|---|
| **Surf** | Continuous low wash bed, plus **wave events**: each break triggers a crash (a noise burst through a falling low-pass with a low thump), a sizzling **wash** sliding up (bright, fizzy noise rising then thinning) and a **backwash** fizz draining away. Strength sets the size and length of each part. Distance (closeness and the world) moves it from close and detailed to a far, low roar |
| **Palms** | Dry frond rustle: filtered noise grains with a papery clatter, following the wind |
| **Gulls** | Rare, distant cries: formant-shaped pitch glides. **Ships switched off** until it passes Michael's ear test (the honesty clause) |
| **Road** | Tyre roar (filtered noise whose level and brightness follow the car's speed) and soft expansion-joint thumps in pairs, spaced by speed |
| **Engine** | A low harmonic purr whose pitch and level follow speed, with gentle load wander |
| **Window wind** | A band-passed whistle and rush that rises with speed |
| **Passing car** | A rare whoosh with Doppler fall, panned across |
| *(reused)* | Crickets (beach at dusk and night), breeze, rain on glass (car window), rain on water (sea), wind, thunder |

- **Wave events** come from the world's ocean swell model (§6.2), so the picture and the sound share the same break moments.
- **Speed:** Coast Road sends its car speed (0…1) every frame.
- **Gulls default to off**, matching the owl and dawn chorus rule from Sets 1 & 2.

## 6. Living Sky additions and the Ocean

### 6.1 Hold the light
- Each world gains a **"Hold the light"** choice: **Follow the sky**, **Golden hour** or **Blue hour**.
  - **Golden hour** holds the sun at about **5°** above the horizon.
  - **Blue hour** holds it at about **−6°**.
- Only the sun's altitude is held. Its azimuth, the moon, the stars, the weather and the clock keep running.
- The sky palette, star visibility and light colour are recomputed for the held altitude.
- The hold belongs to the world, not to the Living Sky settings:
  - Choosing a set never changes Michael's time mode, city or weather.
  - Switching to another world brings the real sky back.
- Defaults: Golden Hour Beach → Golden hour; Coast Road → Blue hour. Moonlit Lake and Spaceship Lounge keep following the sky (no option shown).

### 6.2 Ocean swell (Core, deterministic)
- A swell is a seeded schedule of waves:
  - The **period** is 8–12 s.
  - Waves arrive in **sets of 3–5 larger waves** separated by lulls of 2–4 smaller ones.
  - Each wave has a **strength** of 0…1.
- Weather scales it: clear and light cloud 1×, rain 1.2×, storm 1.8× (bigger and steeper), and mist calms it (0.8×).
- For the beach, each wave produces:
  - a **break** event (time and strength)
  - the **wash front** over time, from 0 at the waterline to 1 at the top of the wet sand, rising fast after the break and draining slowly
- For the road, the same schedule drives the white water at the cliff base and the far surf sound.
- The same seed always gives the same waves (tested).

### 6.3 Ocean layer (shared shading)
- An analytic sea plane seen from the camera's height, with no ray marching:
  - a few long swell components plus finer chop (noise ≤ 2 octaves on Smooth, 3 on High and Ultra)
  - normals from the analytic derivatives
- Shading:
  - **Sky reflection** through the shared `skyRadiance`, with Fresnel.
  - **Sun and moon glitter paths**, **blended by each light's strength**. This fixes the Sets 1 & 2 "light snaps from sun to moon at twilight" minor for the ocean.
  - **Foam** on crests (more in storms) and white water where waves break.
  - **Golden-hour translucence:** when the sun is low and in front of the camera, wave faces glow a soft sea-green.
- Aerial haze toward the horizon follows the mood's warmth and the weather.

## 7. The worlds and visuals

### 7.1 Golden Hour Beach
- **Camera:**
  - Standing near the waterline (about 1.6 m), facing the sun while it's low (altitude < 25°), else facing the moon if it's up, else straight out to sea.
  - The camera pans slowly (τ about 4 s) when the target changes, like the lake.
- **Layers:**
  1. **Living Sky:** the sky with warm haze near the horizon.
  2. **Far:**
     - the horizon
     - a low headland silhouette at one side, in aerial haze
  3. **Ocean:** the shared layer (§6.3) out to the break zone.
  4. **Shore:**
     - The break line where waves break (from the swell events).
     - The **wash** sliding up the sand and draining back (from the wash front).
     - **Wet sand** that mirrors the sky and sun. The mirror fades as the sand dries above the highest recent wash.
     - Dry sand with soft ripple marks.
  5. **Near:**
     - two or three palm silhouettes at the frame edges, swaying with the wind
     - dune grass
  6. **Particles:**
     - spray glints at golden hour
     - sand drifting in the breeze
     - rain dimples on the wet sand and sea
- **Weather:**
  - rain and storm add rougher sea and whitecaps
  - mist becomes sea haze
  - snow falls as flakes
- **Parameters:**
  - Visual (Tide Lines / Galaxy Warp)
  - Hold the light
  - Line glow, Lines linger, Foam from chords
  - Glitter, Haze, Palms
  - Show keys, Keyboard height

### 7.2 Tide Lines (beach visual)
- **Each note** writes a glowing line into the wet sand.
  - Its x position runs left to right by pitch, using the same key layout as Lake Ripples.
  - It is traced over about 0.3 s as if drawn by a finger.
  - Velocity sets its length and glow.
  - Its colour is a warm gold, tinted by the current chord quality: rose for minor, pale gold for major, a cooler white for tense.
- **The tide erases:** when the wash front reaches a line's position on the sand, the line is washed out over about 0.5 s.
- **Holding a note** keeps its line glowing until it's washed.
- **The pedal** writes lines **deeper**: a deep line survives its first wash as a faint trace and is erased by the next.
- **Chords** (3 or more notes within about 60 ms) make the **next wave break in golden foam and spray**. The foam amount comes from "Foam from chords".
- **Budget:** at most 24 lines on Smooth and 48 on High and Ultra. When over the limit, the strongest are kept, in their original order, so lines never pop out by age alone.
- Glints and the foam glow go through the **FlashLimiter**, halved when Gentle visuals is on.

### 7.3 Coast Road
- **Camera:**
  - From the passenger seat looking out the side window, slightly down to the sea from about 60 m up.
  - It faces the sun while it's low, else the moon, else the sea.
  - The road curves gently, so the view sways by **at most ±1.5° roll and ±2° yaw**, slowly (periods > 8 s). It must never feel queasy.
- **Layers** (all screen-space, parallax by depth, travelling left to right):
  1. **Living Sky.**
  2. **Far headlands:** 2–3 ridgelines in haze, scrolling very slowly.
  3. **Ocean:** the shared layer from height, with white water at the cliff base driven by the swell.
  4. **Midground:** coastal scrub and the cliff edge.
  5. **Near:**
     - **guardrail posts** streaming past with motion blur
     - **road lamps** every ~40 m, lit from dusk (sun below 2°) and at night, with a soft glow on the scrub
  6. **Frame:** the car window (reusing the lounge's glass treatment, with faint reflections) and **rain on the glass** from the weather layer.
- **Speed:**
  - The car cruises at a base speed, 0.35 of the range (about 55 km/h).
  - Parallax speeds scale with it.
- **Weather:**
  - Mist becomes **sea fog** rolling over the road.
  - Rain lands on the window.
  - Storms bring lightning out to sea, through the FlashLimiter.
- **Parameters:**
  - Visual (Coast Lights / Galaxy Warp)
  - Hold the light
  - Light trails, Speed response, Glitter bloom from chords
  - Lamps, Fog, Sway
  - Show keys, Keyboard height

### 7.4 Coast Lights (drive visual)
- **Each note** lights a passing light, placed bottom to top by pitch:
  - **low notes** (below MIDI 48): **cat's-eyes** on the road edge
  - **middle notes** (48–71): points along the **guardrail**
  - **high notes** (72 and up): **lamp heads**
- Each light takes its **note's colour** (a soft pitch-class palette shared with the keys). Velocity sets its brightness and **trail length**.
- **Lights stream past** at the car's speed and fade over about 1.5 s, longer when lingering.
- **Speed:**
  - Playing intensity raises the car's speed toward 1 with a rise τ of about 0.8 s.
  - Silence eases it back to cruise with a fall τ of about 3 s.
  - The speed is **capped at 1**, smoothed, and never jumps.
- **Chords** make the **sea's glitter path bloom** in the chord's colour (warm for major, cool for minor, violet for tense). The bloom rises with a τ of about 0.35 s and decays with a τ of about 2.5 s.
- **Budget:** at most 64 lights on Smooth and 128 on High and Ultra, with the strongest kept. A 2,000-note burst is rationed (as with Galaxy Warp's spawn queue) so it streams out over frames.
- Light brightness goes through the FlashLimiter.

### 7.5 Keys
- Both worlds show the on-screen keyboard strip, which can be hidden.
- Golden Hour Beach glows **golden amber**, and Coast Road glows **dashboard amber**.

## 11. Reliability

|---|---|
| Sample-rate change, odd buffer sizes, switching instruments while notes are held | As Felt Glass and Orbit Pad (the same plumbing and tests) |

## 12. Testing

**Automatic (every build):**
- **Honey Tape:**
  - tuning within 2 cents across the range
  - Fine tune and Transpose exact (within 1 cent)
  - softer notes have a lower spectral centroid
  - Breath raises the noise share
  - Strings moves the blend (flute-only and strings-only renders differ in their partials)
  - measured wow depth within ±20% of the dial's target
  - with Reel = 1, a held note falls below −60 dB by 8.5 s
  - releases decay
  - no NaN, infinity, clicks on voice steals, or zipper noise on dials
  - CPU budget
- **Haze Bells:**
  - tuning
  - the strike's clang (around 6.25 f₀) decays at least 20 dB faster than the fundamental
  - harder notes are brighter
  - Organ adds sustained harmonics at 2× and 3× the fundamental
  - the echo's first repeat lands at the brief's dotted-eighth time (within 2 ms)
  - Crush raises the high-frequency noise only when turned up
  - no NaN, clicks or zipper noise
  - CPU budget
- **Plumbing:** both engines load through the app's engine, round-trip their full state and expose their dials to Mapping.
- **Ambience:**
  - each new source sounds in its expected band
  - wave events produce a crash then a wash
  - road noise rises with speed
  - gulls are off by default
  - ducking and cross-fades as before
  - the CPU budget
- **Hold the light:**
  - golden hour holds the sun at 5° and blue hour at −6°, whatever the clock
  - Follow matches the plain sky
  - other worlds are unaffected
- **Ocean swell:**
  - deterministic per seed
  - sets of 3–5 larger waves
  - periods 8–12 s
  - storm makes it bigger
  - the wash front rises after each break and drains
- **Tide Lines:**
  - lines are placed by pitch
  - a wash erases lines it passes
  - pedal lines survive one wash
  - chords flag foam on the next break
  - Smooth keeps the strongest 24
- **Coast Lights:**
  - lanes by pitch
  - speed rises with playing, is capped at 1 and settles back to cruise
  - chord bloom colour by quality
  - burst rationing
- **Worlds:**
  - shaders compile
  - preview frames are non-black and structured at golden hour, blue hour, night, rain, storm and fog, on Smooth and High
  - a 2,000-note burst stays within the frame budget
  - Gentle visuals limits glints and lights
