## 1. Purpose

Two still worlds, and the two biggest instrument gaps closed with two new **cores**: a plucked string and a breath wind. Every set keeps the ratio: a world, a visual, an original instrument, a new sky feature, a new weather, new ambience sounds, tied by a mood. The sky and weather features reach every world with a sky.

### Success looks like
1. **Tea House Koto** sounds like a real plucked string under the fingers: bright near the pick, soft further off, a press that bends it up, a vibrato you shape, and the other strings ringing in sympathy. **Zen Shakuhachi** breathes: a slow airy start, a bend down into darkness under pressure, a burst of breath on a hard note.
2. Both worlds look stunning and stay smooth on **Smooth** on the Intel MacBook Pro.
3. Iridescent clouds and rime frost appear in every world with a sky (rime on the edges of the worlds that have edges: pines, stones, rails).
4. The first **ambience in key**: the temple bell retunes to what you are playing.
5. Pitch bend and aftertouch reach the built-in instruments for the first time (the koto's press, the shakuhachi's meri).
6. One tap on a set loads everything; each part can still be swapped. Demo clips of both instruments and both ambiences are on the release.

### Standing rules (unchanged)
- Everything is a set plus ambience, tied by mood. Sounds are original synthesis; no samples, no protected library touched.
- Credits are text and a link only. These two sets are **born from their places, not from a song**, so they carry no "Inspired by" credit (§11 says how the gallery handles that). If Michael names songs for them, the credits slot in without changing anything else.
- Respect over cliché: original phrasing in the demos and the ambience, no "exotic" shorthand; the instruments carry their techniques (oshi, yuri, meri, kari, muraiki) because they are how the instruments are played, not as decoration.
- Every world runs on Smooth: ≤ 1 MP, no per-pixel ray marching, ≤ 3 noise octaves, no buffers per frame; flares and swells through `FlashLimiter`.
- Settings and sets saved by older builds still load.

### Research that shaped the design
- **Koto:** a 13-string zither of paulownia with movable bridges, plucked with plectra on thumb, index and middle finger; the standard tuning **hirajōshi** (from string 5: E, F, A, B, C, E … a minor-sounding pentatonic). Decay is "quite quick" compared with the zheng but the body rings audibly for several seconds; it is very treble near the bridge and gentler further away. **Oshi** presses the string on the far side of the bridge to raise the pitch (a semitone or a whole tone); **yuri** is a vibrato from a lighter press; **hiki-iro** lowers a struck note slightly. (Ando 1989; Denburg; Pro Musica Nipponia; JASA 2020 FE model.)
- **Shakuhachi:** an end-blown bamboo flute with five holes; the basic scale D, F, G, A, C (1.8 shaku); **meri** (head down) lowers the pitch and darkens, **kari** raises and brightens; **muraiki** is an explosive breath; **yuri** is a slow, irregular vibrato from head movement, not the diaphragm; the sound ideal is "wind in a decaying bamboo grove". (UNSW acoustics; NFA "Shakuhachi, Then and Now".)
- **Snow is acoustically almost dead** (absorption ≈ 0.89): the tea house's Space is small, dry and hushed; the garden's is open air with a low wall, short early reflections and no tail.
- **Iridescence:** very small, uniform cloud droplets or ice particles diffract sunlight into pastel bands within about 10° of the sun (first-order interference beyond that, out to ~40°); thin cloud edges and freshly forming cloud show it best; a pileus cap over a building cumulus is the classic case. (Wikipedia; Shaw & Neiman 2003.)
- **Rime:** supercooled fog freezing onto the windward side of everything it touches, feathering branches, wires and stones white; it builds over hours in still freezing fog and drops off in sun.
- **Composers' precedents:** shakuhachi, koto and temple bells for sacred and still Japanese places (Ghost of Tsushima; Kundun's long reverb for the sacred); wooden flutes for calm.

## 4. The instruments

### 4.1 The plucked-string core (`PluckedString.hpp`)
A Karplus–Strong/waveguide string per voice: a fractional-delay loop (Lagrange 3rd order) at the string's period, a one-pole loss filter whose cutoff sets the material (silk/tetron dark … steel bright) and whose gain sets the decay (T60 from the note's register), a **pick-position comb** (the excitation is a short noise burst shaped by the pick's position 0…1 along the string, so plucking near the bridge is bright), a **dispersion allpass** for a little inharmonicity, and a **body** (4–6 modal resonators: the soundboard's low modes) shared per engine. Pitch changes are continuous (the delay length glides), so **pitch bend** and **press** bend the string without a retrigger. Sympathetic resonance: a bank of open strings (the engine chooses which pitches) excited by each note's output at −30 dB and rung with long decay. Cost: one delay line + 3 filters per voice; 24 voices well under budget.

### 4.2 Tea House Koto
- **Voices:** 24 (pool 30). Each note is one koto string plucked with a plectrum. The **sympathetic bank** is the 13 open strings in hirajōshi (rooted on the lowest held note's pitch class when a phrase starts, else E): notes you play make the open strings ring softly (the koto's "bloom").
- **Pick** (0…1): pick position 0.12 (near the bridge, bright, "treble sounding") … 0.5 (gentle); velocity moves it 0.05 toward the bridge on hard notes.
- **Silk** (0…1): the loss filter's cutoff from 9 kHz (0, a crisp tetron string) to 2.5 kHz (1, a soft silk string) and T60 from 3.5 s to 2 s at A3; the decay shortens an octave up by 0.6×.
- **Body:** paulownia resonances at about 100, 194, 220, 285 Hz (the FE-model peaks) and a 20–40 Hz thump on hard plucks; the **Body** dial 0…1 sets their level.
- **Press (oshi):** the pitch wheel raises the string smoothly up to a whole tone (+200 cents at full), gliding over 25 ms; **aftertouch** gives **yuri**: a slow irregular vibrato (4–6 Hz, ±15 cents × pressure) that only starts 150 ms after the pluck. The **Yuri** dial scales both.
- **Scrape:** a hard note at velocity ≥ 118 adds a short plectrum scrape (2 ms noise burst, 3–8 kHz).
- **Space:** the tea house: a small wooden room, reverb size 0.08–0.3, pre-delay 8 ms, damped; **Width** spreads the 13 strings left (low) to right (high) a little, like sitting at the instrument.
- **Mood:** energy brightens the pick; warmth softens Silk; brightness tilts a high shelf ±3 dB; space adds to Space; motion widens Yuri.
- **Dials, in order:** Pick .55, Silk .45, Body .50, Bloom (sympathetic level) .40, Yuri .30, Space .35, Width .50.
- **Presets:** `tea-house-koto` "Tea House Koto" (.55/.45/.50/.40/.30/.35/.50), `winter-strings` "Winter Strings" (.35/.70/.60/.55/.20/.45/.55), `near-the-bridge` "Near the Bridge" (.15/.25/.40/.25/.35/.25/.45), `night-tea` "Night Tea" (.45/.60/.55/.60/.40/.50/.50).
- **Test hooks:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_SYMPATHY = 209` (the bank off), `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_BODY = 210`.
- **Targets:** tuning within 2 cents (A2–A5); pick 0.15 vs 0.5 on A3: the 2–6 kHz energy over 0–100 ms higher by ≥ 6 dB; silk 1 vs 0: centroid over 0.3–0.6 s lower by ≥ 25%; T60 on A3 2–4 s; a +200 cent bend reads +200 ± 5 cents after 60 ms with no re-attack (no RMS dip over 6 dB); aftertouch 1: a 4–6 Hz pitch wobble of 10–20 cents; sympathy on vs off: with E4 plucked, the energy at B4 (an open string) over 1–2 s higher by ≥ 10 dB; CPU < 6% for a 12-note chord.

### 4.3 The breath-wind core (`BreathWind.hpp`)
A jet-driven flute model light enough for 8 voices: a **breath** source (white noise through a 1–4 kHz band, shaped by the breath envelope) feeding a **bore** (a waveguide delay at the note's period with a reflection low-pass and a non-linear jet (a soft cubic) that locks the oscillation), plus a **tone** path (the bore's self-oscillation, which carries the pitch) mixed with the **air** path (the un-locked noise, which is the airy part). Pitch comes from the bore length (continuous, so bends are smooth); the mix of tone vs air follows breath pressure, so soft notes are mostly air. Cost: one delay + 3 filters + a cubic per voice.

### 4.4 Zen Shakuhachi
- **Voices:** 8 (pool 10): mostly one line, with a few for overlapping phrases.
- **Attack:** the breath rises over 60–350 ms (the **Air** dial): a note begins as air and becomes tone (the tone/air crossfade follows the breath envelope); release 150–400 ms with the air lingering.
- **Breath** (0…1): the air path's level (−24 … −6 dB re the tone).
- **Meri/kari on aftertouch:** pressure bends the pitch **down** by up to −150 cents (the **Bend** dial scales it) and darkens (the reflection low-pass drops from 5 kHz to 1.8 kHz at full pressure); kari is the pitch wheel upward, bright (+100 cents max).
- **Muraiki:** velocity ≥ 112 fires a breath burst (a 120 ms noise swell at +9 dB, 2–6 kHz) at the note's start.
- **Yuri:** a slow irregular vibrato (3–5 Hz, ±8–20 cents, amplitude wandering) that starts after 0.4 s; the **Yuri** dial scales it; it never locks to a steady rate.
- **Bamboo** (0…1): two bore formants (about 600 Hz and 1.9 kHz, Q 3) that give the hollow bamboo colour; and a key-click (a 3 ms tick at 1.5–3 kHz) on each new note.
- **Echo:** a mountain echo, one or two soft repeats 0.35–0.9 s later at −14 dB (the **Echo** dial); **Space** open-air (reverb 0.15–0.5, early reflections from a low wall); **Width** 0.3–0.7.
- **Range:** D4–D6 by design (notes below D4 fold up an octave, so the left hand doesn't honk); the lowest note sounds "ro".
- **Mood:** energy adds breath and muraiki ease; warmth darkens the bore; brightness tilts; space adds Echo and Space; motion widens Yuri.
- **Dials, in order:** Breath .45, Air .55, Bend .60, Bamboo .50, Yuri .40, Echo .30, Space .40, Width .45.
- **Presets:** `zen-shakuhachi` "Zen Shakuhachi" (.45/.55/.60/.50/.40/.30/.40/.45), `mountain-breath` "Mountain Breath" (.60/.70/.70/.45/.50/.70/.60/.55), `hollow-bamboo` "Hollow Bamboo" (.30/.35/.40/.85/.30/.15/.25/.40), `still-air` "Still Air" (.75/.90/.50/.40/.25/.40/.55/.50).
- **Test hooks:** `ISD_P_DEBUG_TONE_ONLY = 211` (no breath, no muraiki, no yuri, no echo), `ISD_P_DEBUG_NO_FOLD = 212`.
- **Targets:** tuning within 5 cents once locked (a flute model drifts a few cents by design; the test allows 5), fine tune and transpose within 2; attack: at Air 1 the RMS reaches −6 dB of its plateau no sooner than 200 ms, at Air 0 within 80 ms; aftertouch 1 at Bend 1: pitch −150 ± 15 cents and centroid lower by ≥ 25%; velocity 120 vs 60: 2–6 kHz energy over 0–150 ms higher by ≥ 6 dB (muraiki); yuri present at 3–5 Hz after 0.6 s; notes below D4 sound an octave up; CPU < 6% for 8 voices.

### 4.5 Sound briefs and the play-along
Briefs in `docs/superpowers/briefs/{tea-house-koto,zen-shakuhachi}.md` (facts with sources, targets, what we do better, playing notes, presets). No song to play along to: the briefs carry **playing notes** instead (the hirajōshi scale on the koto; the five-note scale on the shakuhachi; what the wheel and aftertouch do).

### 4.6 Pitch bend and aftertouch (Sound Core)
`Engine::pitchBend(float semitones)` (range ±2 semitones from the 14-bit wheel) and `Engine::aftertouch(int note, float pressure)` (note −1 for channel pressure, which applies to every held note; poly pressure to one) — virtual with default no-ops. `Synth.cpp` decodes 0xE0, 0xD0 and 0xA0 (today it ignores them). Existing engines stay as they are (no-op), so nothing changes for them. `MIDIInput` already passes these messages to the AU as raw MIDI 1.0 bytes, `MIDIMessage` already parses them and `PerformanceEvent` already carries `.pitchBend(Float)` and `.pressure(Float)` for the visuals.

### 4.7 CPU budget (measured in CI)
Each engine < 6% (koto: 12-note chord with the bank; shakuhachi: 8 held voices with echo); each new ambience scene < 2.5%; the combined budget with all 32 sources at 0.6 < 8%.

## 5. Ambience

Four sources appended (`ISD_AMB_TEMPLE_BELL`, `ISD_AMB_PAPER_SCREENS`, `ISD_AMB_WATER_KNOCKER`, `ISD_AMB_WARBLER`; count 32) with `AmbienceSource.templeBell, .paperScreens, .waterKnocker, .warbler`.

- **Temple bell (in key):** a bonshō: a low strike (hum partials at about 1, 1.6, 2.3, 3.0 × f0 with a 6–12 s decay, a 1 ms clapper click, a slow 0.3 Hz beating) every 120–300 s / density, at −30 dBFS. **Its pitch follows the key:** `isd_ambience_set_key(root 0…11, major 0/1)` sets f0 to the tonic in the bell's octave (A1–G♯2); the bell glides to a new root only between strikes. the key the player is in (§7.1) drives it through `AmbienceScene.key`. This is the first of the "ambience in key" family; the existing chime stays as it is this run.
- **Paper screens:** the soft rustle and tick of shoji in a draught (band-passed noise 1–4 kHz in 0.2–0.6 s flutters every 6–20 s × wind, with an occasional wooden frame creak), at −38 dBFS; indoor.
- **Water-knocker (shishi-odoshi):** the bamboo fills (a thin trickle, 2–4 kHz, 15–40 s / density), tips (a short slosh), and **clacks** on the stone (a bright 2 ms knock with a 60 ms 1.8 kHz ring, at −18 dBFS peak), then the trickle resumes; deterministic from the seed; `isd_ambience_debug_start_knocker` for tests.
- **Bush warbler (uguisu):** the "ho—hokekyo" call: a long rising whistle (2.5 → 3.2 kHz over 0.6 s) then two quick notes, every 40–120 s / density, from spring and summer mornings only (the world gates it by the local season and hour; the engine just plays when asked).
- Levels calibrated as before (continuous beds near −24 dBFS at level 1; event sources by their peaks).
- **WeatherAmbience:** in rime (`rime > 0.3`) everything outdoors is muffled a little (indoors +0.15) and the wind drops.

## 6. Living Sky additions (every world with a sky)

### 6.1 Weather values
`WeatherValues` gains `rime: Float = 0`.

| Kind | cloud | rain | snow | mist | wind | halo | frost | gust | rime | beauty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.rime` "Rime frost" | 0.3 | 0 | 0 | 0.85 | 0.08 | 0.2 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0.5 |

Not wet, not rough, no flashes; `frostFormsOnlyAtNight` false (rime forms any time it is cold and foggy). Appended at the end of `allCases`.

### 6.2 New sky state
`SkyState` gains `dayOfYear: Int` (1…366 on the sky city's local calendar; quick mode advances a day per cycle; it drives the garden's season), `iridescence: Float` (0…1 this spell), `rime: Float` (0…1 how rimed the world is: it **accumulates** over 10–20 minutes in rime weather (τ 12 min toward 1), and melts in sun (τ 6 min toward 0 when the sun is up and rime weather is gone; τ 40 min at night)). Both reset with the sky.

### 6.3 Iridescent clouds (SkyKit)
- **When:** thin cloud (cloud cover 0.15–0.6) with the sun 5°–50° up; a seeded per-spell `iridescence` 0…1 like the halo's ice (random light-cloud spells: 0.3–1 one spell in two, else 0; picked light cloud: 0.6). The Advanced "Iridescence" (0…1.5) scales it.
- **On screen** (`skyRadiance`, in the cloud pass): where the cloud density is thin (0.05–0.5) and the pixel is within 40° of the sun, the cloud colour is tinted by a diffraction band pattern: hue cycles with the angle from the sun **and** with the cloud's local density gradient (the bands follow the cloud's edges), with a pastel saturation that peaks at 10–20° from the sun and fades by 40°. A **pileus** cap appears as a smooth iridescent arc over the top of cumulus-like cloud cells (the noise cell's upper edge) when the sun is low. Never brighter than the cloud's own light + 20%. Reflections get it free.
- **Smooth:** the band pattern is one `sin` of a dot product; no extra noise octaves.

### 6.4 Rime frost (WeatherKit)
- **`WeatherLayer`:** in rime, a fine **ice-fog drift** (reusing the crystals set from Sets 7 & 8 at low density and slower) and no rain/snow.
- **Rime on edges** (`WorldKit/RimeShaders.swift`, a shared MSL helper `rimeEdge(float silhouetteDistance, float rime, float3 normalDir, float3 windDir)`): a feathered white fringe grows on the windward edge of a silhouette by the sky's `rime` (width 0.6%·rime of the view, feathered with 2-octave noise, lit by the sky's horizon colour × 1.3, so it reads white in day and blue-grey at night). The lake's reeds and pines, the cabin's pines and rail, the fjord's rail and village roofs, the bay's palms, and both new worlds' trees, stones and rails call it. The beach and road get rime on the rail/dune grass (road: the rail and signposts).
- **Hold:** none new.
- **Advanced:** `rimeSpeed` (0.25…4, default 1) scales how fast rime builds and melts; `iridescence` (0…1.5, default 1). Both in the Night sky group → rename the group **Sky extras**.

### 6.5 Settings and UI
- `SkySettings` gains `iridescence: Double = 1`, `rimeSpeed: Double = 1` (defaults on decode).
- `SkyControls.weatherName`: `.rime` → "Rime frost".
- The three looks: Gentle iridescence 0.6, rime 0.7; Dramatic 1.4, 1.5; Dreamy 1.0, 1.0. Reset covers both.

## 7. Shared pieces

### 7.1 The key you are playing in (Core)
`PlayingAnalyzer` already estimates the key (a decaying pitch-class histogram correlated against the Krumhansl–Kessler profiles, after 8 notes: `keyRoot`, `keyIsMinor`). It gains `keyConfidence` (the best correlation minus the best one with a different root), and `SceneDirector` gains `public var key: AmbienceKey?` (`struct AmbienceKey: Equatable { root: Int; major: Bool }`), non-nil once the analyzer has a key with confidence ≥ 0.05. `AmbienceScene` gains `key: AmbienceKey?`; the App copies the director's key into each frame's scene before sending it (`ThemeCanvas`), `AppModel.ambienceChanged` notices a key change, and `AmbienceAU` calls `isd_ambience_set_key`.

### 7.2 Optional credits
`InStrumSet.creditID: String?` (decoding old JSON keeps working: the field was always present). Sets without a credit draw a **place card**: `CardArt.place(for setID)` — original art from the world (the tea bowl's steam over snow; the raked circles), breathing at a slow fixed pulse (60/48 s), with the set's name and mood instead of song, artist and album, and no Listen button. `SetsTests.testEverySetHasACardOrPlaceArt`.

## 8. Set 9: Tea House Snow

### 8.1 World
Inside a small tea house, kneeling at a low table: tatami, a hanging scroll, a tea bowl steaming, an iron kettle on a brazier. The **shōji** (paper screens) are slid half open on a snow garden: a stone lantern with a snow cap, a pine bent with snow, a stone water basin with a bamboo ladle, a low fence, and a plum tree with a few red blossoms when it is not snowing hard. Snow falls beyond the screens; the paper glows with the sky's light, its lattice shadows crossing the room. At night the lantern's candle and the brazier light the room; the paper goes blue-grey.
- **Weather:** rain falls as snow here (the cabin's cold rule), fog banks whiten the garden to nothing, rime feathers the pine and the fence, diamond dust glitters.
- **Sky:** the Living Sky through the open screens (horizon low, the garden fills the lower half); pastel dawns tint the paper pink.
- **Parameters:** `visual` ["Steam Lines", "Frost Grow"]; `SkyHold.spec(default: .follow)`; `AuroraMode.spec(default: .follow)`; `screens` .5 ("Screens open", hero; 0 closed: the room is all paper light); `steam` .6 ("Tea steam", hero); `blossom` .4 ("Plum blossom"); `keys.show`, `keys.height`.
- **Frost Grow** works on the screens' paper as a second visual (the Window Kit with a `.paperScreen` material: no glass, frost reads as ice on the paper's lattice panes).
- **Ambience:** `kettle` 0.35 (the iron kettle: the existing source at a lower level), `paperScreens` 0.4 × (0.4 + wind), `templeBell` 0.5 × night-and-dusk (0.25 by day), `wind` 0.1 + gust, `rainOnGlass` → snow ticks (`snowOnGlass` = snow, indoors 0.8 − 0.5·screens), `dawnChorus` 0.2 × dawn; `WeatherAmbience.levels`. Closeness base 0.75.
- **Mood:** `delicate` = (energy 0.2, warmth 0.55, brightness 0.5, space 0.3, motion 0.2).

### 8.2 Visual: Steam Lines (Core: `SteamLines`)
- The tea's **steam** rises from the bowl as 2–4 soft ribbons; **each note** sends a wisp off the ribbon at its key's x (0.2…0.8 of the bowl's width → the ribbon's lateral curl), its height by velocity; **held notes** keep the wisp drawn upward and brighten the steam; **runs** draw the melody's contour as the ribbon's path (the last 12 notes' pitches become the ribbon's lateral offsets up its height); **chords** puff a rounder cloud, warm for major, cool for minor; a hard note sends a snowflake off the lantern's cap outside.
- Cap 24 wisps, 8 spawns per step, τ-fade 3 s; ids wrap. Through `FlashLimiter`.

## 9. Set 10: Zen Rock Garden

### 9.1 World
From the veranda of a temple hall at dawn: a **dry garden** of raked white gravel with five stones in three groups on moss islands, a low earthen wall with a tiled cap, a maple that turns with the season (green, then red in autumn by the sky city's date), a bamboo **water-knocker** by a stone basin, and a tall cedar beyond the wall. Morning light rakes across the gravel ridges; by noon the garden is flat and bright; at dusk long shadows; at night moonlight silver on the gravel.
- **Weather:** rain darkens the gravel and dulls the lines (wetness), snow caps the stones and fills the grooves (snow), rime feathers the maple and the wall's cap, humid nights bring fireflies over the moss, iridescent clouds over the cedar.
- **Parameters:** `visual` ["Rake Lines", "Galaxy Warp"]; `SkyHold.spec(default: .follow)`; `AuroraMode.spec(default: .follow)`; `ridge` .6 ("Rake depth", hero); `moss` .5 ("Moss"); `season` choice ["Follow the date", "Spring", "Summer", "Autumn", "Winter"] default follow (the maple and the warbler); `keys.show`, `keys.height`.
- **Ambience:** `waterKnocker` 0.6, `warbler` 0.5 × spring-summer morning, `breeze` 0.25 + wind, `crickets` 0.4 × summer night, `dawnChorus` 0.3 × dawn, `templeBell` 0.3 (the hall's own bell, in key, rarer: density 0.6), `rainOnWater` 0.5 × rain (the basin), `drips`; `WeatherAmbience.levels`. Closeness base 0.55.
- **Mood:** `orderedCalm` = (energy 0.15, warmth 0.45, brightness 0.55, space 0.5, motion 0.15).

### 9.2 Visual: Rake Lines (Core: `RakeLines`)
- The gravel carries straight **rake lines** across the garden and **circles** around each stone. **Each note** rakes a new ring around the stone nearest its key's x (the five stones map to five key regions), one gravel-width further out, with a strength by velocity; **held notes** keep the rake moving (the ring deepens); **runs** rake a sweeping curve across the gravel from stone to stone; **chords** re-rake the whole bed in a slow wave from the chord's mean x; the grooves **fade** over 90 s back to the morning's pattern (τ by linger).
- The grooves are a 256 × 144 height field (Core, CPU, deterministic) the renderer uploads as a texture when it changes (at most once per frame, on a dirty flag), lit by the sun or moon direction so ridges throw shadows. Cap: the field; events rationed to 12 per step; ids wrap. Through `FlashLimiter` (the wave's brightness).

## 12. Performance
- Both engines: one delay line + a few filters per voice; the koto's sympathetic bank is 13 strings shared per engine, not per voice.
- Iridescence adds one `sin` and a gradient read inside the existing cloud pass; rime edges one noise read on silhouette edges; the rake height field is one texture.
- Both worlds: analytic silhouettes, ≤ 3-octave noise, ≤ 1 MP on Smooth.

## 13. Reliability
- A pitch-bend or pressure that is still "on" when a world or instrument switches is cleared (`allNotesOff` resets bend and pressure to neutral).
- The temple bell never changes pitch mid-ring; a key that cannot be sensed leaves it at its last root (E by default).
- Panic ends every koto string and shakuhachi voice within 50 ms, echo tails included (`allSoundOff`).
- Rime accumulation and the rake field stay bounded over hours; ids wrap.
