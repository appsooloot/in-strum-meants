## What we're matching
- **Register:** the keys sit around D2–D6; the sparks ring above.
- **Tines:**
  - a sine fundamental with a slow second and a decaying third partial (inharmonicity 1.002–1.008 up the keyboard), over a tone-bar partial with a 2–4× longer decay
  - on **A3 (220 Hz)**, pedal down, a decay to −40 dB in **4–9 s**; shorter up top
  - a hammer thump (40–120 Hz, about 10 ms) and a release click (2–5 kHz, about 4 ms) that follow velocity
- **Bark:** the pickup saturates asymmetrically with velocity: A3 at velocity 120 against 40 has **≥ 8 dB** more 2–6 kHz energy over its first 120 ms (normalised by total level)
- **Tine dial:** tine 1 against 0 on A4 raises the 0.2–0.5 s centroid by **≥ 1.2×**
- **Sparkle:** each note throws 2–5 sparks 15–90 ms later (short sines at 5, 7 and 9 × f₀, ±25 cents, 60–250 ms decays, spread in stereo); at Sparkle 1 at least 2 transients in the 3–12 kHz band within 15–120 ms of the note; none at Sparkle 0
- **Tremolo:** a stereo pan tremolo, **4.6 Hz** at 0.5 (2–7 Hz across the dial): the left/right envelope autocorrelation peaks at 1/4.6 s ± 10%
- **Phase:** a four-stage phaser at 0.1–0.3 Hz
- **Tuning:** within 2 cents on the fundamental.
- **Panic:** a 16-note burst then all-notes-off → silence within 50 ms, no voices, no sparks.
- **CPU:** a 12-note chord with sparks under 6% of real time (CI).

## What we'll do better
- **Sparks that scatter like plankton:** every note lights little bells after it, scattered in the stereo field, that fade like a wake.
- **A pickup that barks when you dig in** and stays pure when you play soft.
- **The haze is yours:** tremolo and phase as dials, not a fixed effect.
- **Every part is a dial:** Tine, Bark, Sparkle, Tremolo, Phase, Space, Width.
