# Sound brief: Honey Tape (after "Music to Watch Boys To")

## Facts
- **Song:** "Music to Watch Boys To", Lana Del Rey. *Honeymoon* (2015), **track 2** (4:51). Second single, released 11 September 2015. [Wikipedia: Honeymoon][hm], [Wikipedia: Music to Watch Boys To][mtwbt]
- **Producers:** Lana Del Rey, Rick Nowels and Kieron Menzies. Rick Nowels plays synthesizers. Kieron Menzies did drum programming, percussion and sampler. Strings, winds and keyboards across the album are by Patrick Warren, Leon Michels and David Levita (no track numbers given). [hm]
- **Key:** **C♯ minor**. Main chords i, iv and v (C♯m, F♯m, G♯m). Verse i–VII–VI–vsus4–V; chorus i–VII–VI–V. [Hooktheory][ht], [Tunebat][tb]
- **Tempo:** **132 BPM** in 4/4, felt in half-time (about 66). [ht], [tb]
- **The sound:**
  - Exclaim! describes "synth-y orchestrations, flute-like tones and crawling beats", "doused in … narcotic haze". [Exclaim!][ex]
  - The FADER hears "a distant string section". [FADER][fd]
  - Del Rey compared the feel to a bonus track on Les Baxter's *Jewels of the Sea* (1961 ocean exotica). [mtwbt]
- **Spotify search:** `Music to Watch Boys To Lana Del Rey`

## What we're matching
- **Register:**
  - The flute line sits around C♯4–C♯6.
  - Strings sit underneath, from about C♯3 to C♯5.
  - The play-along sweet spot is the octave above middle C.
- **Tone colour:**
  - The flute is soft and nearly pure, with a breathy edge. Target spectral centroid at mf for C♯5 (554 Hz): **0.55–1.0 kHz**, including breath. A power-weighted centroid of a soft flute sits near 1.1–1.3 × f0: the fundamental dominates.
  - The strings are distant and dark. Target centroid at mf for a C♯4 triad: **0.5–1.0 kHz**.
  - *(Revised while building: the first draft's 1.0–1.8 kHz assumed an amplitude-weighted centroid. Measured at the defaults: flute 616 Hz, strings 601 Hz.)*
- **Attack:**
  - Flute **60–120 ms** (a soft chiff on top).
  - Strings **300–800 ms** swell.
- **Decay and release:**
  - The flute sustains while held and releases in about **250 ms**.
  - The strings release in about **600 ms**.
  - The reverb adds a long, hazy tail.
- **Movement:**
  - Flute vibrato **4.5–5.5 Hz**, depth **±15–25 cents** once grown in (delayed 0.6–1.2 s).
  - Tape wow at the default dial gives about **±4 cents** at 0.4–0.8 Hz, plus faint flutter. The whole sound should feel slightly "replayed".
- **Space:** large and soft, in the haze. A long pre-delay isn't wanted; the reverb sits close behind the sound.

## What we'll do better
- **Playable in tune:** wow is a dial, so it can be zeroed for exact play-along, or pushed for the full old-tape feel.
- **Breath under control:** breath and chiff follow velocity, so soft playing is airy and hard playing speaks clearly.
- **One instrument, two voices:** flute and strings are blended by one dial instead of being two separate tracks.

## Play-along notes
- **Key:** C♯ minor. Play C♯, E, G♯ around the octave above middle C.
- **Tuning:** the recording is at concert pitch as far as the analysis tools show. Leave Fine tune at 0 unless it beats against the record.
- **Strings:** sustained i–VII–VI–V shapes (C♯m–B–A–G♯) sit under the record; the flute can double the vocal's register.
